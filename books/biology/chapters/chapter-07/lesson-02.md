## Concepts

1. **A membrane is a collage of different proteins embedded in the fluid lipid bilayer; proteins determine most of the membrane's specific functions** (p9). Hardness: stated.
2. **Figure 7-7 tour of the plasma membrane**: integral protein, peripheral proteins, transmembrane protein, glycoprotein, glycolipid, carbohydrate, cholesterol, fibres of the extracellular matrix (ECM), microfilaments of the cytoskeleton, extracellular side and cytoplasmic side (p10). Hardness: shown by the figure with labels; no text explains each one on this slide.
3. **Peripheral proteins are bound to the surface of the membrane** (p11). Hardness: stated; no way through the bilayer implied.
4. **Integral proteins penetrate the hydrophobic core of the bilayer** (p11; p10 figure). Hardness: stated, then shown in Figure 7-7.
5. **Integral proteins that span the whole membrane are called transmembrane proteins** (p11). Hardness: definition.
6. **The hydrophobic regions of an integral protein are one or more stretches of nonpolar amino acids, often coiled into alpha helices** (p11). Hardness: stated; not drawn at amino-acid level.
7. **Six major functions of membrane proteins** (p12): transport, enzymatic activity, signal transduction, cell-cell recognition, intercellular joining, attachment to the cytoskeleton and ECM. Hardness: list; each one is shown on p13.
8. **Transport** (p13a). A channel-type protein lets a substance pass with no ATP; a second protein uses ATP to move a substance. Hardness: shown by the two-protein panel (ATP circled).
9. **Enzymatic activity** (p13b). Membrane enzymes speed up reactions on the membrane surface, shown as a protein with arrows for reactants and products. Hardness: shown; the slide gives no reaction.
10. **Signal transduction** (p13c). A signaling molecule binds a receptor on the outside; the signal is passed to the inside of the cell. Hardness: shown; the receptor is circled on the slide.
11. **Cell-cell recognition** (p13d). A glycoprotein's carbohydrate on the outer face is recognised by a neighbouring cell. Hardness: shown.
12. **Intercellular joining** (p13e). Membrane proteins join neighbouring cells together. Hardness: shown; the picture is two cells with a protein bridge, no junction type named.
13. **Attachment to the cytoskeleton and ECM** (p13f). Membrane proteins anchor the cell to the ECM outside and to microfilaments inside. Hardness: shown; the ECM fibres and cytoskeleton are drawn beside the protein.
14. **Cells recognise each other by binding to surface molecules, often carbohydrates** (p14). Hardness: stated.
15. **Membrane carbohydrates are covalently bonded either to lipids (glycolipids) or, more commonly, to proteins (glycoproteins)** (p14). Hardness: stated; the terms are defined and shown on p10.
16. **Carbohydrates on the external side of the plasma membrane vary among species, among individuals, and even among cell types in one individual** (p14). Hardness: stated.

## Vocabulary

- **Integral protein**: a membrane protein that penetrates the hydrophobic core of the bilayer.
- **Peripheral protein**: a membrane protein bound to the surface, not inside the core.
- **Transmembrane protein**: an integral protein that spans the whole membrane.
- **Hydrophobic core, nonpolar amino acids**: the water-avoiding middle of the bilayer; the amino acids that line the protein's span there.
- **Alpha helix**: a coiled protein shape; here the hydrophobic span is often one.
- **Glycoprotein, glycolipid**: a carbohydrate covalently attached to a protein or to a lipid.
- **Carbohydrate (on the membrane)**: short sugar chains on the outer face, used in recognition.
- **Cholesterol**: a steroid in the membrane (taught in lesson 1); shown on p10.
- **Extracellular matrix (ECM)**: fibres outside the cell that membrane proteins attach to.
- **Microfilaments (cytoskeleton)**: the cytoskeleton fibres inside the cell that membrane proteins attach to.
- **Transport, enzymatic activity, signal transduction, cell-cell recognition, intercellular joining, attachment**: the six functions of membrane proteins.
- **ATP**: the energy molecule used by some transport proteins.
- **Signaling molecule, receptor**: the outside messenger and the membrane protein that binds it.
- **Enzyme**: a protein that speeds up a reaction.
- **Extracellular side, cytoplasmic side**: the two faces of the membrane.

## Questions

No question slides; the narration step writes checks and practice.

Question ideas this material supports:

1. Name the three kinds of membrane protein this slide set uses (integral, peripheral, transmembrane), and say where each sits. Tests: the definitions on p11.
2. Why is the hydrophobic region of an integral protein made of nonpolar amino acids? Tests: the bilayer core is water-avoiding (p11).
3. Which part of the figure is the glycoprotein, and which is the glycolipid? Tests: reading Figure 7-7 (a protein versus a lipid carrying the carbohydrate).
4. In panel (a), which pathway needs ATP? Tests: the channel on the left needs no ATP; the protein on the right uses ATP (p13).
5. A signaling molecule binds outside a cell. Which membrane protein receives it, and where does the signal go? Tests: receptor on the outside, signal transduction to the inside (p13c).
6. Name two of the six functions that attach a membrane protein to something outside or inside the cell. Tests: attachment to the cytoskeleton and ECM; intercellular joining is also an attachment-type answer (p12 and p13f).
7. Why can two cells of the same species be told apart by their membranes? Tests: carbohydrates on the external side vary among individuals and cell types, and cells recognise each other by surface carbohydrates (p14).
8. Which bond links a carbohydrate to a protein, and what is the result called? Tests: covalent bond; glycoprotein (p14).
9. Which function would a membrane enzyme perform, and what does it do to the reaction? Tests: enzymatic activity speeds up reactions (p13b).
