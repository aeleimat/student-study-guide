## Concepts

2. **Bulk transport requires energy** (p42). Hardness: stated, no mechanism given on the slide. Teach it as a contrast with passive transport (lessons 3-4).
3. **Exocytosis: transport vesicles migrate to the membrane, fuse with it, and release their contents** (p43). Hardness: stated.
4. **Many secretory cells use exocytosis to export their products** (p43). Hardness: stated, no example named.
5. **Endocytosis: the cell takes in macromolecules by forming vesicles from the plasma membrane** (p44). Hardness: stated; the direction is the reverse of exocytosis.
6. **Endocytosis is a reversal of exocytosis, but it involves different proteins** (p44). Hardness: stated.
7. **Three types of endocytosis: phagocytosis ("cellular eating"), pinocytosis ("cellular drinking"), receptor-mediated endocytosis** (p44; the three panels on p45). Hardness: stated, then shown by the three-panel figure.
8. **Phagocytosis: the cell engulfs a particle in a vacuole (pseudopodium around the food, then a food vacuole)** (p46; p45 and p47 figures). Hardness: stated, shown by a diagram and a TEM of an amoeba engulfing a bacterium.
9. **The food vacuole fuses with a lysosome, which digests the particle** (p46). Hardness: stated. The lysosome step is only in the words; the p47 diagram stops at the food vacuole.
10. **Pinocytosis: extracellular fluid is "gulped" into tiny vesicles, so molecules dissolved in it are taken up** (p46; p45 and p47 figures). Hardness: stated, shown by a diagram and a TEM of pinocytosis vesicles forming in a blood-vessel lining cell.
11. **Receptor-mediated endocytosis: binding of ligands to receptors triggers vesicle formation** (p48). Hardness: stated.
12. **A ligand is any molecule that binds specifically to a receptor site of another molecule** (p48). Hardness: definition.
13. **Coated pits and coated vesicles: a coat protein lines the pit and then the vesicle; the TEM pair shows a coated pit and a coated vesicle** (p45, p47). Hardness: shown by figure and TEM; the coat's exact name is not given.

## Vocabulary

- **Bulk transport**: moving large molecules or particles across the membrane in vesicles; needs energy.
- **Exocytosis**: vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane and releases its contents outside the cell.
- **Secretory cell**: a cell that exports products by exocytosis.
- **Endocytosis**: the cell takes in material by forming vesicles from the plasma membrane.
- **Phagocytosis ("cellular eating")**: engulfing a solid particle in a vacuole.
- **Pseudopodium**: a cell extension that wraps around the particle.
- **Food vacuole**: the vacuole that holds the engulfed particle.
- **Lysosome**: the organelle whose digestive contents break down the particle in the food vacuole.
- **Pinocytosis ("cellular drinking")**: taking in extracellular fluid, and what is dissolved in it, in tiny vesicles.
- **Receptor-mediated endocytosis**: uptake that starts when ligands bind receptors on the membrane.
- **Receptor**: a membrane protein that binds a specific ligand.
- **Ligand**: any molecule that binds specifically to a receptor site.
- **Coated pit, coated vesicle, coat protein**: the membrane region (and then the vesicle) lined by coat proteins during receptor-mediated uptake.
- **Solutes**: dissolved particles in the extracellular fluid (drawn as green dots and purple triangles on the figures).
- **TEM**: transmission electron microscope, the source of the micrographs.
