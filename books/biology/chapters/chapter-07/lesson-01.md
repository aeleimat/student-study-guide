## Concepts

1. **The plasma membrane is the boundary that separates the living cell from its surroundings** (p2). Hardness: stated.
2. **Selective permeability: the membrane lets some substances cross more easily than others** (p2). Hardness: named only (highlighted and glossed); taught in lesson 3.
3. **Phospholipids are the most abundant lipid in the plasma membrane** (p3). Hardness: stated.
4. **Phospholipids are amphipathic: one part hydrophilic (the head), one part hydrophobic (the tails)** (p3; head and tail labels on p4). Hardness: stated, then drawn.
5. **In water, phospholipids form a bilayer: heads face the water on both sides, tails face inward** (p4, with the red handwritten note). Hardness: shown by the figure (cross-section, plus a space-filling single molecule in the bilayer).
6. **The fluid mosaic model: a membrane is a fluid structure with a mosaic of proteins embedded in it** (p3). Hardness: stated; the proteins are not drawn on these slides (lesson 2 covers them).
7. **Lipids move sideways within their own layer: lateral movement, about 10^7 times per second** (p5, p6). Hardness: stated, with a boxed figure.
8. **Most lipids, and some proteins, drift laterally** (p5). Hardness: stated.
9. **Flip-flop, a lipid moving from one layer to the other, is rare: about once per month** (p5, p6). Hardness: stated, with a boxed figure.
10. **Temperature changes membrane state: membranes switch from fluid to solid as they cool; the solidifying temperature depends on the lipids** (p7). Hardness: stated.
11. **Unsaturated tails (with kinks) keep a membrane fluid; saturated tails pack tightly and make it less fluid, more viscous** (p7; p6 panel b). Hardness: stated, then shown side by side (fluid vs viscous).
12. **Membranes must be fluid to work; they are usually about as fluid as salad oil** (p7). Hardness: stated; the salad oil comparison is the slide's own.
13. **Cholesterol is a steroid in the animal cell membrane that buffers fluidity: at warm temperatures (37 °C) it restrains phospholipid movement; at cool temperatures it prevents tight packing and keeps the membrane fluid** (p6 panel c; p8). Hardness: stated, with a figure (cholesterol between the tails).

## Vocabulary

- **Plasma membrane**: the boundary of the living cell.
- **Selective permeability**: the membrane lets some substances cross more easily than others (named here; taught in lesson 3).
- **Phospholipid**: a lipid with a phosphate-containing head and two fatty-acid tails; the most abundant lipid in the plasma membrane.
- **Amphipathic**: having both a hydrophilic and a hydrophobic region.
- **Hydrophilic head**: the water-loving part of a phospholipid.
- **Hydrophobic tail**: the water-hating part, made of fatty-acid hydrocarbon chains.
- **Bilayer**: the two-layer sheet of phospholipids, tails facing inward.
- **Fluid mosaic model**: the membrane is a fluid structure with a mosaic of proteins embedded in it.
- **Lateral movement**: a lipid moving sideways within its own layer.
- **Flip-flop**: a lipid moving from one layer of the bilayer to the other (transverse movement).
- **Fluid / viscous**: flowing and loosely packed; thick and tightly packed.
- **Solid state** (of a membrane): the state a membrane switches to as it cools.
- **Saturated fatty acid**: a fatty-acid tail with no double bonds; straight, packs tightly.
- **Unsaturated fatty acid**: a fatty-acid tail with one or more double bonds; the double bond makes a kink.
- **Kink**: the bend at a double bond in an unsaturated tail.
- **Cholesterol**: a steroid in animal cell membranes; it modulates fluidity at warm and cool temperatures.
- **Steroid**: a lipid with four fused carbon rings (the cholesterol shape).

## Questions

No question slides; the narration step writes checks and practice.

Question ideas this material supports (each with what it tests):

1. Which end of a phospholipid is hydrophilic, and which is hydrophobic? (amphipathic regions)
2. Why do phospholipids in water form a bilayer, with heads facing the water and tails facing inward? (hydrophobic effect, bilayer)
3. Which kind of movement is rare: lateral movement or flip-flop? (lipid mobility, the two kinds of movement and their rates)
4. A membrane is cooled and becomes solid. Which tail type makes that more likely, saturated or unsaturated? (fatty-acid tails and packing)
5. What does the kink in an unsaturated tail do to the bilayer, and why does the membrane stay more fluid? (kink, looser packing)
6. In the fluid mosaic model, what does "fluid" mean and what does "mosaic" mean? (the model's two parts)
7. At 37 °C, does cholesterol increase or decrease phospholipid movement? (cholesterol's warm-temperature effect)
8. At a cool temperature, what does cholesterol do to packing, and why does that keep the membrane fluid? (cholesterol's cool-temperature effect)
9. Name the two parts of the bilayer that face water, and the part that faces the other tails. (labels on the bilayer)
10. Why is "selective permeability" a consequence of the bilayer's structure? (a preview of lesson 3; the bilayer lets nonpolar molecules cross but blocks charged ones)

### Questions

- `q1` id `m-faces`, `choice`: "In a bilayer in water, which part of each phospholipid faces the water?" Right: the hydrophilic head. Wrong: the hydrophobic tails (they hate water and tuck inward); both ends equally (the ends differ, so the molecule is amphipathic); neither (the outer heads touch the water on both sides). Picture: the settled bilayer with heads and tails lit one at a time.
- `q2` id `m-motion`, `grid` (lateral movement / flip-flop, `multi: false`): rows: "a lipid swaps places with its neighbour in the same layer" (lateral); "a lipid moves from one layer to the other" (flip-flop); "happens about ten million times per second" (lateral); "happens about once per month" (flip-flop). Why: lateral movement stays within one layer and is fast; flip-flop needs the head to cross the tails and is rare. Wrong hint: "lateral" on the layer-to-layer row: that jump is flip-flop. Picture: the bilayer with the two arrows.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice"):
  - `p-parts` `grid` (hydrophilic / hydrophobic, `multi: false`): "the phosphate head" (hydrophilic); "the fatty acid tails" (hydrophobic); "the part that faces the extracellular fluid" (hydrophilic); "the part that faces the middle of the bilayer" (hydrophobic).   - `p-why` `choice`: "Why do phospholipids in water form a bilayer?" Right: the hydrophilic heads face the water and the hydrophobic tails tuck together away from it.   - `p-flip` `choice`: "A phospholipid moves from the outer layer to the inner layer. What is this called, and how often does it happen?" Right: flip-flop, about once per month.
