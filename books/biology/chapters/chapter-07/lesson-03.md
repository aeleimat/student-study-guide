## Concepts

1. **Concept 7.2 opener** (p15). A cell must exchange materials with its surroundings, a process controlled by the plasma membrane. Hardness: stated.
2. **Plasma membranes are selectively permeable and regulate the cell's molecular traffic** (p15). Hardness: stated; the term is named, not explained. The explanation (the bilayer and the proteins) comes on the next slides.
3. **Hydrophobic (nonpolar) molecules dissolve in the lipid bilayer and cross it rapidly** (p16; hydrocarbons as the example). Hardness: stated.
4. **Polar molecules such as sugars do not cross the membrane easily** (p16). Hardness: stated; no reason given on the slide, so the lesson must supply it (hydrophilic, not soluble in the hydrophobic core).
5. **Transport proteins let hydrophilic substances across the membrane** (p17). Hardness: stated.
6. **Channel proteins have a hydrophilic channel that molecules or ions use as a tunnel** (p17). Hardness: stated.
7. **Aquaporins are channel proteins that facilitate the passage of water** (p17). Hardness: stated; the name is introduced here and not used again on these slides.
8. **Carrier proteins bind a molecule and change shape to shuttle it across** (p18). Hardness: stated; the shape change is the only mechanism given.
9. **A transport protein is specific for the substance it moves** (p18). Hardness: stated.
10. **Concept 7.3: passive transport is diffusion across a membrane with no energy investment** (p19). Hardness: stated (title).
11. **Diffusion is the tendency of molecules to spread out evenly into the available space** (p19). Hardness: stated.
12. **Each molecule moves randomly, but a population can show net movement in one direction** (p19). Hardness: stated; this is the key idea the lesson should show with a picture of random motion.
13. **Dynamic equilibrium: as many molecules cross one way as the other** (p19; p20 equilibrium panels). Hardness: stated, then shown. The equilibrium point is a steady state, not a stop.
14. **One solute and two solutes diffuse independently**: each runs down its own gradient, and the two net movements can go in opposite directions (p20, panel b: orange right, purple left). Hardness: shown by the figure only; the words "two solutes" are the only text.
15. **Concentration gradient: the difference in a substance's concentration from one area to another** (p21). Hardness: stated.
16. **Substances diffuse down their gradient and no work is needed to move them that way** (p21). Hardness: stated.
17. **Diffusion across a biological membrane is passive transport because it needs no energy from the cell** (p21). Hardness: stated.
18. **Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane** (p22). Hardness: stated.
19. **Water diffuses from the region of lower solute concentration to the region of higher solute concentration** (p22; direction shown in the p23 U-tube). Hardness: stated; the direction is the one the student most often reverses, so the lesson should check it.
20. **The U-tube: water moves toward the sugar side until the sugar concentrations match** (p23). Hardness: shown by the figure. The slide's final panel has equal sugar concentration but a higher water level on the sugar side. The slide does not explain why (hydrostatic pressure); the lesson must not say the levels end equal.

## Vocabulary

- **Plasma membrane, selectively permeable**: the cell's outer membrane; lets some substances through and not others.
- **Hydrophobic (nonpolar), hydrophilic, polar**: the chemistry that decides whether a molecule crosses the bilayer on its own.
- **Lipid bilayer**: the membrane's phospholipid core, which nonpolar molecules dissolve in.
- **Transport protein, channel protein, aquaporin, carrier protein**: the three named kinds of transport protein on these slides, plus the aquaporin as a special channel.
- **Specific**: a transport protein moves one substance (or one kind).
- **Diffusion, net diffusion, dynamic equilibrium**: spreading out; the net one-way movement of a population; the steady state with equal crossings each way.
- **Concentration gradient**: the difference in concentration from one area to another.
- **Passive transport**: movement across a membrane with no energy input from the cell.
- **Osmosis**: diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
- **Solute, solvent (water)**: the dissolved substance (sugar, dye) and the water it is dissolved in.

## Questions

No question slides; the narration step writes checks and practice. Ideas this material supports, each with what it tests:

1. Why do hydrocarbons cross the bilayer quickly while sugars do not? Tests the polar/nonpolar rule (p16) applied to a new molecule.
2. Given a list of molecules, which need a transport protein and which can cross alone? Tests the bilayer-permeability rule (p16) against the transport-protein rule (p17).
3. What is the difference between a channel protein and a carrier protein? Tests the tunnel versus shape-change distinction (p17, p18).
4. What is an aquaporin, and what does it carry? Tests the named special case (p17).
5. A transport protein moves glucose but not sodium. What does that show? Tests specificity (p18).
6. A population of molecules shows net movement to the right while each molecule moves at random. Explain. Tests random motion versus net movement (p19).
7. At dynamic equilibrium, is any molecule still moving? Tests the steady-state idea (p19, p20).
8. In the two-solute figure, why do the two solutes move in opposite directions? Tests independent gradients (p20, panel b).
9. Diffusion of a substance is passive transport. Why does that need no energy? Tests the no-work definition (p21).
10. Water is on the left of a membrane with less sugar than the right. Which way does water move, and why? Tests the osmosis direction (p22, p23), the one students most often reverse.
