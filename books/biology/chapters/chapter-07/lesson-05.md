## Concepts

1. **Facilitated diffusion is passive transport aided by transport proteins; it still runs down the concentration gradient** (p29). Hardness: stated in the bullets, with the diffusion/facilitated-diffusion contrast drawn on p35.
2. **Channel proteins are corridors for one specific molecule or ion** (p29, p31a). Hardness: stated; the channel panel shows a solute passing straight through a pore.
3. **Aquaporins are channel proteins that facilitate the diffusion of water** (p30). Hardness: stated, not drawn.
4. **Gated ion channels open or close in response to a stimulus** (p30). Hardness: stated, not drawn; the stimulus is not named on the slide.
5. **Carrier proteins change shape slightly to move the solute-binding site across the membrane** (p30, p31b). Hardness: stated, then shown by two carrier states: solute bound facing out, then released facing in. The slide does not say which states are open where in words; the picture shows it.
6. **Active transport moves solutes against their concentration gradient, using energy, usually from ATP** (p32; title p32). Hardness: stated, with the word "against" in bold on p32.
7. **Active transport lets cells keep concentration gradients that differ from their surroundings, and it is done by specific membrane proteins** (p33). Hardness: stated.
8. **The sodium-potassium pump is one active transport system, and it runs as a cycle of six steps** (p33; cycle on p34). Hardness: named on p33, then drawn in six steps on p34. See Figures for the exact steps.
9. **Summary: passive transport (diffusion, facilitated diffusion) needs no energy; active transport needs ATP** (p35). Hardness: shown as one comparison figure; the bullet and the figure both carry it.

## Vocabulary

- **Facilitated diffusion**: passive movement of a solute across the membrane with a transport protein; still down its gradient.
- **Channel protein**: a protein that forms a corridor (pore) for a specific molecule or ion.
- **Aquaporin**: a channel protein for water.
- **Gated channel (ion channel)**: a channel that opens or closes in response to a stimulus.
- **Carrier protein**: a transport protein that binds the solute and changes shape to move it across.
- **Solute, solute-binding site**: the molecule or ion being moved; the part of the carrier that holds it.
- **Active transport**: movement of a solute against its concentration gradient, using energy.
- **ATP, ADP, phosphate (Pi)**: the energy carrier; the spent form; the phosphate group that ATP gives to the pump.
- **Sodium-potassium pump**: the active transport protein that moves Na⁺ out and K⁺ in.
- **Passive transport, diffusion**: the group that needs no energy input (the bracket on p35).
- **Concentration gradient**: a difference in solute concentration across the membrane; "down" is passive, "against" is active.
- **Extracellular fluid / cytoplasm**: the two sides of the membrane (labelled on p31 and p34).

   - **Step 1.** The pump is open toward the cytoplasm. Three Na⁺ bind inside the pump (two are shown inside, one enters from the cytoplasm side, red arrow into the pump).

Re-check the orientation against the picture before narrating it: the cue is the K⁺ bound on the upper side.
   - **Step 6.** The pump opens to the cytoplasm and releases the two K⁺ (red arrow, K⁺ outside the pump, K⁺ in the cytoplasm), then returns to step 1.

   Each panel: the membrane is drawn as grey heads and tan tails (cream-gold tails); the pump is indigo. The printed "Pi" in step 4 is a P with a subscript i.

## Numbers

- Per cycle: 1 ATP (hydrolysed to ADP plus Pi); 3 Na⁺ pumped out; 2 K⁺ pumped in. This is shown by the six-step figure (p34); the slide does not write "3" and "2" in words. Teach it as the picture's count.
- The slides give no quantities for concentrations, rates or sizes. Concentration is shown only as [Na⁺] high/low and [K⁺] high/low on p34, step 1.
- Slide numbers: concept headings "Concept 7.4" (p32).

9. On the summary slide, which panel needs no protein and which needs ATP? (diffusion, no protein; active transport, ATP)
10. Why does the pump need to alternate between the two sides of the membrane? (so the binding site is open to one side at a time; a carrier-style shape change)
