## Concepts

In slide order. Hardness: definition only / explained / quantified.

1. **Polynucleotide** (s13). Nucleotide polymers are linked into a polynucleotide. Hardness: definition only.
2. **Phosphodiester link, 3′ to 5′** (s13, s15). Adjacent nucleotides are joined by a covalent bond between the –OH on the 3′ carbon of one nucleotide and the phosphate on the 5′ carbon of the next. The s15 figure labels the bond "phosphodiester bond", with an "ester bond" at each end of the phosphate. Hardness: explained (figure shows three nucleotides T, C, G, with 5′ and 3′ circled).
3. **Sugar-phosphate backbone with bases as appendages** (s13, s16). Repeating sugar-phosphate units; bases hang off. Hardness: explained.
4. **Base sequence is unique for each gene** (s13, s14). The sequence along a DNA or mRNA polymer is unique per gene; one DNA molecule includes many genes. Hardness: stated, not explained.
5. **Double helix** (s14, s16). Two polynucleotides spiral around an imaginary axis. Hardness: explained (figure).
6. **Antiparallel** (s14, s16). The two backbones run in opposite 5′ to 3′ directions. Hardness: defined; the figure shows 5′ and 3′ ends at opposite ends of each strand.
7. **Base pairing by hydrogen bonds** (s14, s16). A always with T, G always with C. Hardness: stated rule; the figure shows dotted H-bond lines.
8. **DNA replication, semiconservative** (s16, summary s20). Old strands unwind, a free nucleotide is about to be added to each new strand; each daughter molecule has one old and one new strand. Hardness: shown in a figure; named only on the summary slide (s20).
9. **Percent rule** (questions only, Q37-39). If A is x percent, T is x percent, and G = C = (100 - 2x) / 2. Hardness: quantified (in questions).

## Vocabulary

- **Polynucleotide**: a polymer of nucleotides.
- **Phosphodiester bond**: covalent bond joining the 3′ carbon (via its OH) of one nucleotide to the 5′ phosphate of the next.
- **Sugar-phosphate backbone**: the repeating sugar and phosphate line of a strand.
- **5′ end / 3′ end**: the ends of a strand; the 5′ end has a free phosphate on a 5′ carbon, the 3′ end a free hydroxyl on a 3′ carbon.
- **Double helix**: two polynucleotides spiraling around one axis.
- **Antiparallel**: running in opposite 5′ to 3′ directions.
- **Complementary bases / base pair**: A with T, G with C, joined by hydrogen bonds.
- **Gene**: a stretch of DNA with its own base sequence (one molecule has many).
- **Old strand / new strand; template**: the parts of a replicating helix.
- **Semiconservative replication**: each daughter helix keeps one old strand and gets one new strand.

## Possible problems

1. **s13 does not name the reaction that forms the link.** It is a dehydration (water leaves), as the summary slide describes for polymers. Not stated on s13.
2. **s13 "sequence of bases... is unique for each gene".** Read as "different from gene to gene"; "unique" is loose (repeats and shared motifs exist).
3. **s14 "two backbones run in opposite 5′ to 3′ directions"** is compressed. Clearer: where one strand runs 5′ to 3′ downward, its partner runs 5′ to 3′ upward.
4. **s14 "imaginary axis".** Fine for a lecture; the lesson draws a ladder first, then twists it.
5. **s14 states the pairing rule with no reason.** The reason (a two-ring purine faces a one-ring pyrimidine; donors and acceptors line up) is not given.
6. **s15 shows one nucleotide of each of T, C, G, not a real sequence.** It is a bond figure.
7. **s15 "ester bond" twice inside "phosphodiester bond".** The bond is two ester linkages to one phosphate; the lesson uses only the name.
8. **s16 does not say the new nucleotide is added at the 3′ end.** True and needed: the new strand grows from its 3′ OH, the same bond as in concept 2.
9. **s16 shows replication without naming it; "semiconservative" appears only on s20.**
10. **s16 omits the enzyme (DNA polymerase), the leading and lagging strands and the fork's two directions.**
11. **No hydrogen-bond counts on the slides.**
12. **s17 is the summary table of four classes, not a nucleic-acid figure.**
13. **The slide text layer writes the prime mark as "¢".** Written as ′ (U+2032).
14. **Q46 asks for the incorrect statement.** Do not copy "hydrolysis builds polymers" as a fact.

## Errata

Decisions on the flagged problems (numbers refer to "Possible problems").

1. The link is shown as dehydration: a water molecule leaves when the 3′ OH meets the 5′ phosphate (matches lesson 1).
2. "Unique for each gene" is taught as "the order of bases differs from gene to gene and carries the message".
3. Antiparallel is shown as opposite 5′ to 3′ directions, with the ends labelled on both strands.
4. The picture straightens the helix to a ladder and then twists it.
5. A light reason is given: a two-ring purine always faces a one-ring pyrimidine, so every rung has the same width (ties to lesson 3). It is an addition to the slides.
6-7. Only the name "phosphodiester bond" is used.
8. New nucleotides are added at the 3′ hydroxyl of the growing strand (stated; extends s16).
9. "Semiconservative replication" is named in the lesson.
10. Enzyme, fork directions and the leading and lagging strands are not taught.
11. H-bond counts (2 and 3) are not used.
12. s17 is not used as a figure; the wrap ties lessons 1 to 4 together in words.
13. The prime is written ′.
14. Not used as a fact.

### Questions

- `q1` id `l4-link`, choice, "In a strand, which two positions are joined by a phosphodiester bond?" Options: (a) the 3′ hydroxyl of one nucleotide and the 5′ phosphate of the next (right); (b) the 5′ phosphates of two neighbours; (c) two bases, by hydrogen bonds; (d) the 1′ carbons of two sugars. Why: the strand is built end to end, OH to phosphate. Wrong (b): two phosphates would not make a sugar-phosphate-sugar line; (c): that holds two strands together; (d): the 1′ carbon holds the base.
- `q2` id `l4-comp`, blanks, "The partner of 5′ A T G C C A 3′ reads, from its 5′ end: ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___". Answer T G G C A T (the partner is 3′ T A C G G T 5′, read from its 5′ end). Hint: pair each base, then flip the ends. Check the answer order against the picture's labels.
- `q3` id `l4-pct2`, blanks, "A DNA molecule is 35 % guanine. Cytosine: ___ %, adenine: ___ %." Answers 35 and 15 (100 - 70 = 30, half is 15). Hint: G pairs with C, then A and T share the rest. Compute in the page's code.
