## Concepts

1. **Four classes of large biological molecules** (p2). Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids. Hardness: definition only (list).
2. **Macromolecule** (p2). A large molecule of thousands of covalently connected atoms. Hardness: definition only.
3. **Structure and function are inseparable** (p2). Hardness: stated, not shown here.
4. **Polymer and monomer** (p3). A polymer is a long molecule of many similar building blocks; the blocks are monomers. Hardness: explained.
5. **Three of the four classes are polymers** (p3): carbohydrates, proteins, nucleic acids. Lipids are not (p3 by omission; p18 states "Lipids are not true polymers"). Hardness: stated.
6. **Dehydration (condensation) reaction** (p4, p5a). Two monomers bond with the loss of a water molecule. Hardness: explained with a figure (HO and H ends, H2O leaves, new bond).
7. **Enzymes speed up dehydration** (p4). Enzymes are macromolecules. Hardness: definition only.
8. **Hydrolysis** (p4, p5b). The reverse of dehydration: a water molecule is added and a bond breaks, giving monomers. Hardness: explained with the figure.
9. **Diversity of polymers** (p6). Thousands of different macromolecules per cell; variation among cells, more within a species, more between species; an immense variety from a small set of monomers. Hardness: explained qualitatively (no counts).

## Vocabulary

- **Macromolecule**: very large molecule, thousands of covalently bonded atoms.
- **Polymer**: long molecule of many similar building blocks.
- **Monomer**: small building-block molecule.
- **Condensation / dehydration reaction**: two monomers join, losing a water molecule.
- **Enzyme**: macromolecule that speeds up a reaction (here dehydration).
- **Hydrolysis**: adding water to break a bond and split a polymer into monomers.
- **Covalent bond**: bond made by shared electrons.
- **Carbohydrate, lipid, protein, nucleic acid**: the four classes.

## Possible problems

1. **p2 "four classes of large biological molecules" vs p3 "three of the four are polymers".** Lipids are called macromolecules but are not polymers (p18: "not true polymers"). A reader may think all four are chains.
2. **p2 "thousands of covalently connected atoms".** Lipids (fats) are far smaller than that, so the definition fits the three polymer classes best.
3. **p4 "Enzymes are macromolecules that speed up the dehydration process".** Enzymes also speed up hydrolysis (digestion); the slide mentions only dehydration. Enzymes are mostly proteins and are not used up (neither is said).
4. **p4 "condensation reaction or more specifically a dehydration reaction".** Dehydration is condensation in which the small molecule lost is water; the slide does not explain this.
5. **p5 figure shows 1-2-3-4 as a chain with HO and H ends, not real molecules.** Schematic; the real group on each monomer is not shown (it varies by class).
6. **p6 "vary even more between species".** Qualitative, no data; the monomer counts (20 amino acids, 4 nucleotides) are not on the slides.
7. **p18 list is garbled.** "Carbohydrates, Lipids" under heading 1, then "Proteins, Nucleic acids" run into "Monomer: ..." under heading 2. Read as the four classes.
8. **Slides 1-6 give no example of a real polymer.** The lesson uses a bowl of pasta (starch, a carbohydrate) as an outside everyday scene.

## Errata

1. Lipids: taught as the fourth class that is large but not a chain of monomers (so not a true polymer, as p18 says). "Macromolecule" is introduced for the four classes as the slide does, without insisting that lipids have thousands of atoms.
2. Enzymes: the lesson says enzymes speed up dehydration (p4), shows the enzyme free to work again (outside, standard), and says gut enzymes also drive hydrolysis (outside, standard). "Enzymes are proteins" is not taught here.
3. Condensation is not used as a second term; the voice says dehydration.
4. Bond rule: n monomers have n - 1 bonds, so building or fully breaking a chain of n releases or uses n - 1 water molecules (shown from the figure: 4 units, 3 bonds).
5. Starch/glucose, 20 amino acids, glycerol plus three fatty acid tails, and lipids being grouped by avoiding water are outside facts (standard), used only in the new beats `examples` and `lipids`. Digestion and the pasta bowl are outside everyday examples for the scene only; no figures are stated about them.
6. Diversity: no counts of monomer kinds are given; the letters analogy (26 letters) is outside and labelled as an analogy.
7. The slide 18 formatting problem is ignored; its four sections are used as the wrap.
8. Q21's wrong circle is noted for lesson 2.

### Questions

- `q3` id `c-pair` (new, after `lipids`), choice, "Which monomer and polymer pairing is correct?" Options: (a) amino acid and carbohydrate; (b) glucose and protein; (c) nucleotide and nucleic acid (right); (d) fatty acid and nucleic acid. Why: nucleotides make DNA and RNA; amino acids make proteins; glucose makes starch.
- `q1` id `c-count`, choice, "A chain of 5 monomers is fully digested into single monomers. Which reaction is this, and how many water molecules does it use?" Options: (a) dehydration, 4 water released; (b) hydrolysis, 4 water added (right); (c) hydrolysis, 5 water added; (d) dehydration, 5 water released. Why: breaking a chain apart is hydrolysis, which adds water; 5 units are joined by 4 bonds, so 4 breaks use 4 water. Wrong (c): water is added once per bond, not per monomer. Wrong (a), (d): that is building, not breaking.
- `q2` id `c-diverse`, choice, "Cells make thousands of different polymers from a small set of monomers. Why is that possible?" Options: (a) each polymer uses its own special monomers; (b) the same monomers can be joined in different orders and lengths (right); (c) water changes the monomers each time; (d) enzymes become part of every polymer. Why: like letters making many words. Wrong (a): the set is small; (c) water only leaves or enters at the bond; (d) enzymes are not consumed.
- `final1` ('Chapter practice'):
  - `p-terms` grid, true/false: a polymer is a chain of many similar monomers (T); lipids are polymers like proteins (F, large but not chains of monomers); dehydration forms a bond and releases water (T); hydrolysis builds polymers by removing water (F, it adds water and breaks bonds); enzymes speed up dehydration (T).
  - `p-build` blanks: "A cell joins 6 monomers into one chain by dehydration. Bonds formed: ___; water molecules released: ___" 5, 5 (n - 1).
  - `p-break` blanks: "A chain of 8 monomers is fully hydrolysed. Water molecules used: ___; monomers released: ___" 7, 8.
  - `p-split` blanks (new): "A polymer is fully hydrolysed using 9 water molecules, one per bond. It had ___ monomers." 10 (bonds + 1).
  - `p-pairs` (new) grid, true/false: starch is made of glucose (T); a protein is made of nucleotides (F, amino acids); DNA is made of nucleotides (T); a fat is a long chain of identical fatty acids (F, glycerol plus three tails).
  - `p-lipid` choice: "Which class of large biological molecule is NOT built from repeating monomers?" Right: lipids. Wrong: carbohydrates, proteins, nucleic acids (all polymers).
