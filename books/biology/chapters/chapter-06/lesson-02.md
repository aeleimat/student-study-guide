## Concepts

1. **Concept 6.3 opener** (p8). Genetic information is housed in the nucleus and carried out by ribosomes. Hardness: stated.
2. **Eukaryotic cells have internal membranes that partition the cell into organelles** (p8). Hardness: stated.
3. **Plant and animal cells have almost the same organelles** (p8; shown by the maps on p9 and p10). Hardness: stated, then shown by two maps side by side.
4. **Whole-cell maps: animal (p9) and plant (p10)**. The same organelle set, with the animal cell's centrosome and flagellum and the plant cell's cell wall, central vacuole, chloroplasts and plasmodesmata. Hardness: identification (labels).
5. **The nucleus holds most of the cell's DNA and most of its genes; it is usually the largest organelle** (p8, p11). Hardness: stated.
6. **Nuclear envelope: a double membrane, each membrane a lipid bilayer, enclosing the nucleus and separating it from the cytoplasm** (p11, p12). Hardness: stated; inner and outer membrane shown in the p12 cutaway.
7. **Nuclear pores regulate entry and exit of molecules** (p11, p12). Hardness: stated; pore complex shown in TEM (p12).
8. **Nuclear lamina: a protein layer that maintains the nucleus's shape** (p11; TEM on p12). Hardness: stated.
9. **Chromatin is DNA plus protein** (p13; p9 and p10 labels, p12 "DNA + protein" handwriting). Hardness: definition.
10. **Chromatin condenses to form chromosomes** (p13). Hardness: stated, no timing given.
11. **The nucleolus is an area in the nucleus where ribosomal RNA (rRNA) is made** (p13; p9 and p10 labels and handwriting "produces rRNA"; p12 "makes rRNA"). Hardness: stated.
12. **Ribosomes are particles made of rRNA and protein** (p14, p15). Hardness: definition.
13. **Ribosomes carry out protein synthesis in two places: free in the cytosol, or bound to the outside of the ER or the nuclear envelope** (p14). Hardness: stated.
14. **Free and bound ribosomes look alike in a TEM; bound ones sit on the ER surface** (p15). Hardness: shown by the figure.
15. **A ribosome has a large subunit and a small subunit** (p15 diagram). Hardness: definition; not to scale.
16. **Ribosomes make proteins, and so do rough ER (studded with bound ribosomes) and smooth ER (none)** (p9, p10 labels and handwriting; p15). Hardness: labelled only; the ER itself is taught in lesson 3.

## Vocabulary

- **Eukaryotic cell**: a cell with internal membranes and organelles, including a nucleus.
- **Organelle**: a membrane-bounded compartment (or structure) with a job in the cell.
- **Nucleus, nuclear envelope**: the organelle that holds most DNA; its double membrane.
- **Nuclear pore, pore complex**: the gate through the nuclear envelope; the protein structure that forms it.
- **Nuclear lamina**: protein layer that gives the nucleus its shape.
- **Chromatin, chromosome**: DNA plus protein; chromatin condensed into chromosomes.
- **Nucleolus, rRNA**: the nuclear area where rRNA is made; ribosomal RNA.
- **Ribosome**: particle of rRNA and protein that makes proteins.
- **Free ribosome, bound ribosome**: in the cytosol; on the outside of the ER or nuclear envelope.
- **Large subunit, small subunit**: the two halves of a ribosome.
- **Cytosol, cytoplasm**: the fluid inside the cell membrane; the cell's contents outside the nucleus.
- **Rough ER, smooth ER**: named on the maps (taught in lesson 3).
- **Plasma membrane**: the cell's outer membrane.
- **Cytoskeleton, microfilaments, intermediate filaments, microtubules**: named on the maps (taught in lessons 6-7).
- **Centrosome, flagellum, microvilli**: animal-cell labels on p9.
- **Central vacuole, cell wall, plasmodesmata, chloroplast**: plant-cell labels on p10.
- **Lysosome, peroxisome, Golgi apparatus, mitochondrion**: named on the maps (taught in lessons 3-5).
- **TEM**: transmission electron microscope, the source of the micrographs.

## Questions

2. What is chromatin made of, and what does it become when it condenses?

## Possible problems

1. **Handwritten, wrong (p13, red numbered list under the nucleolus):** "1 gives the ribosomes their shape; 2 aligns tRNA and mRNA during translation; 3 ensures accuracy; 4 catalyzes peptide bond formation." These describe the ribosome, not the nucleolus. The nucleolus makes rRNA; the ribosome's rRNA and protein do the shaping, aligning and bond-making during translation. Do not teach them as nucleolus functions. (The "catalyzes peptide bond formation" point is a true ribosome fact, but not a nucleolus one.)
2. **Printed, loose (p8):** "Ribosomes use the information from the DNA to make proteins." Ribosomes read mRNA, which is copied from DNA; they do not read DNA directly. Teach it as mRNA-mediated (lesson 2 of Chapter 5 handled the same point). Keep this loose wording out of the narration.
3. **Printed, true but loose (p8, p11):** "The nucleus contains most of the DNA" and "holds most genes". Mitochondria and chloroplasts carry their own DNA, so "most" is right and "all" would be wrong. Teach it as "most", not "all".
4. **Printed, loose (p13):** "Chromatin condenses to form chromosomes" gives no timing. Chromosomes are visible as condensed chromatin mainly during cell division; the slide implies it is always so. Teach the timing from the textbook, not from the slide.
5. **Printed, loose (p14):** "Ribosomes carry out protein synthesis in two locations." The slide lists two places for the cell's main ribosomes; mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own ribosomes too. Keep to "two places" for the cytoplasm and nuclear envelope, and do not claim it is the whole cell.
6. **Picture could mislead (p9):** the animal cell has a flagellum, and most animal cells have none (sperm are the usual example). Teach it as a feature of some animal cells, not all. The centrosome is also animal-only (not on the plant map).
7. **Picture could mislead (p12):** the rough ER in the cutaway is drawn continuous with the outer nuclear membrane. The slide does not state this; it is true in Campbell biology, so it may be taught as a fact, labelled as drawn.
8. **Picture could mislead (p12, p15):** the close-up of the envelope and the ribosome diagram are not to scale; the pore complex and the ribosome look much larger against the nucleus and the 0.5 µm bar than they are. Tag both "not to scale" and do not quote sizes from them.
9. **Handwritten, OK (p9, p10):** all the handwritten notes on the two maps are correct (rough ER with ribosomes; smooth ER with no ribosomes and lipid synthesis; nucleolus makes rRNA; chloroplast does photosynthesis; central vacuole stores water; lysosome digests and recycles; and so on). The notes for the cytoskeleton (shape and movement; support; organelle movement) are loose but not wrong.
10. **Handwriting, unreadable or stray (p8, p11):** a red "divide" over "partition" (fine as a gloss); a blue circle on "most" (fine); on p11 a stray "Nuclear" tag by the bullet and blue and Arabic marks over "encloses" that cannot be read reliably. Nothing in them is used.

## Errata

1. p13 handwritten list (shapes ribosomes, aligns tRNA and mRNA, accuracy, peptide bonds): not used as nucleolus facts. They describe the ribosome. The nucleolus makes rRNA and builds ribosome subunits; the voice only says the ribosome grips messenger RNA and builds a polypeptide.
2. p8 "Ribosomes use the information from the DNA": taught as ribosomes read the messenger RNA copy, not the DNA (refers back to Chapter 5 lesson 2). Beat `ribo` says it outright.
3. "Most" DNA and genes: said "most, not all" (mitochondria and chloroplasts carry a little DNA).
4. Chromatin to chromosomes: taught as "when a cell is about to divide", from Campbell, not the slide.
5. "Two locations" for ribosomes: said "some free, others bound", no claim that these are the only places in the whole cell.
6. Flagellum: "some animal cells, like a sperm cell". Centrosome and lysosomes are "on this map" animal-only (the plant map does not draw them).
7. p12 rough ER continuous with the outer nuclear membrane: said "as drawn here". Tag "not to scale" on the envelope close-up and the ribosome.
8. No sizes are quoted (no scale bars used). Handwriting on the two maps (all correct) is not used as a source.

## Plan

Taught, in order: (1) an everyday backdrop (leaf and cheek), then the animal-cell map with nucleus and ribosomes lit; (2) compare the animal and plant maps: shared set, plant-only (wall, central vacuole, chloroplasts, plasmodesmata), animal-only on this map (centrosome, lysosomes, and a flagellum on some cells); quick check q1; (3) the nucleus: largest organelle, most DNA and genes, double-membrane envelope, nuclear pores and pore complexes (the gate the mRNA used in Chapter 5), nuclear lamina, rough ER continuous with the outer membrane; (4) chromatin (DNA + protein), chromosomes when dividing, nucleolus makes rRNA and builds ribosome subunits; quick check q2; (5) ribosomes: rRNA + protein, large and small subunit, read the mRNA not the DNA, free vs bound (same look, different place); (6) a worked case joining both stops: gene to mRNA out through the pore, subunits out through the pore, subunits clamp on the mRNA, free ribosome (protein works in the cytosol) vs bound (protein mostly for a membrane or export, lesson 3); (7) wrap and 5 practice questions.
Dropped: tRNA and the mechanics of translation, sizes, the lamina's proteins, the cytoskeleton and other organelles on the maps beyond naming them (later lessons).
Order: intro, maps, q1, nucleus, inside, q2, ribo, example, wrap, final1, finish.
Pacing: about 700 words of voice (261 s measured), 9 narrated lesson beats plus q1, q2, final1 and finish, 7 questions (2 checks inside, q1 and q2, plus 5 practice). Voice total is in the reply; target 250-330 s.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-cells`, `grid` (animal / plant / both, `multi: false`): "Which cell has it?" Rows: cell wall (plant); chloroplast (plant); centrosome (animal; on this map); nucleus (both); rough ER (both); central vacuole (plant). Why: only the plant cell has the wall, chloroplasts and large central vacuole; the animal map has the centrosome; both cells share the nucleus and ER. Wrong hints: "both" on a plant-only row: the animal map has none; "plant" on nucleus: animal cells are eukaryotic too and have a nucleus.
- `q2` id `c-nucleolus`, `pickEls`: "Click the part of the nucleus that makes rRNA." Stations: nucleolus (right), chromatin, nuclear pore, nuclear lamina. Why: the nucleolus is where rRNA is made. Wrong: chromatin is DNA plus protein; the pore only controls traffic; the lamina is a protein net that keeps the shape.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice"):
  - `p-job` `grid` (`multi: false`), columns "gate", "keeps the shape", "makes rRNA", "holds DNA and protein": nuclear pore (gate); nuclear lamina (keeps the shape); nucleolus (makes rRNA); chromatin (holds DNA and protein). Each why states the job. Picture: the nucleus with the four parts lit one at a time.
  - `p-made` `choice`: "What is a ribosome made of?" Right: rRNA and protein. Wrong: DNA and protein (that is chromatin); a lipid bilayer (that is a membrane such as the envelope); protein only (it has rRNA too). Picture: a split ribosome.
  - `p-where` `grid` (free / bound / both, `multi: false`): "works in the cytosol and makes proteins that function there" (free); "sits on the outside of the rough ER" (bound); "is attached to the nuclear envelope" (bound); "is made of rRNA and protein" (both); "has a large and a small subunit" (both).   - `p-dna` `choice`: "Which sentence about DNA in a eukaryotic cell is right?" Right: most of it is in the nucleus, with a little in mitochondria and chloroplasts. Wrong: all of it is in the nucleus (the two organelles keep some); none is in the nucleus (that is a prokaryote's nucleoid, with no envelope); it is in the nucleolus only (the nucleolus makes rRNA).
