## Concepts

1. **The cytoskeleton is a network of fibres in the cytoplasm that organises the cell's structures and activities and anchors many organelles** (p34). Hardness: definition.
2. **Three main fibre types, ranked by thickness**: microtubules (thickest), microfilaments (thinnest), intermediate filaments (mid-range) (p34). Hardness: stated.
3. **Roles of the cytoskeleton: support and shape, motility with motor proteins, regulation of biochemical activity** (p35). Hardness: stated.
4. **Vesicles move along microtubule tracks; ATP-powered motor proteins walk along the track and grip a receptor on the vesicle** (p35, p36). Hardness: explained by the figure (a schematic and an EM of vesicles on a microtubule).
5. **Microtubules are hollow rods, about 25 nm across, 200 nm to 25 µm long** (p35). Hardness: stated (numbers).
6. **Microtubule functions: shaping the cell, guiding organelle movement, separating chromosomes in cell division** (p35, p37). Hardness: stated.
7. **A microtubule wall is 13 columns of tubulin molecules; tubulin dimers (alpha and beta tubulin) are the repeating unit** (p37). Hardness: explained by the figure.
8. **Microfilaments (actin filaments) are two intertwined strands of actin, about 7 nm across** (p38). Hardness: explained by the figure (actin subunit drawing).
9. **Microfilament functions: cell shape, changes in shape, muscle contraction, cytoplasmic streaming, cell motility, cell division** (p38). Hardness: stated.
10. **Intermediate filaments are supercoiled fibrous proteins of the keratin family, 8 to 12 nm across, and anchor the nucleus and some organelles; they form the nuclear lamina** (p39). Hardness: explained by the figure (rope-like subunit drawing). The slide says "keratin family" as the subunit; see Possible problems.
11. **The centrosome is a microtubule-organising centre, near the nucleus in many cells** (p40, p41). Hardness: definition.
12. **In animal cells the centrosome has a pair of centrioles, each with nine triplets of microtubules in a ring** (p40, p41). Hardness: explained by the figure (EM and drawing).
13. **Cilia and flagella are microtubule-containing extensions from some cells; microtubules control their beating** (p42). Hardness: definition.
14. **Cilia and flagella share a structure: a core of microtubules sheathed by the plasma membrane, a basal body that anchors them, and dynein, a motor protein that drives bending** (p43). Hardness: stated.
15. **Flagella move in an undulating wave; cilia beat in a power stroke and a recovery stroke** (p44). Hardness: explained by the figure (stroke drawings, photos with 5 µm and 15 µm bars).
16. **The 9 + 2 arrangement: nine outer microtubule doublets around two central microtubules, with radial spokes and cross-linking proteins; the basal body has nine triplets and no central pair** (p45, Figure 6.24). Hardness: explained by the figure (three panels and a 3D drawing).
17. **Dynein walking: dynein arms alternately grab, move and release the outer microtubules; cross-links limit the sliding, so the doublets bend the cilium or flagellum** (p46). Hardness: explained (the slide's chain: dynein movement, then limited sliding, then bending).

## Vocabulary

- **Cytoskeleton**: network of fibres throughout the cytoplasm that organises structures and activities.
- **Microtubule**: hollow rod of tubulin, the thickest cytoskeletal fibre.
- **Tubulin, tubulin dimer**: protein subunit of microtubules; a dimer is one alpha plus one beta tubulin.
- **Microfilament, actin filament, actin subunit**: the thinnest fibre, two strands of actin.
- **Intermediate filament, keratin, nuclear lamina**: mid-range fibre of coiled fibrous proteins; the lamina is the intermediate-filament layer under the nuclear envelope.
- **Motor protein, dynein, kinesin**: ATP-powered protein that moves along a fibre (dynein, kinesin on microtubules; myosin on microfilaments in other lessons).
- **Vesicle, receptor**: a membrane bubble carrying cargo; the receptor on it binds the motor protein.
- **Monorail**: the slide's word for a microtubule track.
- **Centrosome, microtubule-organising centre**: the region where microtubules grow from.
- **Centriole**: barrel of nine triplets of microtubules; a pair in animal centrosomes.
- **Cilium (pl. cilia), flagellum (pl. flagella)**: microtubule-containing extensions. Cilia are short and many; flagella are long and few (slide shows the contrast in the beat, not in number).
- **Basal body**: anchor at the base of a cilium or flagellum; nine triplets in a ring.
- **Doublet, triplet**: groups of two or three fused microtubules.
- **Radial spoke, central pair, cross-linking protein**: parts of the 9 + 2 core that hold the arrangement together.
- **Power stroke, recovery stroke**: the cilium's two beat phases.
- **Undulating movement**: flagellum wave (handwritten on slide 44).

## Numbers

- Microtubule diameter: 25 nm, lumen 15 nm (p37). 25 nm is 0.025 µm, one-tenth of the 0.25 µm bar on the EM.
- Microtubule length: 200 nm to 25 µm (p35).
- Microtubule wall: 13 columns of tubulin molecules (p37).
- Actin filament: 7 nm (p38).
- Intermediate filament: 8 to 12 nm (p39).
- Thickness ranking: microtubule 25 nm > intermediate filament 8 to 12 nm > microfilament 7 nm (p34, p37 to p39).
- Centrioles: a pair per centrosome in animal cells; nine triplets per centriole in a ring (p40). Nine triplets is 27 microtubules per centriole (derived from the slide, not printed).
- Cilium cross section: nine outer doublets plus two central microtubules (9 + 2; handwritten on p45; the printed label says "central microtubule", singular).
- Basal body: nine triplets (p45).
- Scale bars: 0.25 µm (p36 EM, p41 EM); 10 µm (p37 photo, p38 photo); 5 µm (p39 photo, p44 flagella photo); 15 µm (p44 cilia photo); 0.5 µm (p45 longitudinal cilium); 0.1 µm (p45 cross sections, both).
- Cilium length: not given on these slides (the 15 µm bar is on the cell-covered SEM).

## Questions

These slides have no question slides in this chapter, so there is nothing to copy. Raw material for the practice set; each is something a student should answer after the lesson:

1. Name the three cytoskeleton fibres in order from thickest to thinnest, and give each one's diameter.
2. What do motor proteins do on a microtubule, and what powers them? (Walk along it, using ATP; they carry vesicles and organelles.)
3. Why is a microtubule called a track, and what is it made of? (Tubulin dimers; 13 columns form the wall.)
4. What is the centrosome, and what is its microtubule-organising role? (A microtubule-organising centre near the nucleus.)
5. How many centrioles are in an animal-cell centrosome, and how are the microtubules arranged in each? (A pair; nine triplets in a ring.)
6. What is the 9 + 2 arrangement, and what do the nine and the two stand for? (Nine outer doublets, two central microtubules.)
7. What does a basal body do, and how many triplets does it have? (Anchors the cilium or flagellum; nine triplets.)
8. What is the difference between the flagellum's and the cilium's beat? (Flagellum: undulating wave; cilium: power stroke and recovery stroke.)
9. Which protein drives the bending, and how do the dynein arms make it bend? (Dynein; arms grab, move, release the outer doublets; cross-links limit the sliding, so the doublets bend.)
10. Which fibre type anchors the nucleus and forms the nuclear lamina? (Intermediate filaments.)

## Possible problems

1. **Printed, loose, p34**: "Intermediate filaments: fibers with diameters in a mid-range" gives no number here; the number (8 to 12 nm) comes on p39. Teach it with the number.
2. **Printed, loose, p39**: the protein subunit is given as "one of several different proteins of the keratin family", which is true for keratin fibres but is not the whole intermediate-filament family (the nuclear lamina is made of lamins, not shown). Do not say all intermediate filaments are keratin.
3. **Printed, loose, p35**: "Cytoskeletons may regulate biochemical activities" uses the plural "Cytoskeletons" for one structure; read it as "the cytoskeleton may regulate". Also "25 microns" is 25 µm; say micrometres in the voice.
4. **Printed, loose, p44**: the flagella and cilia drawings have no scale; the photos do (5 µm, 15 µm). Do not transfer the photo scale to the drawings, and do not state a cilium length, which the slide does not give.
5. **Printed, loose, p46**: "How dynein 'walking' moves flagella and cilia?" puts "walking" in quotes; dynein arms do not step like feet. Say the arms grab and release in turn.
6. **Printed, loose, p45**: the printed label is "Central microtubule" (singular) but the cross section has two central microtubules (9 + 2). The handwritten "+2 central microtubules" is right; teach two.
7. **Handwritten, loose, p44**: the note "Pushes fluid or moves the organism forward" sits beside the cilium power stroke, which is correct for cilia; but do not extend it to flagella (they undulate). Teach the two beats separately.
8. **Handwritten, loose, p38**: "movement of cytoplasm that circulates organelles inside the cell" describes cytoplasmic streaming; streaming carries organelles in some cells, not all. Say "can carry organelles".
9. **Handwritten, not wrong but ambiguous, p37**: the Arabic "the whole tube" next to 25 nm refers to the outer diameter; the printed "25 nm with 15-nm lumen" is clearer. Use the printed numbers.
10. **Handwritten, checked and correct**: p36 (microtubules are tracks; motor proteins move vesicles or organelles along them), p35 (motility = movement), p42 (cilia and flagella contain microtubules; they help movement; they differ in beating), p45 (9 + 2 arrangement; nine triplets in a ring for the basal body), p46 (dynein movement, limited sliding, bending). No handwritten claim is factually wrong in this set.
11. **Picture risk, p36 and p41**: the EM scale bar is 0.25 µm, and a 25 nm microtubule is one-tenth of it. Do not draw the vesicle or the centriole at a size that contradicts the 25 nm and 7 nm numbers; microtubules must look thicker than microfilaments and intermediate filaments (25 nm vs 7 nm and 8 to 12 nm).
12. **Picture risk, p40 and p41**: in an animal centrosome the two centrioles sit at right angles to each other (the p41 figure shows it). Draw one end on (ring of nine triplets) and one side on.
13. **Picture risk, p45**: the basal body has nine triplets and no central pair; the cilium has nine doublets and a central pair. Do not put the central pair in the basal-body drawing, and do not draw dynein arms on the central pair (dyneins are on the outer doublets only).
14. **Picture risk, p44**: the power stroke and recovery stroke arrows must match the slide (power stroke pushes fluid or the organism forward; recovery stroke returns the cilium). A flipped stroke or a wrong arrow direction would teach the wrong motion.
15. **Picture risk, p37 and p39**: the drawn microtubule is a hollow tube, with a visible lumen; the intermediate filament is a rope of coiled strands, not a hollow tube. Do not draw them alike.
16. **Picture risk, p36 and p37**: the slides do not show microtubule polarity (plus and minus ends); do not add it.

## Errata

1. Intermediate filaments (p34 gives no number): taught with 8 to 12 nm from p39.
2. Keratin (p39): the voice never says all intermediate filaments are keratin. The lamina and the other two fibres are a hook only (lesson 7).
3. p35 "Cytoskeletons may regulate": voiced "help regulate its chemistry". "25 microns" voiced as micrometers.
4. p44: no cilium length is stated; drawings carry no scale and no photo bar is moved onto them. Flagellum = undulating wave, cilium = power stroke plus recovery stroke, taught separately. "Flagella long, few; cilia short, many" is standard Campbell and is said as "usually".
5. p46: "walking" is not said of dynein; arms "grab, move, let go". The voice's "slip past each other" if the doublets were free is standard Campbell; the cross-links limit it, so it bends.
6. p45: two central microtubules (9 + 2), not the printed singular. Dynein arms on the outer doublets only; the basal body has nine triplets and no central pair.
7. Streaming, muscle and plus/minus ends are not taught or drawn. The two centrioles are drawn at a right angle (one end on, one side on), as p41 shows.
8. Nine triplets = 27 microtubules is arithmetic from p40 (derived, said as a sum). "Monorail" is a spoken analogy only.
9. No handwritten claim was wrong. All questions are new (no question slides in this chapter).

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-fibres`, pickEls: "Click the thickest fibre." Three fibres at relative sizes. Right: the mint hollow microtubule. Why: microtubules are about 25 nm, intermediate filaments 8 to 12 nm, microfilaments 7 nm. Wrong: gold rope (8 to 12 nm, middle); pink bead chain (7 nm, thinnest).
- `q2` id `c-dynein`, choice: "Dynein arms slide the doublets, yet the cilium bends. What turns the sliding into a bend?" Right: cross-links between the doublets limit the sliding. Why: doublets cannot slip freely, so sliding on one side bends the whole cilium. Wrong: the central pair pushes it sideways (it carries no dynein); the basal body pulls it (it only anchors it); the plasma membrane squeezes it (it only covers the core).
- `final1` ('Chapter practice'):
  - `p-order` choice: "Which lists the fibres from thickest to thinnest?" Right: microtubule, intermediate filament, microfilament. Wrong: microfilament first (reversed); intermediate filament first (swaps the top two); microtubule, microfilament, intermediate filament (swaps the last two).
  - `p-motor` choice: "How does a motor protein move a vesicle?" Right: it grips a receptor on the vesicle and walks along a microtubule, powered by ATP. Wrong: it pushes the vesicle through the cytosol with no track; it is itself a microtubule that slides; it runs on light, not ATP.
  - `p-count` blanks: "How many triplets are in one centriole?" 9. "How many microtubules in its ring?" 27. Hint: nine triplets of three tubes.
  - `p-where` grid, columns centriole / cilium / basal body (multi): nine triplets and no central pair (centriole, basal body); nine doublets plus two central microtubules (cilium); found in the centrosome (centriole); anchors a cilium to the cell (basal body); carries dynein arms on its outer doublets (cilium).
