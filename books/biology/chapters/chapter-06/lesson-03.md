## Concepts

1. **The endomembrane system has six components** (nuclear envelope, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vacuoles, plasma membrane); they are either continuous or linked by vesicles (p16). Hardness: stated (list); the vesicle link is named, not explained.
2. **The ER is more than half the total membrane in most eukaryotic cells** (p17). Hardness: stated.
3. **The ER is continuous with the nuclear envelope** (p17). Hardness: stated.
4. **Two regions of the ER: smooth ER has no ribosomes; rough ER has ribosomes on its surface** (p17). Hardness: defined; shown in the p18 picture.
5. **ER lumen and cisternae are the internal space of the ER** (p18 labels; the handwritten "internal space" note agrees). Hardness: definition (figure labels).
6. **Transitional ER gives off transport vesicles** (p18 figure: a red arrow from the transitional region to a bud-off vesicle). Hardness: explained by the figure only; no mechanism.
7. **Smooth ER functions: synthesizes lipids, metabolizes carbohydrates, detoxifies poison, stores calcium** (p19). Hardness: stated; four named functions, no mechanism.
8. **Rough ER: bound ribosomes make glycoproteins for secretion; it distributes transport vesicles and is the membrane factory of the cell** (p19). Hardness: stated.
9. **Glycoprotein: a protein covalently bonded to carbohydrates** (p19). Hardness: defined.
10. **The Golgi apparatus is a group of flattened membranous sacs called cisternae** (p20). Hardness: defined.
11. **Golgi functions: it modifies products of the ER, manufactures certain macromolecules, and sorts and packages materials into transport vesicles** (p20). Hardness: stated; no mechanism.
12. **Golgi polarity: the cis face is the receiving side, the trans face is the shipping side, and material moves cis to trans** (p21 cartoon with a red arrow). Hardness: explained by the figure.
13. **Golgi scale: the TEM shows the stack at 0.1 µm** (p21). Hardness: figure only.

## Vocabulary

- **Endomembrane system**: the nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles and plasma membrane, which are continuous or linked by vesicles.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)**: a membrane network, continuous with the nuclear envelope; the biosynthetic factory.
- **Smooth ER**: ER with no ribosomes; lipids, carbohydrate metabolism, detoxification, calcium storage.
- **Rough ER**: ER with ribosomes on its surface; makes glycoproteins for secretion and membranes.
- **Ribosome (bound)**: a ribosome attached to the rough ER surface.
- **Transitional ER**: the region of ER that gives off transport vesicles (p18 labels it; the slide does not say what it is).
- **ER lumen**: the internal space of the ER.
- **Cisternae**: flattened membranous sacs (in the ER figure, the stacked sheets; in the Golgi, the stacked sacs).
- **Transport vesicle**: a membrane bubble carrying material between components.
- **Glycoprotein**: a protein covalently bonded to carbohydrates.
- **Golgi apparatus**: the stack of cisternae that modifies, makes, sorts and ships materials.
- **cis face**: the receiving side of the Golgi.
- **trans face**: the shipping side of the Golgi.
- **Macromolecule**: a large molecule (slide 20 does not say which).

## Possible problems

**Printed text (loose or wrong):**
1. p16: "The components are either continuous or connected via transfer by vesicles" is loose. "Either/or" suggests every component is one or the other; the plasma membrane and vacuoles are not shown linked here, and the slide does not say how each component is linked. Decide in the narration as a loose statement.
2. p17: "Two distinct regions" is incomplete. The p18 figure shows a third part, the transitional ER, and the slide never mentions it.
3. p17: "Rough ER has ribosomes on its surface" is loose. Ribosomes are on the cytoplasmic (outer) face of the membrane, not on a generic surface; the slide does not say which face.
4. p19: "Distributes transport vesicles, proteins surrounded by membranes" reads as if the ER distributes proteins; the appositive refers to the vesicles, and the punctuation is off.
5. p19: "Has bound ribosomes to secrete glycoproteins" is narrow. Rough ER also makes membrane proteins and other products that are not secreted; the slide's "secrete" alone misleads.
6. p19: "Metabolizes carbohydrates" (smooth ER) is vague; the slide does not say which carbohydrates or how.
7. p20: "Manufactures certain macromolecules" is vague; the slide does not name them. Do not name them in the narration without a source.
8. p20: "Golgi apparatus-" hyphen and "vesicles" in a different font: trivial, no content problem.
9. p21: the cartoon does not say which face of the Golgi faces the ER or the plasma membrane. The standard textbook convention (cis face toward the ER, trans face toward the plasma membrane) is not on these slides; mark it as added, not printed.

**Handwritten notes (checked against the printed text and standard biology):**

## Errata

1. p16 "either continuous or connected via vesicles": taught as "continuous with each other, or linked when vesicles carry material"; nuclear envelope and ER shown continuous, ER to Golgi shown by vesicles. No per-component claim beyond that. Lysosomes, vacuoles and the plasma membrane are named only and wait for lesson 4.
2. p17 "two distinct regions": taught as two regions plus the transitional ER that p18 draws (a budding part of the same network, not a third kind).
3. p17 "ribosomes on its surface": taught as "on the outside of its membrane" (cytoplasmic face); the picture draws dots on the outside only. Added, not printed.
4. p19 "distributes transport vesicles, proteins surrounded by membranes": voiced as "hands its products on in transport vesicles, small bubbles of membrane with cargo inside". The ER does not distribute proteins itself.
5. p19 "secrete glycoproteins": voiced as "many are made for secretion"; rough ER also adds membrane (the slide's membrane factory). Not every product leaves the cell.
6. p19 "metabolizes carbohydrates": kept as stated, no detail added.
7. p20 "manufactures certain macromolecules": kept vague; none named.
8. p21 cis/trans: "cis face near the ER, trans face toward the cell surface" is the standard convention, added, not printed. The voice says cis is near the ER; the picture draws it that way.
9. Handwritten "make protein" beside rough ER is not used; taught as bound ribosomes build proteins that enter the lumen. "has no ribosomes", "has ribosomes", "make lipids", "internal space" are correct. Arabic notes unused.
10. Cartoon scale: ribosome dots are drawn large; tag the picture "not to scale". Give the Golgi no size.
11. Added examples (not on slides, standard Campbell content): a liver cell for detox, a pancreas cell secreting digestive enzymes as glycoproteins.
