## Concepts

1. **Concept 6.5 opener** (p27). Mitochondria and chloroplasts change energy from one form to another. Hardness: stated.
2. **Mitochondria are the sites of cellular respiration** (p27), a metabolic process that generates ATP. Hardness: definition.
3. **ATP is the product** (p27, handwritten gloss "Adenosine tri phosphate"). Hardness: name only.
4. **Chloroplasts are the sites of photosynthesis** (p27), in plants and algae. Hardness: definition.
5. **Shared features of mitochondria and chloroplasts** (p27): not part of the endomembrane system; double membrane; proteins made by free ribosomes; contain their own DNA. Hardness: stated as a list (see Possible problems 1).
6. **Mitochondria are in nearly all eukaryotic cells; they have two membranes** (p28). Hardness: stated.
7. **Cristae: the inner, folded membrane** (p28). They increase surface area for the enzymes that make ATP. Hardness: explained (the slide says why; the figure shows the folds).
8. **Two compartments in the mitochondrion** (p28, p29): the intermembrane space (between the two membranes) and the matrix. Hardness: stated; the figure shows where each is.
9. **The matrix is where some steps of cellular respiration happen** (p28). Hardness: stated (named steps are not given).
10. **Chloroplasts are plastids that contain pigment** (p30): found in leaves and green organs of plants and in algae. Hardness: definition.
11. **Chloroplast contents** (p30): chlorophyll, a green pigment; enzymes and molecules that work in photosynthesis. Hardness: stated.
12. **Thylakoids stacked into grana, in a stroma** (p30, p31): thylakoids are membranous sacs; stacks are grana; the stroma is the internal fluid. Hardness: explained (figure).
13. **Peroxisomes: single membrane, oxidative** (p32): they break down free radicals (printed; see Possible problems 6), produce hydrogen peroxide, convert it to water and release oxygen as a by-product. Hardness: explained (handwritten equation 2 H2O2 to 2 H2O + O2 with catalase).
14. **Relative sizes of the three organelles** (p33): a micrograph with a 1 µm bar. Hardness: picture only (see Possible problems 9).

## Vocabulary

- **Mitochondrion**: organelle of cellular respiration that makes ATP; two membranes.
- **Cellular respiration**: metabolic process that generates ATP.
- **ATP**: adenosine triphosphate, the energy carrier.
- **Cristae**: folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Intermembrane space**: the gap between the outer and inner membranes.
- **Matrix (mitochondrial)**: the inside of the inner membrane.
- **Chloroplast**: plastid that captures light energy for photosynthesis.
- **Plastid**: family of pigment-containing organelles (chloroplast is one kind).
- **Chlorophyll**: green pigment that captures light.
- **Photosynthesis**: light-driven process that the chloroplast performs.
- **Thylakoid**: membranous sac in the chloroplast.
- **Granum** (grana, plural): stack of thylakoids.
- **Stroma**: fluid inside the chloroplast, around the thylakoids.
- **Endomembrane system**: membranes that are continuous or linked by vesicles; mitochondria and chloroplasts are not part of it.
- **Free ribosome**: ribosome in the cytosol, not on the ER (printed label "free ribosomes in the mitochondrial matrix").
- **Peroxisome**: single-membrane organelle for oxidation; makes and breaks down hydrogen peroxide.
- **Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)**: product of oxidation, converted to water.
- **Catalase**: enzyme that converts H2O2 to water and oxygen (handwritten only).
- **Oxidation**: reaction in which oxygen is used to break down molecules (peroxisome title).
- **Free radical**: printed "free radicles" (typo, see Possible problems 6).

## Questions

There are no question slides in this chapter, so there are no circled answers to check here. Raw material for the practice set (what a student should be able to answer after the lesson):

1. Which organelle carries out cellular respiration and generates ATP? (mitochondrion)
2. Which organelle carries out photosynthesis in plants and algae? (chloroplast)
3. What are the two membranes of a mitochondrion, and what is the space between them called? (outer and inner membranes; intermembrane space)
4. How do cristae help the mitochondrion make ATP? (more surface area for the ATP-making enzymes)
5. What are the thylakoids and the granum, and where is the stroma? (membranous sacs; stacks of thylakoids; the fluid around them)
6. Name two features mitochondria and chloroplasts share that set them apart from the endomembrane system. (double membrane; own DNA; own ribosomes; not endomembrane)
7. What does a peroxisome make, and what happens to it? (hydrogen peroxide, converted to water, with oxygen released)

## Possible problems

1. p27 "Have proteins made by free ribosomes" and "Contain their own DNA": loose. Mitochondria and chloroplasts do have their own DNA and ribosomes, but most of their proteins are made by ribosomes in the cytosol. Teach "own DNA and their own ribosomes", not "their proteins are made by free ribosomes".
2. p27 "Chloroplasts - plastids: pigment containing organelles": loose. Plastids are a family and a chloroplast is one kind. Teach "chloroplasts are a type of plastid".
3. p28 handwritten "lysosomes -> pH = 5": wrong place. It is a lesson 4 fact (lysosomes are acidic), not about mitochondria. Misleading if read as a mitochondrial fact; do not use it here.
4. p28 "Cristae create two compartments": loose wording. The outer membrane and the folded inner membrane together make the intermembrane space and the matrix. Teach the two spaces, not a cause.
5. p29 "Free ribosomes in the mitochondrial matrix": the word "free" is a label, not a process. Keep to the figure: ribosomes sit in the matrix.
6. p32 "break down free radicles": typo for radicals, and loose. The peroxisome makes hydrogen peroxide and converts it to water and oxygen. Teach the equation, not "breaks down free radicals".
7. p32 "Oxygen is used to break down different types of molecules": loose. Oxygen is a reactant in oxidation of several substrates, and the slide names no substrates. Keep it to "oxygen is used in oxidation reactions".
8. p32 "convert it to water" (no oxygen named, then "liberate oxygen as a by product"): incomplete on the slide. The full equation is handwritten (2 H2O2 to 2 H2O + O2, with catalase). Use the equation and name catalase only as the enzyme in the handwritten note, flagged as not printed.
9. p33 "Relative sizes of organelles": the 1 µm bar and the printed labels do not give reliable relative sizes. The labelled peroxisome profile looks about 2 to 3 µm wide against the bar, which is larger than usual (about 0.5 to 1 µm); the mitochondrion looks about 1 µm. The chloroplast is cut off. Do not draw or state sizes from this micrograph without a caveat.
10. Diagrams F1 and F2 are not to scale (only the EM bars apply). Do not let a bar read as applying to the cartoon.
11. F3: the peroxisome has a crystalline core (not labelled, not discussed). Do not name it or teach it.
12. Ribosome drawing: the printed text says the mitochondrion and chloroplast have ribosomes; the figures show ribosomes as small dots. Draw them small and on the stroma or matrix side only, not on membranes.
13. Catalase and the equation 2 H2O2 to 2 H2O + O2 appear only in handwriting. Verify before use in narration; they are standard biology but not on the printed slides.

## Errata

1. p27 "proteins made by free ribosomes" / "own DNA": taught as "own DNA and own small ribosomes, but most of their proteins are made on free ribosomes in the cytosol and imported". Not "their proteins are made by free ribosomes".
2. p27 "plastids: pigment containing organelles": taught as "a chloroplast is one kind of plastid, a family of organelles that contain pigments".
3. p28 handwritten "lysosomes -> pH = 5": not used (lesson 4 fact).
4. p28 "Cristae create two compartments": not taught as a cause. Picture and voice name the intermembrane space (between the membranes) and the matrix (inside the inner membrane); cristae are taught as extra surface for the ATP-making enzymes.
5. p29 "free ribosomes in the matrix": drawn as small dots in the matrix (and stroma), never on a membrane; voiced as "small ribosomes of its own".
6. p32 "break down free radicles": dropped. Taught: oxidation uses oxygen; some reactions make hydrogen peroxide (harmful); catalase converts it: 2 H2O2 -> 2 H2O + O2. Catalase and the equation are handwritten only but standard biology: kept, as "an enzyme called catalase". Oxidation is "reactions that use oxygen to break down molecules" (no substrates named).
7. p33 relative sizes: NOT used. No size comparison among the three, nothing drawn to a common scale. Cartoons carry a "not to scale" tag. The 0.1 µm and 1 µm bars are not drawn.
8. Crystalline core of the peroxisome (p33): not drawn, not named.
9. Both cutaways are schematic: put a "not to scale" tag in the corner of the mitochondrion and chloroplast pictures.
10. Added beyond the slides (standard Campbell): photosynthesis builds sugar from CO2 and water and releases O2; respiration in a leaf cell uses that sugar and O2; a runner's muscle has many mitochondria. "Stacked like coins" is an analogy only.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-matrix`, pickEls: "Click the compartment inside the inner membrane." Clickable: outer membrane, intermembrane space, cristae, matrix. Right: matrix. Why: it is inside the inner membrane and is where some steps of cellular respiration happen. Wrong: intermembrane space (the gap between the two membranes); cristae (folds of the inner membrane, not a compartment); outer membrane (a membrane, not a compartment).
- `q2` id `c-sort`, grid, columns Mitochondrion / Chloroplast: "makes ATP by cellular respiration" (mito); "site of photosynthesis" (chloroplast); "has cristae" (mito); "has thylakoids stacked in grana" (chloroplast); "has a matrix" (mito); "has a stroma" (chloroplast). Why per row from the beats. (Double membrane and own DNA are shared, so no such row.)
- `final1` ('Chapter practice'):
  - `p-cristae` choice: "Why does the inner mitochondrial membrane fold into cristae?" Right: more surface for the enzymes that make ATP. Wrong: to hold the DNA (the DNA lies in the matrix); to catch light (mitochondria do not use light); to join the ER (mitochondria are not endomembrane).
  - `p-grana` pickEls on a chloroplast: "Click one granum." Clickable: a stack of discs (right), the stroma, the outer membrane, a single disc. Why: a granum is a stack of thylakoids. Wrong: the stroma is the fluid around them; the outer membrane is the envelope; a single disc is one thylakoid, not a stack.
  - `p-share` choice: "Which statement is true of both mitochondria and chloroplasts?" Right: they have a double membrane and their own DNA. Wrong: part of the endomembrane system (they are not); make all their own proteins (most are imported from the cytosol); a single membrane (that is the peroxisome).
  - `p-perox` choice: "What does a peroxisome do with the hydrogen peroxide its reactions make?" Right: converts it to water and releases oxygen. Wrong: turns it into ATP (that is respiration); stores it for the Golgi (not endomembrane flow); builds sugar from it (that is photosynthesis).
  - `p-terms` grid, true/false: a mitochondrion has two membranes (T); a chloroplast is a kind of plastid (T); the stroma is a stack of thylakoids (F, it is the fluid; the stack is a granum); a peroxisome has a double membrane (F, single); photosynthesis happens in the mitochondrion (F, chloroplast); ATP is the energy carrier made by cellular respiration (T).
