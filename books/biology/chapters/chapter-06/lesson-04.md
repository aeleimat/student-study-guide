## Concepts

1. **Lysosomes are membranous sacs of hydrolytic enzymes that digest macromolecules** (p22). Hardness: definition.
2. **Lysosomal enzymes hydrolyse proteins, fats, polysaccharides and nucleic acids** (p22). Hardness: stated (list).
3. **Phagocytosis: a cell engulfs a particle and forms a food vacuole; a lysosome fuses with the food vacuole and digests its contents** (p22, p23a). Hardness: explained by the figure.
4. **Autophagy: lysosomes use enzymes to recycle old organelles and macromolecules** (p22, p23b). Hardness: explained by the figure (a damaged mitochondrion and peroxisome are enclosed in a vesicle, then digested).
5. **A plant or fungal cell may have one or several vacuoles** (p24). Hardness: stated.
6. **Food vacuoles are formed by phagocytosis** (p24). Hardness: stated.
7. **Contractile vacuoles, in many freshwater protists, pump excess water out of the cell** (p24). Hardness: stated.
8. **Central vacuoles, in many mature plant cells, hold organic compounds and water** (p24, p25). Hardness: explained by the micrograph and drawing (the vacuole fills most of the cell; the cytosol is a thin layer around it).
9. **The endomembrane traffic route: rough ER makes proteins; transport vesicles carry them to the cis face of the Golgi; the Golgi modifies, sorts and ships them from the trans face** (p26). Hardness: explained by the figure (the words "endomembrane system" are not printed on the slides).
10. **Vesicles leaving the trans Golgi go to lysosomes or fuse with the plasma membrane to release their contents outside the cell** (p26). Hardness: explained by the figure; the purple sac is not labelled on the slide.

## Vocabulary

- **Lysosome**: membranous sac of hydrolytic enzymes that digest macromolecules.
- **Hydrolytic enzyme, macromolecule**: enzyme that breaks bonds using water; large molecule (protein, fat, polysaccharide, nucleic acid).
- **Phagocytosis**: a cell engulfs a particle and encloses it in a food vacuole.
- **Food vacuole**: vesicle formed by phagocytosis; the lysosome fuses with it.
- **Digestion**: breakdown of the contents by lysosomal enzymes.
- **Autophagy**: lysosomes recycle old organelles and macromolecules.
- **Vacuole**: membrane-bound compartment; kinds: food, contractile, central.
- **Contractile vacuole**: pumps excess water out of freshwater protists.
- **Central vacuole**: large vacuole of mature plant cells; holds water and organic compounds.
- **Cytosol**: the fluid region around the central vacuole (labelled on p25).
- **Rough ER, smooth ER**: endoplasmic reticulum with (rough) or without (smooth) ribosomes on its surface (names on p26 only; the ribosome difference is not printed).
- **Transport vesicle**: vesicle carrying products from the ER to the Golgi (handwritten on p26).
- **cis Golgi, trans Golgi**: the face that receives vesicles from the ER; the face that ships them out.
- **Plasma membrane**: the cell's outer membrane (fusion point of the last vesicle, p26).
- **Peroxisome, mitochondrion, vesicle**: organelles named in the autophagy panels (p23).
- **Pseudopodia**: false feet that engulf food (handwritten on p22 only).
- **Endomembrane system**: the lesson title term; not printed on the slides.

## Questions

There are no question slides in this chapter, so there is no printed question to test. Raw material for the practice set (what a student should be able to answer after the lesson):

1. What is a lysosome, and which kinds of molecules do its enzymes break down?
2. Name the four steps of phagocytosis in order (engulf, food vacuole forms, lysosome fuses, enzymes digest).
3. What does autophagy recycle, and what does a damaged organelle become inside the vesicle?
4. Which vacuole pumps excess water out of a freshwater protist, and which holds water and organic compounds in a mature plant cell?
5. Where does a food vacuole come from?
6. Trace the route of a protein made on the rough ER to the plasma membrane (ER, transport vesicle, cis Golgi, trans Golgi, vesicle, plasma membrane).
7. Which face of the Golgi receives vesicles from the ER, and which face ships them out?
8. What happens when a vesicle from the trans Golgi fuses with the plasma membrane?

## Possible problems

Printed slide text (loose or wrong):
1. p22 "Phagocytosis: when certain cells can engulf another cell": loose. Phagocytosis takes in particles or food (p23 says "particle"); "another cell" is one case only.
2. p24 "A plant cell or fungal cell may have one or several vacuoles": reads as if only plant and fungal cells have vacuoles; food and contractile vacuoles occur in other cells (phagocytic cells and protists). Teach it as "many cells, including plant cells".
3. p24 "Contractile vacuoles ... pump excess water out of cells": correct for freshwater protists; the slide's "many" is vague, and the lesson should say "some freshwater protists".
4. p25 micrograph labels: the cytosol is a thin layer and its label line is short; no size is printed for the cell or its parts.
5. p26 no scale bar on the whole traffic figure, and the diagram's sizes are not to scale; the picture must not imply any size.
6. p26 the purple sac at the trans Golgi is not labelled as a lysosome on the slide; the link is only the handwritten "substance goes out" and the textbook view. The lesson may say "a lysosome" only as the text presents it.
7. p26 the "endomembrane system" is not a printed phrase on these slides; it comes from the lesson title.

Handwritten notes (checked; wrong or misleading):
8. p26 handwritten 1: "Smooth ER: makes protein from ribosomes" is WRONG. Rough ER, which has ribosomes on its surface, makes proteins; smooth ER has no ribosomes (the printed figure shows no dots on the labelled smooth ER tubules). The note must not be used.
9. p26 handwritten 2 "transport vesicle" sits next to the smooth ER label, but the vesicles in the figure bud from the ER region near the rough ER. Students may read them as coming from smooth ER only. Do not tie vesicles to the smooth ER label.
10. p26 handwritten 4: "Golgi apparatus: modifies and packs and ships the protein" is loose: the Golgi also processes lipids; "protein" is only one of its products (the slide says "proteins and lipids" from the ER in note 3).
11. p26 handwritten 6: "vesicle fuses with plasma membrane, to sort contents outside the cell" is WRONG: the vesicle releases its contents outside the cell; sorting happens inside the Golgi, not outside it.
12. p26 handwritten 5 "substance goes out" is vague; it does not say whether this is a lysosome, a secreted product or a membrane part.
13. p22 handwritten sketch "Food vacuole → Lysosome" reads as the vacuole turning into a lysosome. The lysosome fuses with the food vacuole; the vacuole does not become a lysosome.
14. p22 handwritten "Break down waste" (main function 2) is loose: the printed text says autophagy recycles old organelles and macromolecules.
15. p23 (a) handwritten step numbers are inconsistent: circled 1 at engulfment is right; the food-vacuole stage carries a circled 3 (the list says step 2); there is no circled 2; a stray red "2" sits by "fuses" in the list. The lesson should use the list order (1 to 4), not the circled numbers.
16. p23 (a) red scribble beside the particle is illegible; ignore.
17. p22 Arabic note "enzymes that use water to digest large molecules" is correct (hydrolysis).

Picture risks:
18. p23 micrograph A: many dense bodies look alike; the "Lysosome" label points to one of several. The picture must light one body only and not call the others lysosomes.
19. p23 micrograph B: the labels "Vesicle containing two damaged organelles", "Mitochondrion fragment" and "Peroxisome fragment" are hard to tell apart in the photo; the picture should show one membrane round both organelles, and the label lines should not cross each other.
20. p23 diagrams: the lysosome (purple) must look the same in (a) and (b), and the enzymes (blue dots) should be on the lysosome edge, as printed.
21. p26 the rough and smooth ER are one continuous network in the figure; drawing them as two separate organelles would mislead. Show the smooth ER as tubules without dots and the rough ER with dots, joined.
22. p26 the Golgi cis face must face the ER and the trans face must face the plasma membrane and the purple sac; reversing them is the most likely picture error.
23. p25 the vacuole fills most of the cell in both the drawing and the micrograph; do not show it as a small sac.

## Errata

1. p22 "engulf another cell": taught as "engulfs a particle (a bacterium, food)", as p23 says.
2. p24 vacuoles in "plant or fungal" cells: taught as "many cells, plant and fungal cells among them"; food vacuoles are in phagocytic cells and protists.
3. p24 contractile vacuole: "many freshwater protists"; the picture is a pond protist.
4. p26 purple sac: drawn and called a lysosome, as the textbook figure presents it; the slide does not label it.
5. "Endomembrane system" is named in the voice (lesson title), with a one-line definition: the connected membranes (nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, vesicles, lysosomes, plasma membrane).
6. Handwritten p26 notes: #1 "smooth ER makes protein" NOT used (rough ER, with ribosomes, makes proteins; smooth ER has no dots). #2 vesicles are drawn budding from the ER region (rough), never tied to the smooth label. #4 Golgi "modifies, sorts and packs" products (proteins and lipids). #6 the vesicle RELEASES its contents outside (exocytosis); sorting happens in the Golgi. #5 "substance goes out" is replaced by "a vesicle to the plasma membrane releases its contents".
7. p22 sketch "food vacuole -> lysosome" and p23 circled numbers: the lysosome FUSES with the food vacuole; steps follow the list order 1 to 4. "Break down waste" becomes "recycle old organelles".
8. No sizes are given; the three scale bars are not used. Tag the traffic figure "not to scale". Rough and smooth ER are drawn joined (one network); cis faces the ER, trans faces the plasma membrane and lysosome.
9. Micrograph A: not used as a photo (the picture is drawn).
10. Added beyond the slides (standard Campbell): hydrolytic = uses water, loose enzymes would digest the cell, exocytosis named, nuclear envelope joined to the ER, a plant cell wilts when its central vacuole loses water.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-phago-order`, blanks (number steps 1 to 4): "engulf the particle" = 1; "a food vacuole forms" = 2; "a lysosome fuses with the vacuole" = 3; "enzymes digest the contents" = 4. Why: the lysosome fuses with a finished food vacuole, then digests. Wrong orders: digestion before fusion (no enzymes yet); fusion before the vacuole exists (nothing to fuse with).
- `q2` id `c-vacuoles`, grid, choose the vacuole (food / contractile / central): formed by phagocytosis (food); pumps excess water out of a pond protist (contractile); holds water and organic compounds in a mature plant cell (central); fills most of the cell and keeps a lettuce leaf crisp (central); a bubble holding a swallowed bacterium (food); a star-shaped collector of extra water (contractile).
- `final1` (Chapter practice):
  - `p-lyso` choice: "What is a lysosome?" Right: a membranous sac of hydrolytic enzymes that digest macromolecules. Wrong: a sac of ribosomes that builds proteins (ribosomes are not in a sac); a stack that ships proteins (the Golgi); a store of water in plants (central vacuole).
  - `p-kinds` choice: "Lysosomal enzymes can digest which molecules?" Right: proteins, fats, polysaccharides and nucleic acids. Wrong: only proteins (all four); only fats and sugars (proteins and nucleic acids too); none, they only store (they digest).
  - `p-auto` choice: "What is autophagy?" Right: lysosomes recycle old organelles and macromolecules. Wrong: a cell engulfing a particle (phagocytosis); a pond cell pumping out water (contractile vacuole); a vesicle fusing with the plasma membrane (exocytosis).
  - `p-route` blanks, number the route 1 to 5: "rough ER makes the protein" = 1; "a transport vesicle carries it" = 2; "the cis Golgi receives it" = 3; "the trans Golgi ships it out" = 4; "a vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane" = 5.
  - `p-fork` choice: "A vesicle from the trans Golgi fuses with the plasma membrane. What happens?" Right: it releases its contents outside the cell. Wrong: it sorts the contents outside (sorting happens in the Golgi); it makes proteins on the membrane (ribosomes on rough ER do); it digests the cell (lysosome enzymes stay in their sac).
