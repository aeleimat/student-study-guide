## Concepts

1. **Cells are the basic units of life** (p2). Organisms are made of cells; the cell is the simplest collection of matter that can live. Hardness: stated.
2. **Cell structure and cell function are related** (p2). Hardness: stated (no mechanism on the slide).
3. **All cells come from earlier cells** (p2). Hardness: stated.
4. **Microscopes show cell detail the unaided eye cannot see** (p3). Hardness: stated.
5. **How a light microscope works** (p3). Visible light passes through a specimen, then through glass lenses, which magnify the image. Hardness: explained (process, three steps).
6. **The light microscope is the simplest type** (p3). Hardness: stated.
7. **Cell fractionation separates organelles** (p4). Cells are broken open and their major organelles separated from each other with ultracentrifuges. Hardness: explained (process).
8. **Fractionation shows what each organelle does** (p4). Biochemistry and cytology correlate cell function with structure. Hardness: stated.
9. **All cells are prokaryotic or eukaryotic** (p5). Bacteria and archaea are prokaryotic; protists, fungi, animals and plants are eukaryotic. Hardness: stated (classification).
10. **Features shared by all cells** (p5). Plasma membrane; cytosol (semifluid substance); chromosomes, which carry genes; ribosomes, which make proteins. Hardness: stated.
11. **Prokaryotic cells** (p6). No nucleus; DNA in an unbound region called the nucleoid; no membrane-bound organelles; cytoplasm bounded by the plasma membrane. Hardness: stated.
12. **Eukaryotic cells** (p6). Much larger than prokaryotic cells; a membrane-bound nucleus holding the DNA; membrane-bound organelles; cytoplasm between the plasma membrane and the nucleus. Hardness: stated (size has no number).
13. **Parts of a typical rod-shaped bacterium** (p7). Fimbriae, nucleoid, ribosomes, plasma membrane, cell wall, capsule, flagella, bacterial chromosome. Hardness: explained by the figure (function given for each part in the handwriting only; see Possible problems).

## Vocabulary

- **Organism**: a living thing; all organisms are made of cells.
- **Cell**: the simplest collection of matter that can live.
- **Microscope, light microscope (LM)**: an instrument that magnifies; the LM passes visible light through a specimen and glass lenses.
- **Specimen**: the thing being viewed.
- **Unaided eye**: the eye without an instrument.
- **Cell fractionation**: breaking cells and separating their organelles.
- **Ultracentrifuge**: a very fast centrifuge used to separate organelles.
- **Organelle**: a membrane-bound (or structured) part of a cell with a function.
- **Biochemistry, cytology**: the chemistry of cells; the study of cell structure.
- **Prokaryotic cell**: a cell with no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles (bacteria and archaea).
- **Eukaryotic cell**: a cell with a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (protists, fungi, animals, plants).
- **Plasma membrane**: the membrane around every cell.
- **Cytosol**: the semifluid substance inside the cell.
- **Cytoplasm**: everything between the plasma membrane and the nucleus (in eukaryotes).
- **Nucleus, nucleoid**: the membrane-bound DNA-holding region of a eukaryote; the unbound DNA region of a prokaryote.
- **Chromosome**: the structure that carries genes.
- **Ribosome**: the site where proteins are made.
- **Fimbriae**: short hair-like structures on a bacterium.
- **Cell wall**: outer layer giving protection and support.
- **Capsule**: a sticky outer protective layer.
- **Flagella**: whip-like structures that move the cell.

## Possible problems

Printed text (flagged, not decided here):
1. p3 "Light microscope is the simplest." Unqualified. It is the simplest type of microscope listed, not the simplest instrument in general. Teach as "the simplest type" or do not stress it.
2. p2 "The cell is the simplest collection of matter that can live." Campbell wording; fine as a definition, but it is a definition, not a mechanism.
3. p5 "Archaebacteria." The domain is now called Archaea. The slide's term is older. Teach as "archaea" (and say the slide's name).
4. p5 "Chromosomes (carry genes)" listed as a feature of all cells. True as far as it goes (a prokaryote's DNA is its chromosome), but the slide does not say that a prokaryote's chromosome has no envelope.
5. p5 "Protists, fungi, animals, and plants" as eukaryotes is fine, but the slide gives no examples of protists.
6. p6 "much larger" with no numbers. Do not invent a size range.
7. p7 figure (a) and the labels "Nucleoid" and "Bacterial chromosome" point to the same blue tangle. The nucleoid is the region; the chromosome is the DNA inside it. The picture must show one region with two names, not two separate things.
8. p7 figure (a) is a single rod-shaped bacterium (the slide says "typical"). Bacteria also come in spheres and spirals; the picture must not suggest all bacteria are rods.
9. p7 (b) scale 0.5 µm is for this one species. Do not generalise a size from it.
10. p7 "Flagella" drawn as a bundle of whips. The slide does not say how they move the cell (rotation). Do not add a mechanism that the slide does not give.
11. Concept 6.2 heading: "internal membranes that compartmentalize their functions." The slides name the compartments but do not explain why separation helps.

Handwritten marks (checked against the printed text and standard biology):
H1. p4 "main function: separates organelles" (blue, next to the title). Misleading: separating organelles is the purpose of the lab method, not a function of the cell. Do not teach it as a cell function.
H2. p7 "Flagella = responsible of movement." Grammar ("responsible for"). Content is right; the slide gives no how.
H3. p7 "Fimbriae = help bacteria attach to surfaces." Correct (standard), but not printed on the slide; it is a student's addition, so use it only as taught, marked as a note.
H4. p7 "Capsule = a sticky outer protective layer," "Cell wall = provides protection and structural support," "Plasma membrane = controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell," "Ribosomes = protein synthesis," "Nucleoid = the region containing bacterial DNA." All correct as standard biology.
H5. p5 "The plasma membrane surrounds the cell and regulates the movement of substances into and out of it." Correct.
H6. Arabic notes on p2-p6 (organisms, cells, specimen, magnifies, unaided eye, cell fractionation, ultracentrifuge, prokaryotic/eukaryotic cells, semifluid, correlate). Translations match the printed terms; no wrong claims.

Nothing in the handwriting on slides 1-7 is factually wrong except H1 (misleading) and the grammar in H2.

Picture risks:
- Scale: the only scale is 0.5 µm on the TEM panel. Do not draw the schematic (a) to that scale, and do not draw a eukaryote next to the bacterium with an invented size.
- Ribosomes on p7 are small dots; keep them smaller than the nucleoid.
- The capsule must read as a pale outer ring, separate from the cell wall.
- Keep the TEM panel grey, as printed; do not recolour it into the chapter palette.

## Errata

1. p3 "Light microscope is the simplest": taught as "the simplest type of microscope". Electron microscopes are added in one sentence (the p7 TEM panel needs it); no mechanism beyond "beams of electrons".
2. p4 H1 "main function: separates organelles": not taught as a cell function. Fractionation is a lab method; it separates organelles so their jobs can be tested.
3. p5 "Archaebacteria": voice says the slide's name once, then "archaea".
4. p5 "chromosomes (carry genes)" in all cells: kept; the two-types beat says a prokaryote's DNA has no membrane around it.
5. p6 "much larger": no numbers. The size comparison is qualitative ("many prokaryotic cells could fit inside one"), drawn with a "not to scale" tag. Do not draw sizes with units.
6. p7 nucleoid and bacterial chromosome: drawn as ONE region (a dashed outline) holding ONE coiled chromosome, two labels.
7. p7 "typical" rod: voice says bacteria also come as spheres and spirals; the picture shows a rod with a sphere and a spiral small beside it.
8. p7 (b) 0.5 µm: voiced as "half a micrometer, for this one species". Keep the panel grey. Do not draw (a) to this scale.
9. p7 flagella: "whip like parts that move the cell", no mechanism. Fimbriae "help the cell attach to surfaces" (H3, standard, taught).
10. Concept 6.2 compartment benefit: not explained on slides; voice says "small compartments that each do one job" only.
11. H2 grammar not used. Handwritten H4/H5 definitions are correct and used.
12. Added from standard Campbell (not on the slides): E. coli and baker's yeast as the two worked cases; electron microscopes in one sentence.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-frac`, choice: "What does the ultracentrifuge do in cell fractionation?" Right: spins broken cells so the organelles separate from one another. Why: heavier parts settle first, so different organelles end up apart and can be tested. Wrong: it magnifies the cells (that is a microscope's job); it makes new cells (cells come only from earlier cells); it passes light through the specimen (that is a light microscope).
- `q2` id `c-compare`, grid, columns prokaryotic / eukaryotic / both (`multi: false`): has a plasma membrane (both); DNA in a nucleoid with no membrane (prokaryotic); membrane-bound organelles (eukaryotic); ribosomes (both); a nucleus wrapped in a membrane (eukaryotic); usually much larger (eukaryotic).
- `final1` ('Chapter practice'):
  - `p-micro` choice: "How does a light microscope magnify a specimen?" Right: visible light passes through the specimen and then through glass lenses. Wrong: it spins cells in a tube (that is fractionation); a beam of electrons passes through magnets (that is an electron microscope, a different type); light bounces off the eye (the eye is the end of the path, not a lens).
  - `p-groups` grid, prokaryotic / eukaryotic, `multi: false`: bacteria (prokaryotic); archaea (prokaryotic); fungi (eukaryotic); plants (eukaryotic); animals (eukaryotic); protists (eukaryotic).
  - `p-dna` choice: "Where is the DNA in a prokaryotic cell?" Right: in the nucleoid, a region with no membrane around it. Wrong: inside a membrane-bound nucleus (that is a eukaryote); inside the ribosomes (they make proteins); in the capsule (that is the outer protective layer).
  - `p-parts` grid, columns capsule / cell wall / fimbriae / flagella (`multi: false`): moves the cell (flagella); a sticky outer protective layer (capsule); helps the cell attach to surfaces (fimbriae); protects and supports the cell, just outside the plasma membrane (cell wall).
  - `p-shared` choice: "Which of these is NOT found in every cell?" Right: a membrane-bound nucleus. Wrong: a plasma membrane (all cells have one); ribosomes (all cells make proteins); chromosomes carrying genes (all cells have them, even if not in a nucleus).
