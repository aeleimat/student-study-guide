## Concepts

1. **Microfilaments are actin filaments, solid rods about 7 nm across** (p47). Hardness: stated (a number and a structure).
2. **A microfilament is a twisted double chain of actin subunits** (p47). Hardness: stated (definition only; the slide gives no detail of the subunits).
3. **Structural role: microfilaments bear tension and resist pulling forces inside the cell** (p47; handwritten "resist pulling forces"). Hardness: stated.
4. **The cortex: a 3-D network of microfilaments just inside the plasma membrane that helps support the cell's shape** (p47). Hardness: stated.
5. **Bundles of microfilaments form the core of microvilli in intestinal cells** (p47). Hardness: stated (one example).
6. **Motility: microfilaments that drive cell movement also contain myosin, besides actin** (p48). Hardness: stated.
7. **In muscle cells, thousands of actin filaments run parallel, and thick myosin filaments interdigitate with the thin actin filaments** (p48, with thin = actin and thick = myosin in the handwriting). Hardness: explained by figure 6.26(a).
8. **Myosin motors shorten a muscle cell: the myosin heads pull the actin filaments past the myosin filaments** (p49, figure 6.26(a), with red arrows pointing inward). Hardness: explained by the figure; the slide names "myosin motors" and "contraction" without describing the sliding.
9. **Amoeboid movement: localized contraction by actin and myosin drives it, and the cell crawls by extending pseudopodia** (p49 figure 6.26(b); p50). Hardness: explained by the figure.
10. **Pseudopodia are cell extensions used for movement; the cell moves toward them along a surface** (p50; handwritten "pseudopodia = cell extensions used for movement"). Hardness: stated.
11. **Cytoplasmic streaming is a circular flow of cytoplasm inside cells, which speeds the distribution of materials** (p51, and figure 6.26(c) with arrows on plant cells). Hardness: explained by the figure (the flow is shown; its speed-up role is stated).
12. **In plant cells, actin-myosin interactions and sol-gel transformations drive cytoplasmic streaming** (p51). Hardness: named only; "sol-gel transformation" is not defined on the slides.
13. **Intermediate filaments are 8-12 nm across: larger than microfilaments, smaller than microtubules** (p52). Hardness: stated (numbers and a comparison).
14. **Intermediate filaments support cell shape and fix organelles in place** (p52). Hardness: stated.
15. **Intermediate filaments are the most permanent cytoskeleton fixtures of the three classes** (p52; the red box marks "more permanent"). Hardness: stated (comparison, no mechanism).

## Vocabulary

- **Cytoskeleton**: the network of protein fibres in the cell (named on p52; the chapter's three classes are microfilaments, microtubules and intermediate filaments).
- **Microfilament, actin filament**: the thinnest cytoskeleton fibre (7 nm), built from actin subunits.
- **Actin subunit**: the protein unit a microfilament is built from.
- **Myosin**: a motor protein that works with actin to produce movement.
- **Myosin filament, myosin head**: the thick filament made of myosin, and the part of each myosin molecule that grips and pulls on actin.
- **Interdigitate**: thick and thin filaments overlap, interleaved, without joining.
- **Cortex**: the outer layer of cytoplasm just under the plasma membrane, a gel with an actin network.
- **Microvillus (plural microvilli)**: a small projection on an intestinal cell's surface, with a microfilament bundle as its core.
- **Motility**: the ability to move.
- **Amoeboid movement**: crawling by extending and retracting pseudopodia, driven by actin and myosin.
- **Pseudopodium (plural pseudopodia)**: a temporary cell extension used for movement and feeding.
- **Cytoplasmic streaming**: the circular flow of cytoplasm inside a cell.
- **Sol-gel transformation**: a change between a more fluid (sol) and a more solid, gel-like (gel) cytoplasm (named only, not defined on the slides).
- **Intermediate filament**: a cytoskeleton fibre of 8-12 nm, more permanent than the other two classes.
- **Organelle**: a membrane-bound or structured part of the cell (used on p52, "fix organelles in place").

## Questions

There are no question slides in this chapter. Raw material for the practice set (answers worked out from the printed text; no circled answers exist for this lesson). A student should be able to:

1. Give the diameter of a microfilament and say what it is built from (7 nm; actin subunits as a twisted double chain).
2. Name the main structural job of microfilaments (bearing tension, resisting pulling forces) and where the cortex is (just inside the plasma membrane).
3. Say which protein, besides actin, a motile microfilament system needs, and what the thick and thin filaments are in muscle (myosin thick, actin thin).
4. Explain how muscle contraction is shown in figure 6.26(a): the myosin heads pull actin filaments past myosin filaments (interdigitated, parallel).
5. Identify, from figure 6.26(b), the cortex (gel with actin network), the inner cytoplasm (more fluid) and a pseudopodium, and say what amoeboid movement uses (actin, myosin, pseudopodia).
6. Describe cytoplasmic streaming and its effect (a circular flow that speeds the distribution of materials), and name the two things the slide says drive it in plant cells.
7. Compare the three cytoskeleton classes by diameter: intermediate filaments (8-12 nm) are larger than microfilaments (7 nm) and smaller than microtubules.
8. State the job of intermediate filaments (support cell shape, fix organelles in place) and why they are called the most permanent.
9. Read a scale bar: 100 µm for the amoeba, 30 µm for the plant cells, 0.5 µm for the muscle micrograph.

## Possible problems

1. **p47 "twisted double chain of actin subunits"**: loose. A microfilament is a double helix (two strands of actin subunits twisted together); "chain" alone could be read as one line. Picture: draw two twisted strands, not one row.
2. **p48 handwritten "Actin + Myosin -> movement"**: loose. The arrow implies actin and myosin alone make movement. Movement needs ATP to power the myosin heads, which the slides never mention. Do not show actin and myosin "moving by themselves" without an energy source, and do not say ATP unless it is taught.
3. **p49 figure (a) scale**: the 0.5 µm bar sits on the micrograph at top, not on the schematic. The schematic is not to scale. A picture must not put a 0.5 µm bar under the schematic or give its filaments real widths.
4. **p49 figure (a) red arrows**: they point inward to the centre of the unit, which shows filaments sliding past each other (shortening). Picture must show the filaments sliding, not the filaments themselves getting shorter.
5. **p49 figure (a) red Arabic note at lower left**: the handwriting is too small and faint to read reliably in the image. Do not use it; it is not needed for the lesson.
6. **p49 figure (b) photograph**: the cell's species is not given, and the cortex and inner cytoplasm are a photographic gel-sol difference, not drawn filaments. Picture must not draw individual actin filaments in the amoeba photograph as if they were visible.
7. **p50 "Localized contraction ... also drives amoeboid movement"**: "also" suggests the same mechanism as muscle, but the figure shows no myosin motors in the amoeba and the slide does not say how the contraction is arranged. Teach it as "localized contraction by actin and myosin" and do not draw a sarcomere into the amoeba.
8. **p51 "Cytoplasmic streaming is a circular flow of cytoplasm within cells"**: loose. The figure (c) shows streaming in plant cells only; the slide's general statement is for cells in general. Teach as "in many cells, and clearly in plant cells"; do not claim all cells stream.
9. **p51 "sol-gel transformations drive cytoplasmic streaming"**: named but not defined; the mechanism is not explained on the slides. Do not invent one. Keep the term to "a change between a more fluid and a more gel-like cytoplasm" only if it is needed, and mark it as the slide's term.
10. **p52 "more permanent cytoskeleton fixtures"**: "permanent" means more stable and less quickly rebuilt, not fixed forever. Picture and voice must not show intermediate filaments as unchangeable.
11. **Handwritten p47 numbers 1-3**: correct as far as they go ("resist pulling forces", "support cell shape", "form the core of microvilli"). No problem.
12. **Handwritten p50 "uses pseudopodia", "allows cells to crawl along a surface"**: correct, not wrong.
13. **Handwritten p51 Arabic (distribution of materials inside the cell)**: correct; it matches the printed streaming line.
14. **Handwritten p48 Arabic "parallel" and "interdigitate"**: correct translations of the printed terms.
15. **Handwritten p48 "Myosin -> thick", "Actin -> thin"**: correct; they match the printed text and the figure.
16. **Handwritten p50 "driven by actin and myosin"**: true for amoeboid movement as the slide describes, with the same ATP caveat as problem 2.
17. **Slide 47 "solid rods about 7 nm"**: correct as printed. No problem.
18. **Slide 52 "8-12 nm", "larger than microfilaments but smaller than microtubules"**: correct as printed. No problem.
19. **Picture risk, figure 6.26 layout**: the "Muscle cell" label overlaps the "Figure 6.26" heading on the slide; no problem for the lesson, but do not copy that overlap.

## Errata

1. "Twisted double chain of actin subunits" (p47): taught and drawn as two strands of beads twisted into a helix.
2. p48 "Actin + Myosin -> movement": ATP is never named. The voice says "the heads need energy from the cell"; the picture may show a small spark at the heads, without the letters ATP.
3. Fig 6.26(a): the schematic has no scale bar. The 0.5 µm bar belongs only to the muscle micrograph (beat `scale`). Filaments are shown sliding past each other, never shortening; the voice says so.
4. Arabic note on p49: not used.
5. Amoeba (b): no filaments drawn inside the amoeba and no sarcomere; cortex = darker rim "gel with actin network", inner = lighter "more fluid". Taught as "localized contraction by actin and myosin", no mechanism invented.
6. Streaming: shown for a leaf (plant) cell; the voice says "inside a leaf cell" and "in plant cells". No claim that all cells stream.
7. Sol-gel: named once as the slide's term, glossed as "more fluid and more gel-like cytoplasm".
8. "Most permanent": taught as more stable, not unchangeable; the picture must not show them as frozen (the other two fibres flicker as they are rebuilt, the gold ropes stay).
9. Added, not on these slides: microtubule width 25 nm (from lesson 6; check against lesson 6's notes) and the roles line "rails / anchors / cables" in `compare`.
10. Added analogies: tent cables, interdigitated fingers, boats on a river.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-muscle`, choice: "What happens when a muscle cell contracts?" Right: myosin heads pull actin filaments past the myosin filaments, so the cell shortens. Why: the filaments slide and the overlap grows. Wrong: the actin and myosin filaments each get shorter (they keep their length, they slide); microtubules push the ends apart (not the job here); the cortex dissolves into the cytoplasm (unrelated).
- `q2` id `c-order`, order (or pickEls on three drawn fibres): "Put the three cytoskeleton fibres from thinnest to widest." Right: microfilament (7 nm), intermediate filament (8-12 nm), microtubule (25 nm). Why: "intermediate" means it sits between the other two. Wrong orders: microtubule first (it is the widest); intermediate first (it is thicker than microfilaments).
- `final1` ('Chapter practice', 6 questions):
  - `p-diam` choice: "What is a microfilament built from?" Right: actin subunits twisted into a double strand. Wrong: tubulin dimers in a hollow tube (a microtubule); rope-like protein fibres (intermediate filaments); myosin heads (the partner motor).
  - `p-jobs` grid, microfilament / intermediate / both: bears tension (microfilament); forms the cortex (microfilament); core of a microvillus (microfilament); fixes organelles in place (intermediate); supports cell shape (both); most permanent (intermediate).
  - `p-ameba` pickEls on the amoeba drawing: "Click the part that extends for movement." Right: the pseudopodium. Why: the cell moves toward it. Wrong: the cortex rim (gel with actin, not the extension); the inner cytoplasm (more fluid, it flows); the surface (not part of the cell).
  - `p-stream` choice: "What does cytoplasmic streaming do in a plant cell?" Right: circulates cytoplasm so materials are distributed faster. Wrong: thickens the cell wall (not its job); fixes organelles permanently (the intermediate-filament job); shortens the cell like a muscle (that is contraction).
  - `p-partner` choice: "Which protein works with actin in muscle contraction, amoeboid movement and streaming?" Right: myosin. Wrong: tubulin (builds microtubules); an intermediate filament protein (a different fibre); collagen (outside the cell, lesson 8).
  - `p-perm` choice: "Why are intermediate filaments called the most permanent of the three?" Right: they are more stable and rebuilt less often, so they hold shape and organelles in place. Wrong: they never change at all (stable, not unchangeable); they are the thickest (they are in the middle); they are made of actin (they are not).
