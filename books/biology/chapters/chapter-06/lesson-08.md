## Vocabulary

- **Extracellular**: outside the plasma membrane.
- **Cell wall**: a rigid layer outside the plasma membrane of plants, prokaryotes, fungi and some protists.
- **Cellulose**: the fibre that gives the plant wall its strength.
- **Primary cell wall, secondary cell wall**: the thin, flexible first wall; the thicker wall added inside it in some cells.
- **Middle lamella**: the layer that cements the primary walls of adjacent plant cells together.
- **Plasmodesmata**: channels through plant cell walls that connect the cytoplasm of adjacent cells.
- **Extracellular matrix (ECM)**: the network of glycoproteins outside animal cells.
- **Collagen**: a fibrous ECM glycoprotein that gives support.
- **Fibronectin**: an ECM glycoprotein that connects the ECM to integrins.
- **Proteoglycan**: a core protein with many polysaccharide chains attached; a proteoglycan complex is many of these together.
- **Polysaccharide, carbohydrate**: a chain of sugars.
- **Integrin**: a membrane receptor protein that binds the ECM and reaches the microfilaments in the cytoplasm.
- **Microfilaments**: actin filaments (named on p58).
- **Intercellular junctions**: direct connections between neighbouring cells.
- **Tight junction**: a seal where membranes of neighbouring cells are pressed together.
- **Desmosome (anchoring junction)**: a rivet-like junction that fastens cells together through intermediate filaments.
- **Gap junction (communicating junction)**: a cytoplasmic channel between adjacent cells.
- **Intermediate filaments**: the filaments that anchor desmosomes (labelled on p62).

## Possible problems

1. **Slide 59 mixes plant and animal junctions.** It lists plasmodesmata beside tight junctions, desmosomes and gap junctions without saying plasmodesmata are plant-only (slides 55 and 60 say so). Loose, to be decided in lesson's Errata.
2. **Slide 53 calls junctions "extracellular structures."** Gap junctions and plasmodesmata are cytoplasmic channels between cells, not extracellular material. Loose.
3. **Slide 54 "prevents excessive water uptake."** Loose: the wall resists swelling once water pressure builds; it does not stop water entering. Grammar also loose ("Cell wall are"; "Plant cell walls are of cellulose fibers embedded in").
4. **Slide 54 wall composition.** "Cellulose fibers embedded in polysaccharides and protein" is loose: the cellulose microfibrils sit in a matrix of other polysaccharides and protein.
5. **Slide 57 "glycoproteins like collagen, proteoglycans, and fibronectin."** Proteoglycans are mostly carbohydrate by mass, so calling them glycoproteins is loose; they are better called proteins with heavy sugar chains.
6. **Slide 57 "ECM proteins bind to integrins: receptor proteins in the plasma membrane."** The colon makes integrins sound like the ECM proteins; the sentence should say integrins are receptors that bind the ECM. Loose.
7. **Slide 55 and slide 60 define plasmodesmata differently**: "channels between adjacent plant cells" versus "channels in plant cell walls." Not contradictory; teach as channels through the walls that connect the cytoplasm of adjacent cells.
8. **Slide 58 inset labels.** The bottom label "Proteoglycan complex" sits under a bracket marked "Proteoglycan molecule," so a student could read the complex and the molecule as the same thing. Loose.
9. **Slide 56 TEM bracket.** The bracket for "Secondary cell wall" spans a band near the outer edge of the micrograph, and the bracket for the primary wall is close to it. Check the PNG at full size before the picture draws the layers; if the bracket marks the wrong band, say so in the lesson, not on screen.
10. **Picture risk, slide 62 (3D figure).** The beaded purple tight-junction strands at the top look like intermediate filaments. The picture must not draw tight junctions as filament bundles; filaments belong to the desmosome plaques.
11. **Picture risk, slide 58.** The figure's colours (collagen as blue-lavender striped rods, proteoglycans green) differ from the kit's chapter colours; the picture should use the kit colours and not copy the slide's colours.
12. **Picture risk, scale.** Each micrograph has its own scale bar; the three insets must not be drawn to a common scale. The 3D cell is schematic and needs a "not to scale" tag.
13. **Typography.** Scale labels "1  µm" and "0.1  µm" have double spaces; the text layer may show them oddly. Use "1 µm" and "0.1 µm".
14. **Handwritten, slide 57, blue note under fibronectin (left)**: reads roughly "a protein that helps support ECM components in the cells." Misleading: fibronectin is an ECM protein outside the cell, not a support protein inside it. Do not use.
15. **Handwritten, slide 54, red underline and note near "prevents excessive water uptake"**: partly illegible; it appears to restate the uptake point. Do not use.
16. **Handwritten, slide 56, numbered list (1 middle lamella, 2 primary, 3 secondary, 4 plasma membrane)**: the order is correct from the middle lamella inward to the membrane, but the list does not say what the numbers refer to on the picture. Do not copy the numbers onto the picture without a stated order.
17. **Handwritten notes checked and correct (not wrong)**: slide 53 (junctions between cells), slide 55 ("adjacent"), slide 57 (support and strength; proteins linked to sugar chains; receptor for integrins), slide 58 (ECM provides support; fibronectin connects ECM with integrins; proteoglycan complex contains protein with sugar chains; ECM binds the cell), slide 60 ("from a plant cell to an adjacent plant cell"), slide 62 ("prevent leakage"; "strength and support" for desmosomes; "communication between adjacent cells" for gap junctions). No change needed.

## Errata

1. Slide 59 mixes plant and animal junctions: the voice says plasmodesmata are the plant junction, and tight junctions, desmosomes and gap junctions are the animal ones. Gap junctions are named as the animal counterpart of plasmodesmata.
2. Slide 53 "extracellular structures": the voice says "three things to meet" (walls, matrix, junctions). Junctions are connections between cells, not material outside them, so they are not called "extracellular".
3. Slide 54 "prevents excessive water uptake": taught as "helps prevent excessive water uptake; the cell swells until the wall pushes back; the wall does not keep water out". Grammar fixed.
4. Slide 54 wall material: "cellulose fibers embedded in other polysaccharides and protein" (rods in concrete); no microfibril detail.
5. Slide 57 proteoglycans: collagen and fibronectin are called glycoproteins; the proteoglycan is a core protein with many sugar chains (bottle brush) and is not called a glycoprotein.
6. Slide 57 integrin colon: integrins are receptors in the membrane that bind the ECM and link to microfilaments.
7. Plasmodesmata: "a pore through the walls lined by the plasma membrane; the cytoplasm of neighbours is continuous".
8. Slide 58 inset: the picture draws one proteoglycan (core protein with sugar chains) and a complex as several together, labelled apart. The voice says only "core protein with many sugar chains".
9. Slide 56 bracket doubt: the picture draws the layers in textbook order (middle lamella between cells, primary on each side, secondary inside, then membrane), not copying the slide bracket. The student's numbers 1-4 are not drawn as numbers from the slide.
10. Picture risks: tight junctions are drawn as beaded fusion points between two membranes, never as filament bundles; filaments only at desmosome plaques; kit colours for the ECM; "not to scale" tag on the tissue row; the three junction close-ups are not on one scale.
11. Scale bars are not used (no scale question) and sizes are not spoken. If shown, write "1 µm".
12. Handwritten notes 14-16: not used.
