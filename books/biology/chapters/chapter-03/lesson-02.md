# Chapter 3, lesson 2: Cohesion and moderation of temperature

Status: user-approved (pilot)

## Concepts

In slide order. Hardness: definition only / explained / quantified.

1. **Four emergent properties of water** (p4). The slide lists four properties that make Earth fit for life: (a) cohesive behavior, (b) ability to moderate temperature, (c) expansion upon freezing, (d) versatility as a solvent. Only a and b belong to this chapter; c and d are lesson 3. Hardness: definition only (list).
2. **Cohesion** (p5). Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules together collectively; this is cohesion. Hardness: explained (the mechanism is named; the bonds are not described further on p5).
3. **Cohesion moves water against gravity in plants** (p5, p6). Hardness: explained (p6 figure shows the column of water held together in water-conducting cells).
4. **Adhesion to cell walls counters gravity** (p5, p6). Water sticks to plant cell walls and helps resist the downward pull. Adhesion is never given a formal definition on the slides; it is described by function and, on p6, by hydrogen bonds to the wall. Hardness: explained (by function and figure), not defined.
5. **Evaporation pulls water upward from the roots** (p6). Water lost at the leaves draws the column upward through water-conducting cells. Hardness: explained only as a figure label; the process is not named or detailed in text.
6. **Surface tension** (p7). Defined as how hard it is to break the surface of a liquid. Related to cohesion (stated, not explained). Hardness: definition only.
7. **Moderation of temperature** (p8). Water takes in heat from warmer air and gives stored heat back to cooler air. A large amount of heat can be absorbed or released with only a slight change in water's own temperature. Hardness: explained qualitatively; the "large amount / slight change" claim is not quantified on the slides.
8. **Kinetic energy and thermal energy** (p9). Kinetic energy is energy of motion. The kinetic energy of random motion of atoms or molecules is thermal energy. Hardness: definition only.
9. **Temperature** (p9). Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the molecules in a body of matter. Rising motion means rising temperature (the slide's handwritten margin note). Hardness: explained (definition plus average-KE link); no formula.
10. **Heat** (p9). Thermal energy in transfer from one body of matter to another is heat. Hardness: definition only.
11. **Calorie, kilocalorie, joule** (p10). A calorie is the heat that raises 1 g of water by 1 °C. Food "calories" are kilocalories (1 kcal = 1,000 cal). Joule is a unit of energy: 1 J = 0.239 cal, 1 cal = 4.184 J. Hardness: quantified.
12. **Specific heat** (p11). The heat that must be absorbed or lost by 1 g of a substance to change its temperature by 1 °C. Water's value is not stated on the slides, but p10 and p11 together imply 1 cal/(g·°C). Hardness: definition plus implied value; not stated as a number.
13. **High specific heat of water keeps temperature within limits that permit life** (p11). Water's high specific heat minimizes temperature fluctuations. Hardness: explained qualitatively.
14. **Hydrogen bonds absorb heat when they break and release heat when they form** (p11). This is the stated mechanism behind concept 13. The slides do not connect it to kinetic energy (why breaking bonds does not raise temperature). Hardness: explained (asserted), not quantified.
15. **Evaporation and heat of vaporization** (p12). Evaporation is the change from liquid to gas. Heat of vaporization is the heat a liquid must absorb for 1 g to become gas. No value is given. Hardness: definition only.
16. **Evaporative cooling** (p12). As a liquid evaporates, its remaining surface cools. Evaporative cooling of water helps stabilize temperature in organisms and bodies of water. The slides do not explain why the surface cools. Hardness: described, not explained.

## Vocabulary

- **Emergent property**: a property of water that comes from its molecular structure as a whole (here, the four properties on p4).
- **Fitness (for life)**: how well Earth's conditions suit living things; the slide uses it as a summary, not a measure.
- **Hydrogen bond**: the attraction between water molecules that gives cohesion and adhesion and that breaks and forms during heat exchange.
- **Cohesion**: water molecules sticking to each other, through many hydrogen bonds together.
- **Adhesion**: water sticking to other materials, here plant cell walls.
- **Water-conducting cells**: the plant cells (two types, shown on p6) that carry water upward.
- **Surface tension**: how hard it is to break the surface of a liquid; related to cohesion.
- **Moderation of temperature**: water evens out temperature changes in air and in living things and bodies of water.
- **Kinetic energy**: energy of motion.
- **Thermal energy**: kinetic energy of random motion of atoms or molecules.
- **Temperature**: the average kinetic energy of the molecules in a body of matter.
- **Heat**: thermal energy moving from one body of matter to another.
- **Calorie (cal)**: heat needed to raise 1 g of water by 1 °C.
- **Kilocalorie (kcal)**: 1,000 calories; the unit printed as "calories" on food packaging.
- **Joule (J)**: SI unit of energy; 1 cal = 4.184 J.
- **Specific heat**: heat needed to change 1 g of a substance by 1 °C.
- **Evaporation**: a substance changing from liquid to gas.
- **Heat of vaporization**: heat a liquid must absorb, per gram, to become gas.
- **Evaporative cooling**: cooling of the remaining liquid surface as molecules evaporate.
- **Stabilize (temperature)**: keep temperature from swinging widely.
- **Fluctuation**: a rise or fall in temperature over time.
- **Gravity**: the downward pull that water's cohesion and adhesion counter in plants.

## Questions

None in source. The slides have no end-of-chapter questions. Handwritten "question" lines on p7 and p12 are student notes, not source questions, and are listed here only for reference. Questions a student should answer after this section:

1. **Explain how cohesion and adhesion together move water up a tree, and say what evaporation at the leaves does.** Tests concepts 3, 4 and 5 and the p6 figure. Difficulty: medium.
2. **Distinguish temperature, thermal energy and heat for a cup of hot water.** Tests concept 8 to 10 definitions. Difficulty: easy to medium (wording trap: "measure of energy").
3. **Convert 1 kcal to joules, and 50 cal to joules.** Tests the p10 unit relations (1 kcal = 4,184 J; 50 cal = 209.2 J). Difficulty: easy (arithmetic).
4. **Explain why a lake stays cooler in summer and warmer in winter than the air around it, using specific heat and hydrogen bonds.** Tests concepts 7, 13 and 14. Difficulty: medium to hard (needs the mechanism applied to a new case).
5. **Explain why an evaporating liquid's surface cools, and why sweating helps stabilize body temperature.** Tests concept 16. The slides only state the effect; a full answer needs energy reasoning the source does not give. Difficulty: hard.
6. **Calculate the temperature rise when a given heat is added to a given mass of water, using c = 1 cal/(g·°C).** Tests concept 12 and the implied value. Difficulty: medium (units are the trap; see Possible problems).

## Possible problems

Flagged, not decided.

1. **p9 "Temperature is a measure of energy that represents the average kinetic energy..."** Temperature is a measure of average kinetic energy, not of energy itself. The first clause conflicts with the standard definition. Suggest the lesson words it as average kinetic energy per molecule.
2. **p9 "Thermal energy in transfer from one body of matter to another is defined as heat."** Compressed wording. Standard: heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. The slide omits the temperature difference.
3. **p8 "releases stored heat to cooler air."** "Stored heat" conflicts with p9, where heat is energy in transfer and not something stored. Standard phrasing: releases thermal energy.
4. **p10 calorie definition "raise the temperature of 1 g of water by 1 °C".** Strictly, the calorie is defined from a specific starting range (about 14.5 to 15.5 °C). Only matters for precise numbers.
5. **p10 and p11 specific heat not given as a number.** Specific heat of water (1 cal/g·°C) is implied but never stated; students may not connect it.
6. **p11 causal link is asserted, not explained.** "Heat is absorbed when hydrogen bonds break" is correct, but the slides do not say why this limits temperature rise (energy goes into breaking bonds rather than raising molecular kinetic energy). Easy for students to misread.
7. **p7 surface tension definition is an operational paraphrase.** "How hard it is to break the surface" is loose; standard definition is force per unit length (or energy per unit area) at the surface. "Related to cohesion" is stated with no mechanism.
8. **p7 image has no caption.** The link to surface tension is implied only.
9. **p6 uses "evaporation pulls water upward" with no name for the process.** Standard textbooks use transpiration and the cohesion-tension mechanism; the slide does not name either. Decide whether to introduce the terms.
10. **p6 scale.** Only the SEM has a scale (300 µm). The molecule inset and the tree are not to scale; students should not read sizes off them.
11. **p12 "As a liquid evaporates, its remaining surface cools."** No reason is given. Standard explanation: the most energetic molecules escape, so the average kinetic energy left behind drops. Without it, students may think evaporation removes "cold" rather than energy.
12. **p12 heat of vaporization has no value.** Definition only; a lesson that uses a number needs an external source.
13. **p10 handwritten example, unit mismatch.** The margin example "200 kcal into 20 (kg?) of water, ΔT = 200/(20 × 1) = 10 °C" is correct only if mass is in kg and c is in kcal/(kg·°C). If mass is 20 g (c in cal/g·°C), the answer is 200,000 / 20 = 10,000 °C. The handwriting is hard to read; confirm the unit before using it.
14. **p4 lists c (expansion upon freezing) and d (versatility as a solvent), which are lesson 3 content.** Mention only; do not teach here.
15. **p4 "fitness for life" is a metaphor.** Fine for a lecture slide; it is not a measurable quantity.
16. **Concept "adhesion" is never formally defined on the slides.** Defined only by function and by the p6 figure.

## Errata

Decisions on the flagged problems (numbers refer to "Possible problems").

1. Temperature is taught as the average kinetic energy of the molecules, never "a measure of energy". Thermal energy is the total of the random motion; the lesson shows a bathtub of warm water holding more thermal energy than a cup of boiling water, so temperature is not the total.
2. Heat is thermal energy in transfer from the warmer body to the cooler one (temperature difference stated).
3. "Stored heat" is dropped. Water takes in and gives out thermal energy; heat is only the transfer.
4. The calorie is taught as 1 g of water by 1 degC; the 14.5 to 15.5 range is left out (no effect at this level).
5. Specific heat of water is stated as a number: 1 cal/(g.degC) = 4.184 J/(g.degC). Dry sand is given as about 0.2 cal/(g.degC) (outside figure, rounded, used only for contrast, shown as "about").
6. Causal link made explicit: a large share of incoming heat goes into breaking H-bonds instead of speeding molecules up; forming bonds hands it back. Shown as a heat budget in the picture.
7. Surface tension: resistance of the surface to being broken, from cohesion, with the surface molecule pulled inward and sideways as the mechanism. No force-per-length definition.
8. The spider picture is replaced by a drawn water strider; the link to cohesion is spelled out.
9. The process at the leaves is named "evaporation pulls the column"; transpiration and cohesion-tension are not introduced. Adhesion is defined (water sticking to other material, here the cell wall).
10. No sizes are read off the pictures; the tree, tube and molecules are schematic (small "not to scale" tag).
11. Evaporative cooling is explained by the average: the fastest molecules leave, so the average kinetic energy left behind drops.
12. Heat of vaporization uses an outside value, about 580 cal/g near body temperature (about 2,430 J/g), shown as "about". The 100 degC value is not shown.
13. The handwritten example is not used. Units are fixed by the lesson: cal with g (c = 1 cal/(g.degC)); kcal only for conversions and food.
14. Items c and d of the four-property list (expansion on freezing, solvent) are not taught here (lesson 3).
15. "Fitness for life" is not used as a term.
16. Adhesion is defined in the lesson (see 9).

## Plan

Taught: cohesion from H-bonds (molecule level), water rising in a tree (evaporation pulls, cohesion keeps the chain, adhesion holds it to the wall), surface tension, thermal energy vs temperature vs heat, calorie/kilocalorie/joule with conversions, specific heat with a worked number, why H-bonds make water's specific heat high, coasts and cells, evaporative cooling and heat of vaporization.

Dropped: the four-property list slide as a topic, the SEM micrograph and its 300 micrometre scale, the names transpiration and cohesion-tension, the 14.5 degC definition, heat of vaporization at 100 degC.

Order: intro, part A (cohesion, tree, surface tension, check 1), part B (temperature and heat, units, specific heat, bond mechanism, climate, check 2, evaporative cooling), wrap, practice (4 questions in one set), finish. Reordering: surface tension comes after the tree, which shows why cohesion matters.

Pacing: about 870 words of narration, 15 beats, 6 questions (2 checks, 4 practice). Beats run 20-46 s of voice; each introduces one new picture idea at a time. Voiced audio is 310 s (5.2 min); questions and holds add about 1.7 minutes, so about 6.9 minutes in all.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-cohesion`, choice, "Imagine the hydrogen bonds between molecules in the trunk's water column vanished. What happens when the leaves lose water?" Options: (a) the column is pulled up as before; (b) the column breaks into separate bits and nothing is lifted (right); (c) the water gets hotter; (d) the water goes up faster. Why: with no bonds a leaving molecule cannot drag its neighbour. Wrong (a): it works only because molecules hold each other; (c), (d): not a heat or speed effect; the bonds transmit the pull.
- `q2` id `c-dT`, blanks, "900 cal of heat go into 300 g of water. The temperature rises by ___ degC." Answer 3 = 900 / (300 x 1). Hint: divide by mass times 1 cal/(g.degC). Compute in the page's code.
