## Concepts

1. **A catalyst speeds up a reaction without being consumed by it** (p35). Stated.
2. **An enzyme is a catalytic protein** (p35). Stated.
3. **Sucrase hydrolyses sucrose (C12H22O11) plus water into glucose and fructose (each C6H12O6)** (p35, p36). Stated, drawn: hexagon and pentagon joined by O; water splits into H and OH, one on each sugar.
4. **Every reaction between molecules breaks bonds and forms bonds** (p37). Stated.
5. **Activation energy (EA), the free energy of activation, is the initial energy needed to start a reaction** (p37). Stated.
6. **Activation energy is often supplied as thermal energy absorbed from the surroundings** (p37). Stated.
7. **Energy diagram: reactants, a hump (transition state), products lower; EA is the climb; delta G < 0 is the drop** (p38). Drawn: A-B and C-D bonded at the left, A-B and C-D joined by wavy weak bonds at the top, A-C and B-D at the right.
8. **Enzymes lower the EA barrier** (p39, p40). Stated and drawn (a second, lower hump in red).
9. **Enzymes do not change delta G; they hasten reactions that would occur eventually** (p39, p40). Stated; drawn (same start and end levels).
10. Added (not on the slides): why a cell cannot simply heat up (heat would damage proteins and speed every reaction at once); with a lower barrier more molecules cross at body temperature; a worked example with numbers.

## Vocabulary

- **Catalyst**: speeds up a reaction, not consumed.
- **Enzyme**: a catalytic protein.
- **Sucrase, sucrose, glucose, fructose**: the enzyme; table sugar (a disaccharide); its two monosaccharides.
- **Hydrolysis**: splitting a molecule by adding water.
- **Activation energy (EA)**: free energy of activation; the energy needed to start a reaction.
- **Transition state**: the unstable, high-energy arrangement at the top of the hump; old bonds strained, new ones not yet formed.
- **Reactants, products**; **free energy**; **progress of the reaction**.
- **Delta G**: the change in free energy between reactants and products (negative here, exergonic).

## Possible problems

- **p35 handwriting**: "sucrose + H2O" with a two-way arrow to "glucose + fructose". Loose: the slide's own arrow is one-way, and sucrase hydrolysis runs one way in the cell (strongly downhill). Enzymes do catalyse both directions of a reversible reaction, so the note is not false in general, but it is not this slide's point.
- **p38 printed "delta G < O"**: a letter O, meant as 0. Voice says "negative".
- **p38 handwriting**: Arabic labels (transition state, activation energy, free energy, reactants, products) all correct. Red boxes only.
- **p38 figure**: the products are drawn rearranged (A-C, B-D), a generic swap, not a real reaction. Taught as "bonds break and new bonds form".
- **p39 bar figure**: its red arrow runs down from the reactants level and is easy to read as "the enzyme lowers the energy", which is wrong; it shows delta G. The picture here does not reuse it.
- **p39 handwriting**: red box on "Enzymes", underlines on "do not" and "change". Correct emphasis.
- **p40 handwriting** "enzyme cannot change delta G of reaction": correct.
- **p37 "initial energy needed to start a reaction"**: fine; EA is a barrier on the way, not energy lost (for a reaction that releases energy overall, it is regained going downhill). The voice does not call it "lost".
- **p37 "free energy of activation"**: the usual name; "activation energy" used in the voice.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-ea`, `choice`, BAND: "What is activation energy?" Right: the energy needed to get a reaction started (the climb to the transition state). Wrong: the energy the reaction releases (that is the drop, delta G); the energy an enzyme contains (an enzyme is not an energy source); the energy of the products (not a barrier). Hint: look for the hill. Picture: the diagram with only the pink climb bracket lit.
- `q2` id `c-change`, `grid` (columns Changes / Stays the same, `multi: false`): "An enzyme joins the reaction. What changes?" Rows: the activation energy (changes, lower); delta G of the reaction (same); the products formed (same); how fast the reaction runs (changes, faster); the enzyme itself (same, not consumed). Why: the enzyme lowers the barrier and the speed follows; delta G depends only on start and end levels; the products are the same; the enzyme comes out unchanged. Wrong hints: "changes" for delta G: the start and end levels did not move; "same" for activation energy: that is the one thing an enzyme lowers. Picture: the two-curve diagram with the item lit per row.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice"):
  - `p-sucrase` `choice`: "Sucrase acts on sucrose and water. What does the reaction make?" Right: glucose and fructose. Wrong: glucose and water (water is a reactant, split into H and OH); sucrase and fructose (the enzyme is not turned into a product); starch (nothing is being joined). Picture: the sucrase row with the products hidden.
  - `p-heat` `choice`: "Why can't a cell simply heat up to cross the activation energy barrier?" Right: heat strong enough would damage its proteins and speed every reaction, not only the wanted one. Wrong: heat cannot supply activation energy (it can, and often does); a cell has no energy (it has plenty); activation energy does not matter in cells (it is the reason enzymes exist). Picture: the cell with heat arrows and a limp enzyme.
  - `p-curve` `grid` (Lower / Same / Higher, `multi: false`): "With an enzyme added, the ..." Rows: height of the hump (lower); free energy of the reactants (same); free energy of the products (same); delta G (same). Why: only the barrier moves. Wrong hint: "higher" or "lower" for delta G: both levels stayed. Picture: two curves, the row's feature lit.
  - `p-num` `blanks`: "Without an enzyme a reaction must climb 20 kcal/mol; with one, 8 kcal/mol. The enzyme lowers the barrier by ___ kcal/mol. The reaction's delta G is -5 kcal/mol without the enzyme, so with it, delta G is ___ kcal/mol." Answers 12 and -5. Hint: subtract for the barrier; delta G does not change. Picture: the example diagram with the brackets hidden.
  - `p-uphill` `choice`: "A reaction would only go uphill (positive delta G) without added energy. If you add the right enzyme, what happens?" Right: it stays uphill; an enzyme only hastens reactions that would occur eventually. Wrong: it runs downhill (an enzyme cannot change delta G); it runs uphill faster (it does not help an uphill reaction go); the enzyme supplies the energy (an enzyme is not an energy source). Picture: an uphill curve with an enzyme badge and a question mark.
