## Concepts

1. **A cell does three main kinds of work: chemical, transport, mechanical** (p25, p29). Stated.
2. **Energy coupling is using an exergonic process to drive an endergonic one; most of it in cells is mediated by ATP** (p25). Stated.
3. **ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the cell's energy shuttle: ribose, adenine, three phosphate groups** (p26, p27a). Stated and drawn.
4. **ATP hydrolysis gives ADP + inorganic phosphate (Pi) and releases energy** (p27b, p28). Drawn; the ink writes the equation ATP + H2O -> Pi + ADP.
5. **The energy comes from the change to a state of lower free energy, not from the phosphate bonds themselves** (p28). Stated; this corrects the loose bullet above it.
6. **ATP hydrolysis powers all three kinds of work; the coupled reaction is overall exergonic** (p29). Stated.
7. **Worked coupling: glutamic acid + NH3 -> glutamine is +3.4 kcal/mol; ATP hydrolysis is -7.3; net -3.9** (p30). Drawn in three panels (a, b, c). Two steps: ATP phosphorylates glutamic acid (ADP leaves), then NH3 displaces the phosphate (Pi leaves).
8. **Phosphorylation transfers a phosphate group to another molecule; the recipient is a phosphorylated intermediate** (p31).
9. **Transport work: ATP phosphorylates a transport protein, which changes shape and moves the solute. Mechanical work: ATP binds a motor protein noncovalently and is hydrolysed, and the protein and vesicle move along a cytoskeletal track** (p32).
10. **ATP is regenerated by adding phosphate to ADP, with energy from catabolism; the ATP cycle is a revolving door from catabolic to anabolic pathways** (p33, p34).

## Vocabulary

- **Energy coupling**: an exergonic process driving an endergonic one.
- **ATP / ADP / inorganic phosphate (Pi)**: three phosphates; two phosphates; the free phosphate.
- **Adenine, ribose**: nitrogenous base; five-carbon sugar. Adenine plus ribose is adenosine.
- **Hydrolysis**: breaking a bond by adding water.
- **Phosphorylation, phosphorylated intermediate**: transfer of a phosphate group; the molecule that received it.
- **Chemical, transport, mechanical work**.
- **Motor protein, vesicle, cytoskeletal track**.
- **Catabolic / anabolic pathways, ATP cycle**.
- **Free energy, delta G**: from lesson 3.

## Numbers

- delta G_Glu = +3.4 kcal/mol; delta G_ATP = -7.3 kcal/mol; net delta G = -3.9 kcal/mol (3.4 - 7.3 = -3.9, checked).
- ATP: 3 phosphates; ADP: 2 phosphates. Three kinds of work.
- Added from Campbell (not on slides): a working cell recycles ATP about 10 million times per second (voice says "millions of times every second").

## Possible problems

- **p32 (b) ink** "(1) ATP gives Pi for one reactant. (2) Phosphorylated reactant is more reactive. - **p32 (a) ink** "(2) Pi changes the protein's shape": loose. The phosphate group (P) covalently attached to the protein changes its shape; "Pi" is the free phosphate released afterwards. Slide 31's printed "phosphate group" is the better term.
- **p27 ink** "ATP + H2O -> Pi + ADP" is correct (energy is released; the slide's glow shows it).
- **p25 Arabic ink** ("movement" beside mechanical; "a reaction in the cell that releases energy and the energy is used to run other reactions"; "coupling") is correct. **p29** ink "needs energy" beside endergonic is correct. **p31** ink "needs energy" over "endergonic" is right, and "an intermediate compound with a phosphate group added" is right. **p33** "to ATP" and "regenerated" correct. **p34** ink "releases energy" at ATP + H2O is right; "is used to regenerate ATP from ADP + Pi" beside catabolism is right.

- **Slide wording**: p26 calls ATP "the cell's energy shuttle" (fine). p27(b) draws ATP's "P" circles; the slide's "energy" glow is just the released energy.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-source`, `choice`: "Where does the energy released by ATP hydrolysis come from?" Right: the change to products with lower free energy (ADP + Pi are more stable than ATP). Wrong: breaking the phosphate bonds, which are special high-energy bonds (breaking any bond takes energy; the energy comes from the lower free energy of the products); the water that is added (water is just a reactant and holds no stored energy to give); the adenine base (it is not changed). Hints: think of a ball rolling downhill; where is the energy, in the string or the height difference? Picture: the energy ladder, ATP high and ADP + Pi low.
- `q2` id `c-direction`, `grid` (exergonic / endergonic, `multi: false`): rows: glutamic acid + NH3 to glutamine on its own (endergonic); ATP hydrolysis (exergonic); the coupled glutamine reaction overall (exergonic); a motor protein walking a vesicle uphill, as the work being done (endergonic); catabolism that remakes ATP (exergonic). Why: exergonic releases energy (delta G below zero), endergonic needs it. Wrong hints: "exergonic" on the glutamine row: its delta G is +3.4; "endergonic" on the coupled row: net is -3.9. Picture: energy bars lit per row.
