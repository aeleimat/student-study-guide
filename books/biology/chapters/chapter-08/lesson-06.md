## Concepts

1. **The reactant an enzyme acts on is its substrate** (p41). Stated, labelled on p42a.
2. **The enzyme binds its substrate, forming an enzyme-substrate complex** (p41, p42b, p44). Stated and drawn.
3. **The active site is the region of the enzyme where the substrate binds** (p41, p42a). Stated; drawn as a notch in a space-filling enzyme (the substrate is a tiny red cluster beside it).
4. **Enzymes are specific: the pocket's shape and chemistry fit their substrate** (title p41 "Substrate Specificity"; p44 shapes). Hardness: the title states it, the bullets do not; Campbell supplies the reason.
5. **Induced fit: the substrate entering brings the active site's chemical groups into positions that enhance catalysis** (p41; p42a to b shows the notch closing on the substrate). Stated and drawn.
6. **The active site lowers the E_A barrier by orienting substrates correctly, straining substrate bonds, providing a favourable microenvironment, and covalently bonding to the substrate** (p43). Stated as a list, not drawn.
7. **The catalytic cycle, six steps** (p44): 1 substrates enter the active site; 2 held by weak interactions (the enzyme-substrate complex); 3 the active site lowers E_A and speeds the reaction; 4 substrates converted to products; 5 products released; 6 the active site is available for two new substrate molecules. Stated and drawn as a loop.
8. **Added to reach length (not on the slides, standard Campbell):** rate rises with substrate until the enzyme is saturated; the enzyme is unchanged by the cycle.

## Vocabulary

- **Substrate**: the reactant an enzyme acts on.
- **Active site**: the pocket or groove on the enzyme where the substrate binds (a few amino acids).
- **Enzyme-substrate complex**: the enzyme with its substrate bound.
- **Specificity**: an enzyme's active site fits one substrate (or a few close ones).
- **Induced fit**: the active site changes shape slightly as the substrate enters, closing round it.
- **Activation energy (E_A)**: the energy barrier a reaction must climb.
- **Microenvironment**: the local conditions inside the pocket (for example, acidic).
- **Covalent bond (to the substrate)**: a short-lived bond between a group in the active site and the substrate.
- **Weak interactions**: hydrogen bonds and ionic bonds that hold the substrate in place.
- **Saturation**: every enzyme's active site is occupied, so more substrate cannot raise the rate.
- **Product**: what the reaction makes; released at step 5.

## Questions

No question slides; this lesson writes its own. Ideas:

1. Name the three terms: substrate, active site, enzyme-substrate complex. (definitions)
2. Why is an enzyme specific? (the pocket fits one shape)
3. What does induced fit mean? (the active site adjusts its shape as the substrate binds)
4. List the four ways an active site lowers E_A. (orient, strain, microenvironment, covalent bond)
5. Order the catalytic cycle.
6. After the products leave, what is the enzyme like? (unchanged, ready again)
7. Does an enzyme change the energy change delta G of the reaction? (no, only E_A)
8. Why does the rate stop rising at high substrate? (saturation)

## Errata

1. p41 "cannot change": read as "the enzyme is not changed or used up by the reaction", which is true; the voice says this outright ("it is not used up", "springs back") and also teaches that the active site flexes, as the blue note says. No claim that the enzyme "cannot change" shape.
2. p43 covalent bonding: the voice says "for a moment, and then let go", so the bond is temporary (the Arabic note's wording is loosened).
3. p44 step 2: "weak interactions" named as hydrogen bonds and ionic bonds (Campbell), not on the slide.
4. p44: speak of "two substrates, two products" as the slide draws, without generalising.
5. Specificity: voice states the reason (shape and chemistry of the pocket), added from Campbell.
6. Saturation (not on slides): added from Campbell as a worked example, kept qualitative with no numbers.
7. The E_A / delta G link: the active site lowers E_A only; delta G is unchanged (lesson 5's point, repeated once).
8. Microenvironment example "more acidic than the cell around it" is Campbell's example of a low-pH pocket.

### Questions

Wrong: the substrate changes the enzyme into a different protein for good (the enzyme springs back, it is not permanently changed); the enzyme is made by the substrate (an enzyme is made by the cell, not by its substrate); the substrate is so small it fits any enzyme (enzymes are specific). Hint: think of a hand sliding into a glove. Picture: the enzyme with its notch closing on a substrate, labelled.

Wrong: it only meets that molecule in the cell (location is not why; a different shape cannot bind even if present); it is destroyed by other molecules (an enzyme is not destroyed by non-substrates, it simply does not bind them); the substrate makes the enzyme (the cell does). Which way of lowering the activation energy is this?" Right: straining the substrate's bonds. Wrong: orienting the substrates (it lines two substrates up, it does not stretch a bond); a favourable microenvironment (conditions in the pocket, e.g. more acidic); covalent bonding to the substrate (a brief bond, made and let go). Wrong: the enzyme is used up (it is not consumed); the substrate has changed shape (not what limits the rate); the activation energy has gone up (an enzyme does not raise it).
