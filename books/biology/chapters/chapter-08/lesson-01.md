## Concepts

1. **The cell is a miniature chemical factory where thousands of reactions occur; it extracts energy and applies it to work** (p2). Stated.
2. **Some organisms convert energy to light: bioluminescence** (p2). Stated; the firefly is our example (the slide names no organism).
3. **Metabolism is the totality of an organism's chemical reactions** (p3). Stated.
4. **Metabolism is an emergent property of life, arising from interactions between molecules within the cell** (p3). Stated.
5. **A metabolic pathway begins with a specific molecule and ends with a product; each step is catalysed by a specific enzyme** (p4, p5). Stated and drawn.
6. **Catabolic pathways release energy by breaking complex molecules into simpler compounds; cellular respiration (glucose with oxygen) is the example** (p6). Stated.
7. **Anabolic pathways consume energy to build complex molecules from simpler ones; protein synthesis from amino acids is the example** (p7). Stated.
8. **Bioenergetics is the study of how organisms manage their energy resources** (p7). Stated.
9. **Energy is the capacity to cause change; some forms can do work** (p8). Stated.
10. **Kinetic (motion), heat/thermal (kinetic energy of random movement of atoms or molecules), potential (location or structure), chemical (potential energy available for release in a reaction); energy converts between forms** (p9). Stated.
11. **The diver: more potential energy on the platform, diving converts potential to kinetic, climbing converts muscle kinetic energy to potential, less potential in the water** (p10). Drawn; our cast uses a ball on a ledge.

## Vocabulary

- **Metabolism**: all of an organism's chemical reactions.
- **Emergent property**: a property that arises from interactions of the parts, not from any one part.
- **Metabolic pathway**: a series of reactions from a starting molecule to a product, one enzyme per step.
- **Enzyme**: a catalyst (protein) for one step; taught properly in lesson 5.
- **Catabolic / anabolic pathway**: breaks down and releases energy / builds up and consumes energy.
- **Cellular respiration**: glucose broken down with oxygen; a catabolic pathway.
- **Bioenergetics**: the study of how organisms manage energy.
- **Energy, kinetic, thermal (heat), potential, chemical energy**: as on p8-9.
- **Bioluminescence**: energy converted to light by a living thing.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-cat-ana`, `grid` (catabolic / anabolic, `multi: false`): "Catabolic or anabolic?" Rows: glucose broken down with oxygen (catabolic); building a protein from amino acids (anabolic); releases energy as it breaks molecules down (catabolic); uses energy to build complex molecules (anabolic); breaks complex molecules into simpler compounds (catabolic). Why: catabolic breaks down and releases, anabolic builds and consumes. Hints: "anabolic" on a breakdown row: anabolic builds up; "catabolic" on a building row: catabolic breaks down. Picture: glucose and protein halves, lit per row.
- `q2` id `c-ball`, `choice`: "A ball rolls off a high ledge and falls. What is converted into what?" Right: potential energy into kinetic energy. Wrong: kinetic into potential (that is climbing back up); chemical into thermal (nothing is burning); the potential energy is lost (energy converts, it is not lost). Hint: higher means more potential, and falling means speeding up. Picture: the ledge scene with the ball mid-fall.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice"):
  - `p-meta` `choice`: "What is metabolism?" Right: the totality of an organism's chemical reactions. Wrong: only the reactions that break food down (that is catabolism, only half); the energy stored in a cell (metabolism is reactions, not stored energy); one enzyme-catalysed reaction (it is all of them). Picture: the cell with all reaction arrows lit.
  - `p-path` `choice`: "In a pathway from A to D, what catalyses each step?" Right: a specific enzyme for each step. Wrong: one enzyme for the whole pathway (each step has its own); heat from the cell (enzymes catalyse); the product D (the product is the end of the pathway). Picture: the strip with "?" over each arrow.
  - `p-steps` `blanks`: "A pathway runs A to B to C to D." Rows: "Reactions in the pathway" = 3; "Enzymes needed" = 3; "Molecules from start to product, counting both" = 4. Hint: count the arrows for reactions and enzymes, the squares for molecules. Picture: the strip.
  - `p-forms` `grid` (kinetic / potential, `multi: false`): "Kinetic or potential?" Rows: a ball rolling along the ground (kinetic); a stone held on a high shelf (potential); the random movement of molecules, that is heat (kinetic); a ball at the top of a ledge (potential); chemical energy in glucose (potential). Why: kinetic is motion, heat included; potential is location or structure, chemical energy included. Hint on the heat row: heat is kinetic energy of random movement. Picture: the four energy tiles lit per row.
  - `p-emerge` `choice`: "Why is metabolism called an emergent property?" Right: it arises from interactions between molecules within the cell. Wrong: it is found in a single molecule (no single molecule has it); it appears only in large animals (every living cell has it); it is created from nothing (it comes from molecules interacting, nothing appears from nothing). Picture: the cell with its molecules dimmed and the whole glowing.
  - `p-anab` `choice`: "Making a protein from amino acids is which kind of pathway?" Right: anabolic, it uses energy to build a complex molecule. Wrong: catabolic, it releases energy by breaking down (that is the opposite); catabolic, because proteins are big (it builds one, so it is anabolic); neither, it is not a pathway (it has many steps, each with its own enzyme). Picture: the amino acids joining into a chain.
