## Concepts

1. **Thermodynamics is the study of energy transformations** (p11).
2. **Isolated system: isolated from its surroundings; open system: energy and matter can be transferred between system and surroundings; organisms are open systems** (p11).
3. **First law: the energy of the universe is constant; energy can be transferred and transformed but not created or destroyed** (p12, p13a).
4. **Second law: during every transfer or transformation some energy is unusable, often lost as heat; every transfer or transformation increases the entropy (disorder) of the universe** (p13b, p14).
5. **Spontaneous processes occur without energy input, quickly or slowly; to occur without energy input a process must increase the entropy of the universe** (p15).
6. **Biological order and disorder: cells create order from less ordered materials, organisms replace ordered matter and energy with less ordered forms, energy enters an ecosystem as light and exits as heat** (p16).

## Vocabulary

- **Thermodynamics**: the study of energy transformations.
- **System, surroundings**: the piece of the universe studied; everything else.
- **Isolated system**: no exchange of energy or matter with its surroundings.
- **Open system**: exchanges energy and matter; every organism.
- **First law (conservation of energy)**, **second law**.
- **Unusable energy**: energy that can no longer do work in the cell, often heat.
- **Entropy**: a measure of disorder or randomness.
- **Spontaneous process**: occurs without energy input (not "fast").
- **Heat**, **chemical energy** (from lesson 1).

## Possible problems

- **p11 printed text**: "An isolated system, is isolated from its surroundings" is circular and has a stray comma. Campbell: isolated means no energy or matter exchange. Other slides in the chapter mix "closed" and "isolated" (lesson 3 deals with that); this lesson says only "isolated" and "open".
- **p13 handwriting, Arabic left note**: "energy changed but did not disappear". Correct (first law). The right note "Some becomes heat and Entropy increases." is correct but compressed: the heat is the part that raises entropy.
- **p14 handwriting**: "Some energy transfer as heat and can't do work any more." Meaning is right; wording "transfer" should be "is transferred" or "becomes". Loose: heat can do work between hot and cold regions, but a cell, at a uniform temperature, cannot use it.
- **p14 printed text**: "unusable" means unavailable to do work, not destroyed (the first law still holds). Needs saying so the two laws do not seem to clash.
- **p15 printed and boxed "without energy input"**: correct as Campbell's definition. Risk: students equate spontaneous with fast. The slide says "quickly or slowly"; the voice says so.
- **p15 second bullet**: "must increase the entropy of the universe" is correct for the universe. The system's own entropy may fall (a cell). Taught on p16.
- **p16**: "Organisms also replace ordered forms of matter and energy with less ordered forms" is correct in Campbell. "Energy flows into an ecosystem as light, exits as heat" is correct for ecosystems, not a single cell.
- **p12 red underlines and "conserved"**: fine.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-first-law`, `choice`: "Which statement matches the first law of thermodynamics?" Right: energy can change form, but the total energy of the universe stays constant. Wrong: energy is destroyed when a cell uses it (the first law forbids destroying energy; it changes form); every transformation creates a little new energy (energy is never created); energy cannot change form (it can be transformed, for example chemical to heat). Hints: "Where does the fruit's energy go?"; "Think of a bank balance that never changes, only moves between accounts." Picture: fruit and bars with the fixed total gauge.
- `q2` id `c-spont`, `choice`: "What is true of every spontaneous process?" Right: it occurs without energy input and increases the entropy of the universe. Wrong: it always happens quickly (spontaneous can be slow, like rusting); it decreases the entropy of the universe (the second law forbids this); it needs a steady supply of energy (that is the opposite of spontaneous). Hints: "Rusting is spontaneous. Is it fast?"; "Look at the entropy of the whole universe, not just the system." Picture: the three scenes lit with the rule banner.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice"):
  - `p-systems` `grid` (isolated / open, `multi: false`): "a perfectly sealed, insulated flask" (isolated); "a living cell" (open); "exchanges both energy and matter with its surroundings" (open); "no energy or matter crosses its boundary" (isolated); "every organism" (open). Why: an isolated system exchanges nothing; organisms take in matter and give off heat. Wrong hints: "isolated" on a cell row: a cell takes in food and gives off heat. Picture: flask and cell with arrows, lit per row.
  - `p-heat` `choice`: "A cell uses chemical energy to do work, and some of it leaves as heat. Which law says some energy always becomes unusable this way?" Right: the second law. Wrong: the first law (it only says the total is constant); neither law (both apply); a law about spontaneous speed (there is none). Hints: "Which law mentions entropy?" Picture: the split bar with unusable heat.
  - `p-spont` `grid` (spontaneous / needs energy input, `multi: false`): "a sugar cube dissolving in tea" (spontaneous); "scattered dots lining up in rows" (needs energy input); "an iron nail slowly rusting" (spontaneous); "water running downhill" (spontaneous); "a cell building a protein chain" (needs energy input). Why: spontaneous processes raise the universe's entropy without outside energy; building order needs energy. Wrong hints: "needs input" on rusting: slow does not mean unspontaneous. Picture: scenes lit per row.
  - `p-cell` `choice`: "A cell builds ordered proteins from loose amino acids. Why does this not break the second law?" Right: the cell releases heat and waste that increase disorder around it by more than the order it builds, so the universe's entropy rises. Wrong: because cells are isolated systems (cells are open); because order is created from nothing (no, it uses energy); because the second law only applies to non-living things (it applies to all). Picture: cell with halo bars.
  - `p-blanks` `blanks`: "Energy can be ___ and transformed but never created or destroyed" = "transferred"; "Every transfer increases the ___ of the universe" = "entropy"; "Energy flows into an ecosystem as light and leaves as ___" = "heat". Hints: "A word for disorder."; "The wavy orange arrow." Picture: the ecosystem strip with light in and heat out, blanks over the labels.
  - `p-unusable` `choice` (added by the picture worker for length): "The heat a cell gives off is called unusable energy. What does that mean?" Right: it still exists but the cell can no longer use it to do work. Wrong: destroyed (first law); never energy (heat is energy); turns back into chemical energy by itself (would lower entropy). Picture: a cell, a heat arrow and scattered dots.
