## Concepts

1. Without tight regulation of metabolic pathways, chemical chaos would result (p52). Stated.
2. A cell regulates by switching genes for specific enzymes on or off, or by regulating the activity of enzymes (p52). Stated; the lesson teaches the second.
3. Allosteric regulation: a regulatory molecule binds at one site and affects the protein's function at another site; it may inhibit or stimulate (p53). Stated.
4. Most allosteric enzymes are made of polypeptide subunits; each enzyme has active and inactive forms (p54, p55a). Stated, drawn (four subunits, oscillation).
5. An activator stabilizes the active form; an inhibitor stabilizes the inactive form (p54, p55a). Stated, drawn.
6. Cooperativity: one substrate binding at one active site primes the others, amplifying activity; allosteric because binding at one active site affects catalysis at a different active site (p55b, p56). Stated, drawn.
7. Feedback inhibition: the end product shuts down its own pathway, preventing waste (p57). Stated.
8. Threonine to isoleucine: isoleucine binds the allosteric site of enzyme 1 (threonine deaminase), its active site can no longer convert threonine to intermediate A, the pathway switches off; when isoleucine is used up the active site is available again (p58). Drawn.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-sites`, `grid` (active site / regulatory site, `multi: false`): "Active site or regulatory site?" Rows: where the substrate binds (active); where an activator or inhibitor binds (regulatory); the site a competitive inhibitor would block (active); the site that takes an allosteric regulator, away from the substrate's (regulatory); the site that takes isoleucine on enzyme 1 (regulatory). Why: the substrate fits the active site; allosteric regulators bind a different site and change the shape. Wrong hints: "active" on a regulator row: allosteric means a different site from the active site; "regulatory" on a substrate row: the substrate goes to the active site. Picture: four-subunit enzyme, sites lit per row.
- `q2` id `c-iso`, `choice`: "What does isoleucine do to enzyme 1?" Right: binds its allosteric site, so the active site can no longer convert threonine, and the pathway switches off. Wrong: binds the active site in place of threonine (it binds elsewhere, allosterically); is converted back to threonine (it is the end product); turns on the gene for enzyme 1 (this is regulation of activity, not of genes). Hints: where does it bind; what does it do to the active site. Picture: enzyme 1 with isoleucine at its allosteric site, notch distorted.
