## Concepts

1. **In the presence of O₂, pyruvate enters the mitochondrion through a transport protein** (p26, p27). Stated (p26 bullet); drawn (p27, the transport protein in the membrane, pyruvate arrow from the cytosol).
2. **Before the citric acid cycle can begin, pyruvate is converted to acetyl CoA, which links the cycle to glycolysis** (p26). Stated.
3. **Pyruvate oxidation: the carboxyl carbon of pyruvate leaves as CO₂, the remaining two-carbon fragment is oxidized (NAD⁺ → NADH + H⁺), and coenzyme A is attached, giving acetyl CoA** (p27, steps 1-3). Drawn, numbered 1, 2, 3 on the figure. The step order is 1 CO₂ release, 2 NADH, 3 CoA attachment.
4. **Pyruvate dehydrogenase is the enzyme of pyruvate oxidation; its picture is a large multi-subunit complex, about 10 nm across** (p27). Drawn (a photo, with the 10 nm scale bar); the purple box in the diagram is the same enzyme.
5. **The citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) takes place within the mitochondrial matrix** (p28). Stated.
6. **The cycle oxidizes organic fuel derived from pyruvate; per turn it generates 1 ATP, 3 NADH and 1 FADH₂** (p28). Stated (the numbers are in blue on the slide).
7. **Per pyruvate, the cycle is entered as acetyl CoA; the acetyl CoA is two carbons, and the cycle releases two CO₂ per turn** (p29). Drawn (acetyl CoA as two C beads, two CO₂ leaving the cycle).
8. **Pyruvate oxidation and the cycle are shown as the two stages in the mitochondrion, with pyruvate from glycolysis (2 molecules per glucose)** (p29). Stated (the label "2 molecules per glucose") and drawn.
9. **The citric acid cycle has eight steps, each catalysed by a specific enzyme** (p30, p31). Stated; numbered 1-8 on the figure.
10. **Step 1: acetyl CoA's two-carbon acetyl group joins oxaloacetate (4 C) to form citrate (6 C)** (p30, p31 step 1). Stated and drawn.
11. **Steps 2-8 decompose citrate back to oxaloacetate, so the cycle is a loop** (p30, p31). Stated (p30 bullet); drawn as the ring of molecules.
12. **Step 2: citrate is converted to its isomer isocitrate by removing one water molecule and adding another** (p31). Stated and drawn.
13. **Step 3: isocitrate is oxidized (NAD⁺ → NADH), then loses a CO₂** (p31). Stated and drawn.
14. **Step 4: another CO₂ is lost, the compound is oxidized (NAD⁺ → NADH), and the remaining molecule is attached to coenzyme A by an unstable bond, giving succinyl CoA** (p31). Stated and drawn.
15. **Step 5: CoA is displaced by a phosphate group, which is transferred to GDP to form GTP; GTP can be used to make ATP** (p31). Stated and drawn. This is substrate-level phosphorylation.
16. **Step 6: two hydrogens are transferred to FAD, forming FADH₂ and oxidizing succinate** (p31). Stated and drawn.
17. **Step 7: adding a water molecule rearranges bonds in the substrate** (fumarate to malate) (p31). Stated and drawn.
18. **Step 8: the substrate is oxidized, reducing NAD⁺ to NADH and regenerating oxaloacetate** (p31). Stated and drawn.
19. **The NADH and FADH₂ made by the cycle relay electrons extracted from food to the electron transport chain** (p30). Stated.
20. **Carbon bookkeeping: each pyruvate (3 C) gives one CO₂ at pyruvate oxidation and an acetyl group (2 C) that gives two CO₂ in the cycle, so all three carbons leave as CO₂** (derived from p27 and p31; the slides show the CO₂ steps, not the sum). Derived.
21. **Per glucose (two pyruvate), the stage gives 2 NADH from pyruvate oxidation, 6 NADH and 2 FADH₂ and 2 ATP from the two turns of the cycle** (derived from p28, p29: "2 molecules per glucose", "per turn"). Derived; the slides do not add the totals. Lesson 7 uses these for the ATP count.

## Vocabulary

- **Pyruvate**: the three-carbon end product of glycolysis (from lesson 4), entering the mitochondrion.
- **Transport protein**: the membrane protein that carries pyruvate across the inner membrane into the matrix (p27).
- **Mitochondrial matrix**: the inner compartment where the citric acid cycle runs (p28).
- **Pyruvate oxidation**: pyruvate to acetyl CoA, with CO₂ and NADH made (p27).
- **Pyruvate dehydrogenase**: the enzyme complex of pyruvate oxidation (p27).
- **Coenzyme A (CoA, CoA-SH)**: a carrier molecule that holds the acetyl group as acetyl CoA (p27, p29, p31).
- **Acetyl CoA**: acetyl group (two carbons) attached to coenzyme A; the fuel entering the cycle (p27, p29).
- **Citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle)**: the cycle of eight enzyme-catalysed steps that completes oxidation of the fuel (p28, p30, p31).
- **Oxaloacetate**: four-carbon molecule that accepts the acetyl group and is regenerated at the end of each turn (p31).
- **Citrate**: six-carbon product of step 1 (p31).
- **Isomer**: a molecule with the same atoms in a different arrangement (isocitrate from citrate, p31 step 2).
- **α-ketoglutarate**: five-carbon intermediate after isocitrate loses CO₂ (p31 step 3). Printed as "c-Ketoglutarate" in the text layer; use α.
- **Succinyl CoA, succinate, fumarate, malate**: the intermediates of steps 4-8 (p31).
- **GTP / GDP**: guanosine triphosphate and diphosphate; GTP is the step-5 product and can hand its phosphate to ADP (p31).
- **FAD / FADH₂**: the oxidized and reduced electron carriers of step 6 (p31).
- **Substrate-level phosphorylation**: a phosphate moved straight from a substrate to ADP (or GDP) by an enzyme (p31 step 5).
- **Decarboxylation**: loss of CO₂ from a carboxyl group (p27 step 1; p31 steps 3 and 4). Not printed as a word; the slides say "loses a CO₂".

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter; these are written from the material. Each is for the quick checks or practice set.

1. Where does pyruvate go before the citric acid cycle, and what is it converted into? (It enters the mitochondrion and is converted to acetyl CoA by pyruvate oxidation.)
2. In pyruvate oxidation, which carbon leaves as CO₂, and which carrier is reduced? (The carboxyl carbon of pyruvate leaves as CO₂; NAD⁺ is reduced to NADH + H⁺.)
3. Where does the citric acid cycle take place? (The mitochondrial matrix.)
4. What joins the acetyl group in step 1 of the cycle, and what is the product? (Oxaloacetate joins the acetyl group to form citrate.)
5. How many NADH, FADH₂ and ATP (or GTP) does one turn of the cycle make? (3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP via GTP.)
6. Why does one glucose give two turns of the cycle? (Glycolysis makes 2 pyruvate per glucose, and each pyruvate gives one acetyl CoA, one turn.)
7. In step 5, how is ATP made in the cycle, and does an electron carrier do it? (A phosphate moves from succinyl CoA to GDP, making GTP, which can make ATP. It is substrate-level phosphorylation, not an electron carrier.)
8. Count the carbons: how many CO₂ leave per glucose from pyruvate oxidation plus the cycle, and why does it equal the glucose carbon count? (6 CO₂: 2 from pyruvate oxidation, 4 from the two turns; the six carbons of glucose all leave.)

## Possible problems

- **Slide 27 handwritten red "connection reaction"** (written under the diagram, lower left): a loose name. The usual names are pyruvate oxidation, the link reaction or the transition step. Teach "pyruvate oxidation" and mention the link reaction as the other name.
- **Slide 27 handwritten "Jung shion step"** (lower right, Latin letters): hard to read; it looks like a student's spelling of "junction step", a loose name for the same step. Not used.
- **Slide 27 handwritten red note "Driven from vitamin B"** (with an arrow from the circled coenzyme A): loose. Coenzyme A is made from pantothenic acid (vitamin B₅). The enzyme pyruvate dehydrogenase also needs other B-vitamin-derived coenzymes (thiamine, riboflavin in FAD, niacin in NAD⁺). The note is true only in part. Say "from vitamin B₅ (pantothenic acid)" if mentioned at all; not on the printed slides.
- **Slide 27 circled coenzyme A**: the red circle is a student mark, not a claim. Coenzyme A is a carrier, not an enzyme. The enzyme is pyruvate dehydrogenase.
- **Slide 29 total of cycle NADH**: the figure shows NADH at three places in the cycle (step 3, step 4 and step 8 on p31). That is 3 per turn, consistent with p28. No error.
- **Slide 30 bullet "The next seven steps decompose the citrate back to oxaloacetate"**: loose. Step 2 is an isomerisation and step 1 is a condensation, not a decomposition. The seven steps after step 1 rearrange and oxidize the citrate-derived molecule and end at oxaloacetate. Say "rearrange and oxidize" in the voice.
- **Slide 30 bullet "each catalyzed by a specific enzyme"**: true, but the enzymes are not named; the slides show no enzyme names for steps 1-8. Do not name them in the lesson beyond "a specific enzyme for each step".
- **Slide 31 step 4 note** "The remaining molecule is then attached to coenzyme A by an unstable bond": correct (succinyl CoA's high-energy thioester bond). Keep "unstable bond" as "a high-energy bond" in the voice.
- **Slide 31 step 5 note** "CoA is displaced by a phosphate group, which is transferred to GDP": correct for Campbell (substrate-level phosphorylation); the figure shows Pᵢ going to GDP, not ADP. The ATP at the bottom comes from GTP plus ADP (GTP → GDP and ADP → ATP). Teach "GTP, then ATP" as on the slide.
- **Slide 31 text layer "c-Ketoglutarate"**: the printed name is α-ketoglutarate (text-layer error, the figure reads α-ketoglutarate). Use α.
- **Slide 29 "2 molecules per glucose"**: correct; the figure shows one pyruvate, so the numbers per glucose are doubled in the lesson. Make clear that the cycle runs twice per glucose.
- **Slide 29 "2 NAD⁺ → 2 NADH + 2 H⁺"**: the two NADH here are from the cycle's step 3 and step 4 for one turn, drawn with the bracket "2"; fine as printed. Do not add these to the pyruvate oxidation NADH.
- **Slide 28 colour**: "1 ATP, 3 NADH, and 1 FADH₂" in blue and "per turn" in red is style only. The printed claim is per turn, not per glucose. Keep "per turn".
- **Slide 26 "In the presence of O₂, pyruvate enters the mitochondrion"**: correct for aerobic respiration (pyruvate also enters in other cases in some organisms, which the slides do not raise). Keep "with O₂" as printed.
- **Slide 31 step 8 note** "The substrate is oxidized, reducing NAD⁺ to NADH and regenerating oxaloacetate": correct (malate to oxaloacetate).
- **Slide 31 step 2 note** "removal of one water molecule and addition of another": correct (citrate to isocitrate via aconitase, not named on the slide).
- **Slide 31 "Mastering Biology BioFlix" credit**: an image credit, not content.
- **Slide 31 printed "2 CO₂" and per-glucose CO₂**: the printed slides do not total the CO₂ across the stage; the total of 6 is derived (see Numbers). Label it as derived in the lesson.
- **Slide 27 text layer**: "CH3" and "O–" and "C" are the pyruvate structure (letter soup); the drawn structure is the source.

## Storyboard

A roundabout lets cars in, round and out while staying itself. The respiration map lights pyruvate oxidation and the cycle. Inside the mitochondrion pyruvate slips through a transport protein; one bead leaves as carbon dioxide, NAD+ takes two electrons, coenzyme A clips onto the two remaining carbons. Acetyl joins oxaloacetate into a six-bead citrate and the wheel turns, shedding two CO2 and collecting NADH, GTP/ATP and FADH2, and ends with oxaloacetate again. The carriers set off toward the chain. Then the carbon audit: six beads become six CO2, and the carrier tally dwarfs the four ATP made so far.
