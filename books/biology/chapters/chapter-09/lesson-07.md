## Concepts

1. **The electron transport chain pumps H⁺ from the mitochondrial matrix to the intermembrane space** (p36, bullet 1). Stated and drawn (p39: arrows at complexes I, III and IV).
2. **H⁺ flows back across the membrane through channels in ATP synthase, and the flow drives phosphorylation of ATP** (p36, bullets 2-3). Stated and drawn (p39: the red arrow down through ATP synthase).
3. **Chemiosmosis is the use of energy stored in an H⁺ gradient to drive cellular work** (p36, bullet 4). Stated.
4. **ATP synthase's parts: stator (anchored in the membrane, with an entry channel and an exit channel for H⁺), rotor (a ring of subunits in the membrane), internal rod, and catalytic knob** (p37). Drawn and labelled.
5. **The ATP synthase steps, numbered 1-5 on p37:** H⁺ flow down its gradient into a stator channel (1); H⁺ enter binding sites in the rotor and change each subunit's shape so the rotor spins in the membrane (2); each H⁺ makes one full turn and leaves through a second stator channel into the matrix (3); the spinning rotor turns the internal rod, which stays in the stalk into the knob, held still by part of the stator (4); the turning rod activates catalytic sites in the knob that make ATP from ADP and Pᵢ (5). Stated and drawn.
6. **The energy stored in the H⁺ gradient couples the redox reactions of the electron transport chain to ATP synthesis** (p38, bullet 1). Stated.
7. **The H⁺ gradient is called a proton-motive force, because it has the capacity to do work** (p38, bullet 2). Stated.
8. **Oxidative phosphorylation is the electron transport chain plus chemiosmosis: the first makes the H⁺ gradient, the second makes ATP** (p39, labels 1 and 2 under the bracket). Drawn and stated.
9. **During cellular respiration most energy flows in the sequence glucose → NADH → electron transport chain → proton-motive force (→ ATP)** (p40). Stated (the slide's sequence leaves out FADH₂ and the ATP step; see Possible problems).
10. **The ATP accounting: glycolysis gives 2 ATP and the citric acid cycle gives 2 ATP, both by substrate-level phosphorylation; oxidative phosphorylation gives about 26 or 28 ATP, depending on which shuttle carries the electrons from cytosolic NADH; the total is about 30 or 32 ATP per glucose** (p41). Stated and drawn.
11. **The shuttles span the mitochondrial membrane: cytosolic NADH hands its electrons to a shuttle that delivers them as 2 NADH or as 2 FADH₂ inside the mitochondrion** (p41, "Electron shuttles span membrane", "2 NADH or 2 FADH₂"). Drawn.

## Vocabulary

- **Chemiosmosis**: the use of energy in an H⁺ gradient to drive cellular work.
- **ATP synthase**: the membrane enzyme that makes ATP from ADP and Pᵢ as H⁺ flow through it; the flow is exergonic.
- **Stator**: the part of ATP synthase anchored in the membrane; it has the H⁺ entry and exit channels and holds the knob still.
- **Rotor**: the ring of subunits in the membrane that spins as H⁺ bind and pass.
- **Internal rod**: the stalk that spins with the rotor and reaches into the knob.
- **Catalytic knob**: the part of ATP synthase with the sites that make ATP.
- **Proton-motive force**: the H⁺ gradient seen as a force that can do work.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation**: the electron transport chain together with chemiosmosis.
- **Substrate-level phosphorylation**: an enzyme moves a phosphate from a substrate to ADP (glycolysis and the cycle; not this lesson's mechanism).
- **Electron shuttle**: a carrier pair that carries electrons from cytosolic NADH across the mitochondrial membrane (the slide gives no names).
- **Intermembrane space**: the compartment between the outer and inner mitochondrial membranes; H⁺ are pumped into it.
- **Mitochondrial matrix**: the inside of the mitochondrion; ATP is made at the knob on this side.
- **Cristae** (not named on these slides; the inner membrane's folds are drawn on p37, p39 and p41): folds that give the chain more surface.

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter. The questions below test what the material supports. Each answer is worked out from the slides.

1. Which way are H⁺ pumped in the electron transport chain, and which way do they flow back? (Pumped from the matrix into the intermembrane space; they flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase.)
2. In ATP synthase, which part spins and which part stays still? (The rotor and internal rod spin; the stator and the catalytic knob are held still.)
3. What does ATP synthase's knob do, and what does it use? (It makes ATP from ADP and Pᵢ, using the rotation of the internal rod.)
4. Why is the H⁺ gradient called a proton-motive force? (It stores energy and can do work, so it is a force that drives ATP synthesis.)
5. Why is oxidative phosphorylation the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis together? (The chain makes the H⁺ gradient; chemiosmosis uses it to make ATP. Neither makes ATP alone.)
6. Using slide 41: how many ATP come from glycolysis and the citric acid cycle together? (2 + 2 = 4 by substrate-level phosphorylation.)
7. Using slide 41: why is the total "about 30 or 32" and not one number? (The oxidative phosphorylation part, about 26 or 28, depends on which shuttle carries the electrons from cytosolic NADH across the membrane.)
8. Using slide 41: how many NADH and FADH₂ does the citric acid cycle make per glucose? (6 NADH and 2 FADH₂, from two turns.)

## Possible problems

Handwritten notes checked against the printed text and standard Campbell biology. Flagged here, not decided.

1. **p37, handwritten Arabic in the right margin (two blue scrawls).** Not legible enough to read reliably, and no English claim is written. Nothing in the lesson depends on them. Flag only: the scrawls are a student's notes, not source, and are not used.

2. **p40, "glucose → NADH → electron transport chain → proton-motive force".** Loose: it skips FADH₂ (which feeds complex II, slide 39) and the ATP step, and "glucose → NADH" hides glycolysis and the cycle. Standard Campbell gives the same chain of carriers with FADH₂ included. Teach the sequence as the slide's simplification, with FADH₂ added.

3. **p40 and p41 "most energy flows in this sequence".** Loose but acceptable: most of the ATP comes from the chain, as slide 41 shows (about 26 or 28 of 30 or 32). No change needed beyond noting the proportion.

4. **p41 "About 30 or 32 ATP" as "Maximum per glucose".** The label says maximum, but the number is an estimate; ratios of H⁺ per ATP and the shuttle choice make it an estimate, not a fixed count (standard Campbell says the same). Teach it as an estimate, and avoid "exactly".

5. **p36 "a H⁺ gradient" and p38 "a H⁺ gradient".** Article slip: H is said "aitch", so "an H⁺ gradient" is correct. Teach "an H⁺ gradient" in the narration.

7. **p37, step 1 and step 2 wording.** Printed order is: H⁺ enter a channel in the stator (1), then H⁺ enter binding sites in the rotor (2). This matches standard Campbell. No conflict; note only that step 1 is the entry into the stator, not the rotor.

8. **p39, "Electron transport and pumping of protons" and the NAD⁺ / FAD labels.** The printed figure shows FAD and NAD⁺ as electron acceptors leaving the chain, which is the usual drawing; no conflict. Flag that the slide does not name the FADH₂ entry point as complex II in words: the number "II" sits beside FADH₂ only in the drawing.

9. **p41, "2 NADH or 2 FADH₂" next to the shuttle circles.** The printed slide gives the shuttle pair without names. Campbell names the glycerol-phosphate shuttle (FADH₂ result) and the malate-aspartate shuttle (NADH result); the slide does not, so do not name them unless the narration worker chooses to add them as outside-the-slide material.

10. **p41, the grey starbursts under glycolysis and the cycle (inset on p39).** These unlabelled grey bursts are the slide's own icons for the ATP steps; no conflict, but they are unlabelled in the text layer. Teach them only as the ATP-producing steps named on the slide.

11. **p39, the inset label "OXIDATIVE PHOSPHORY-LATION"** is hyphenated across two lines in print. The hyphen is a line break, not a spelling. Use "oxidative phosphorylation" in the narration.

12. **p41, the third line under the cycle: "+ 2 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation".** This is 2 ATP for the whole glucose (one per turn, two turns), which matches slide 41's "2 ATP", not 1 per turn. Consistent with lesson 5's extract; flag only so the narration does not say "1 ATP" here.
