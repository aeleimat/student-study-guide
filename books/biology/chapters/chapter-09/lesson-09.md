## Concepts

1. **Glycolysis and the citric acid cycle are major intersections connecting many catabolic and anabolic pathways (concept 9.6)** (p51). Stated.
2. **Catabolic pathways funnel electrons from many kinds of organic molecules into cellular respiration** (p52). Stated.
3. **Glycolysis accepts a wide range of carbohydrates** (p52). Stated.
4. **Proteins must be digested to amino acids; the amino groups can feed glycolysis or the citric acid cycle** (p52). Stated, loosely; see Possible problems. Taught as: the amino group leaves as NH₃, and the carbon skeleton feeds in.
5. **Fats are digested to glycerol, which feeds glycolysis, and fatty acids, which feed acetyl CoA production** (p53). Stated.
6. **Fatty acids are broken down by beta oxidation and yield acetyl CoA** (p53, "beta oxidation" in red). Stated.
7. **An oxidized gram of fat produces more than twice as much ATP as an oxidized gram of carbohydrate** (p53). Stated.
8. **The input map: proteins, carbohydrates and fats each enter the same few hubs (glycolysis, acetyl CoA, the citric acid cycle), and oxidative phosphorylation is the common end** (p54). Drawn.
9. **The body uses small molecules to build other substances (biosynthesis, anabolic pathways); these come directly from food, from glycolysis, or from the citric acid cycle** (p55). Stated.
10. **Feedback inhibition is the most common mechanism for control of metabolism** (p56). Stated.
11. **If ATP concentration begins to drop, respiration speeds up; when there is plenty of ATP, respiration slows down** (p56). Stated.
12. **Control of catabolism is based mainly on regulating enzyme activity at strategic points in the pathway** (p56). Stated.
13. **Phosphofructokinase (step 3 of glycolysis, fructose 6-phosphate to fructose 1,6-bisphosphate) is inhibited by ATP and by citrate, and stimulated by AMP** (p57). Drawn (red inhibition arrows, green stimulation arrow).
14. **Closing recap: the whole respiration map, with ATP from oxidative phosphorylation looping back to phosphofructokinase** (p57). Drawn.

## Vocabulary

- **Intersection (metabolic)**: a point where several pathways meet and share intermediates.
- **Catabolic / anabolic**: breaks down and releases energy / builds up and uses energy.
- **Glycerol**: the three-carbon backbone of a fat; enters glycolysis as glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate.
- **Fatty acid**: the long hydrocarbon tail of a fat; broken down by beta oxidation.
- **Beta oxidation**: fatty-acid breakdown that yields acetyl CoA.
- **Acetyl CoA**: two-carbon unit from pyruvate, fatty acids or amino acids; enters the citric acid cycle.
- **Amino group**: the nitrogen part of an amino acid; removed as NH₃.
- **Carbon skeleton**: what remains of an amino acid once the amino group is removed.
- **Biosynthesis**: building molecules from small precursors, using energy.
- **Feedback inhibition**: a product (here ATP or citrate) slows the enzyme that makes it.
- **Phosphofructokinase**: the glycolysis enzyme (step 3) that is the main control point.
- **AMP**: adenosine monophosphate; its rise signals low energy and stimulates phosphofructokinase.
- **Citrate**: the first product of the citric acid cycle; inhibits phosphofructokinase.

## Questions

No question slides exist in this lesson. The practice set comes from the material; answers are given here.

1. Which two molecules inhibit phosphofructokinase, and which one stimulates it? (Inhibit: ATP and citrate. Stimulate: AMP.)
2. Why does a rise in AMP speed up glycolysis? (AMP signals low energy charge; it stimulates phosphofructokinase, so more glucose is split.)
3. What does glycerol feed, and what does a fatty acid become? (Glycerol feeds glycolysis at glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate; a fatty acid is broken down by beta oxidation to acetyl CoA.)
4. When an amino acid is used for energy, what leaves as NH₃ and what feeds into respiration? (The amino group leaves as NH₃; the carbon skeleton feeds pyruvate, acetyl CoA or a cycle intermediate.)
5. Why does high ATP slow respiration? (ATP inhibits phosphofructokinase by feedback, so less fructose 1,6-bisphosphate and less pyruvate are made.)
6. Gram for gram, why does fat give more ATP than carbohydrate? (Fat is more reduced, so its carbon carries more electrons into the chain; the slides say more than twice as much ATP.)
7. Which enzyme is the main control valve of glycolysis, and what is its substrate and product? (Phosphofructokinase; fructose 6-phosphate to fructose 1,6-bisphosphate.)
8. Name the three food types and the hub each one reaches. (Carbohydrates: glycolysis, via glucose; fats: glycolysis (glycerol) and acetyl CoA (fatty acids); proteins: amino acids to pyruvate, acetyl CoA or the citric acid cycle.)

- **Slide 52 "Proteins must be digested to amino acids"**: correct, but the same bullet does not say that the amino acids are then deaminated. Left as a stated step in the picture.
- **Slide 53 has no title** (the title area is blank). Layout only; the content is the three bullets.
- **Slide 53 "more than twice as much ATP"**: the standard comparison (fat roughly 9 kcal/g, carbohydrate roughly 4 kcal/g) supports "more than twice" as a rough statement. It is an energy comparison per gram of oxidized fuel, not an exact ATP count. Keep it as the slide says.
- **Slide 54 input map simplifies glycolysis**: it shows glucose → glyceraldehyde 3-P → pyruvate in a single column (the ten steps are omitted) and draws amino acids entering glycolysis at pyruvate. Both are simplifications of standard Campbell content, not errors; state them as "the map shows the hubs".
- **Slide 54 glycerol arrow**: it enters at glyceraldehyde 3-P, which is correct (glycerol is phosphorylated and converted to dihydroxyacetone phosphate, which becomes glyceraldehyde 3-P). Printed simply as "glycerol" into glycolysis; fine.
- **Slide 56 "feedback inhibition is the most common mechanism for control"**: a standard claim, but the slide gives no evidence. Taught as stated.
- **Slide 57 "Stimulates"/"Inhibits" labels**: the AMP arrow is green and the ATP and citrate arrows are red; the diagram is consistent with Campbell (phosphofructokinase inhibited by ATP and citrate, stimulated by AMP). No problem found.
- **Slide 57 ATP source**: the white line into the ATP star is drawn from oxidative phosphorylation (and glycolysis's payoff), which is simplified; ATP is also made by substrate-level phosphorylation (lessons 4-5). Taught as "ATP from the cell's energy-making stages".
- **Slide 57 "Fructose 6-phosphate" above phosphofructokinase, and "step 3" in the brief**: the slide does not number the steps, so the brief's "step 3" comes from standard Campbell. Taught as step 3 where it matters; flag if the narration numbers it.

## Storyboard

A railway junction becomes the respiration map with three doors lit. Sugars drop into glycolysis; an amino acid loses an NH₃ and its skeleton slides to a door; a fat splits, glycerol joins glycolysis, a fatty acid is snipped into acetyl CoA, and a bar shows fat holding more than twice the energy of sugar. Arrows then run out of the map to a tray of building blocks. A valve in glycolysis appears: ATP and citrate press it shut, AMP opens it. A muscle fibre works, ATP falls, AMP rises, the valve opens; it rests, ATP climbs and loops back to close it. The chapter ends on the whole map lighting stage by stage.
