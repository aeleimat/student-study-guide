## Concepts

1. **Oxidative phosphorylation couples electron transport to ATP synthesis by chemiosmosis (concept 9.4)** (p32). Stated.
2. **After glycolysis and the citric acid cycle, NADH and FADH2 hold most of the energy extracted from food** (p32). Stated.
3. **NADH and FADH2 donate their electrons to the electron transport chain, which powers ATP synthesis** (p32, p34). Stated; drawn as the two carrier entries on p35.
4. **The electron transport chain sits in the cristae of the mitochondrion** (p33). Stated.
5. **Most of the chain's components are proteins, grouped in multiprotein complexes I to IV** (p33, p35). Stated and drawn (four lavender complexes, labelled I, II, III, IV).
6. **Carriers alternate between reduced and oxidized states as they accept and donate electrons** (p33). Stated; drawn as each carrier's arrow in and arrow out.
7. **Electrons drop in free energy as they go down the chain and are finally passed to O2, forming H2O** (p33, p35). Stated; drawn as a staircase from about 50 kcal/mol down to O2 at 0.
8. **Electrons enter at two points: NADH at complex I (via FMN), FADH2 at complex II (via FAD); both feed into Q** (p34, p35). Stated in general terms (p34) and drawn in detail (p35).
9. **Cytochromes, each with an iron atom, pass electrons along the chain to O2** (p34). Stated; cytochromes b, c1, c, a and a3 are drawn in the chain on p35.
10. **The electron transport chain generates no ATP itself** (p34). Stated.
11. **The chain's function is to break the large free-energy drop from food to O2 into smaller steps that release energy in manageable amounts** (p34). Stated; the staircase on p35 shows the steps.

## Vocabulary

- **Electron transport chain**: a series of carriers in the inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae) that passes electrons from NADH and FADH2 to O2.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation**: ATP synthesis driven by electron transport and chemiosmosis (named on p32; the full mechanism is lesson 7).
- **Chemiosmosis**: named on p32 as the coupling mechanism; detailed in lesson 7.
- **Cristae**: folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane (p33).
- **Multiprotein complex**: a group of proteins working together as one unit; complexes I to IV (p33, p35).
- **Carrier, reduced / oxidized**: a carrier that has taken electrons is reduced; one that has passed them on is oxidized (p33).
- **Free energy (G)**: the vertical axis of the staircase on p35, in kcal/mol relative to O2.
- **Cytochrome**: a protein carrier with an iron atom, shown as cyt b, cyt c1, cyt c, cyt a and cyt a3 (p34, p35). The word is printed in red on p34.
- **FMN, FAD**: the flavin carriers shown inside complexes I and II (p35). Not defined on the slides.
- **Fe•S**: iron-sulfur centres shown in complexes I, II and III (p35). Not defined on the slides.
- **Q**: the carrier between complexes I/II and III (p35). The slides do not name it. See Possible problems.
- **NAD+, FADH2, FAD**: oxidized and reduced electron carriers from lesson 2 (p35 shows FADH2 and FAD).

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter. The practice set will be written from the material.

1. Which complex first takes electrons from NADH, and through which carriers do they pass before reaching Q? (Complex I: FMN, then Fe•S, then Q.)
2. Why does FADH2 yield less energy than NADH when both donate electrons? (FADH2 enters at complex II, lower on the staircase, so the drop it feeds into the chain is smaller.)
3. What is the final electron acceptor, and what does it form? (O2, forming H2O together with 2 H+.)
4. Does the electron transport chain itself make ATP? (No. It breaks the free-energy drop into steps; ATP comes from chemiosmosis, lesson 7.)
5. Where in the mitochondrion is the chain? (In the cristae, the folds of the inner membrane.)
6. What is the main purpose of splitting the free-energy drop into small steps? (To release energy in manageable amounts rather than all at once.)
7. Which member of the chain is not a protein? (Q, a small carrier that is not a protein complex; the slides say only that "most" components are proteins. This is not named on the slides; answer from standard Campbell biology.)
8. Roughly how large is the free-energy drop from NADH to O2 on the staircase? (About 50 kcal/mol, from the top of the axis to 0.)

## Possible problems

Handwritten marks: none on slides 32 to 35. Each printed claim below was checked against the figure and standard Campbell biology.

- **Slide 35 NADH total, "about 50 kcal/mol"**: the axis and the staircase show about 50 at the top, which matches the standard figure only loosely. Standard Campbell values for NADH to O2 are about 52 to 53 kcal/mol. Flagged as loose; not decided here.
- **Slide 35 FADH2 level near 43**: the figure places FADH2 below NADH, which is correct; the exact height is read from the image, not printed.
- **Slide 34 "The electron transport chain generates no ATP"**: true for the chain alone, but on its own it could mislead, since ATP is made by ATP synthase via chemiosmosis (p32). Flagged for narration wording, not an error.
- **Slide 32 "NADH and FADH2 account for most of the energy extracted from food"**: loose. The carriers hold most of the electrons from glycolysis and the cycle; the statement is best read as "most of the energy of the carbon breakdown" and should not be read as "most of food's total energy". Flagged as loose.
- **Ubiquinone (Q) on slide 35**: the slides label Q only and never name it. The brief's "ubiquinone" is from standard Campbell biology, not from these slides. Not an error; flagged so the narration names it deliberately.
- **Cristae "give more surface" (brief)**: not printed on slide 33; it is a standard textbook point and is not on the slides. Flagged as added, not as wrong.
- **Slide 33 "The electron transport chain is in the cristae"**: standard Campbell says the chain is in the inner membrane, whose folds are the cristae. The slide is correct in substance but says "cristae" rather than "inner membrane"; both are used in the lesson.
- **Slide 34 has no title** (blank heading area): a layout gap; the lesson will title the scene itself.
- **Slide 34 "O2." with a stray full stop** after the subscript: a typesetting slip; no meaning change.
- **Slide 35 labels FMN, FAD, Fe•S and Q** appear in the figure but not in the text; the brief's table does not mention them. Kept as figure vocabulary, not as concepts to drill.
- **Slide 35 "2 e− (originally from NADH or FADH2)"** at the complex IV end: a loose label, since the electrons arrive via the chain and not directly from NADH; accurate as a summary, flagged only for wording.
