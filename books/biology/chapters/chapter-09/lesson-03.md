## Concepts

1. **Cellular respiration has three stages: glycolysis (breaks glucose into two pyruvate), the citric acid cycle (completes the breakdown of glucose), and oxidative phosphorylation (accounts for most of the ATP synthesis)** (p18). Stated.
2. **Glycolysis takes place in the cytosol and turns one glucose into two pyruvate** (p19). Drawn; the slide shows the cytosol label under the glycolysis box and the mitochondrion label beside the other steps.
3. **Pyruvate oxidation links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle: pyruvate becomes acetyl CoA inside the mitochondrion** (p19). Drawn. The slide does not state this in its bullets; it is in the map only.
4. **The citric acid cycle runs in the mitochondrion and completes the breakdown of glucose** (p18, p19). Stated and drawn.
5. **Oxidative phosphorylation (electron transport and chemiosmosis) runs in the mitochondrion and makes most of the ATP** (p18, p19, p20). Stated and drawn.
6. **Electrons from glycolysis reach oxidative phosphorylation by NADH; electrons from the citric acid cycle reach it by NADH and FADH2** (p19). Drawn: the top path carries glycolysis's NADH electrons, the mitochondrial box carries NADH and FADH2 electrons.
7. **Oxidative phosphorylation is named for being powered by redox reactions** (p20). Stated.
8. **Oxidative phosphorylation accounts for almost 90% of the ATP generated by cellular respiration** (p20). Stated; the slide gives the figure in blue.
9. **A smaller amount of ATP forms in glycolysis and the citric acid cycle by substrate-level phosphorylation** (p19, p20). Stated and drawn (ATP stars under glycolysis and under the citric acid cycle, both labelled substrate-level phosphorylation).
10. **Substrate-level phosphorylation: an enzyme moves a phosphate from a substrate straight to ADP, giving ATP and a product** (p21). Drawn: enzyme, substrate with a P, ADP, product plus ATP. Stated only by the figure's arrows.
11. **Glycolysis's net yield (handwritten): 2 ATP net after 2 ATP invested, with the payoff of 2 ATP and 2 NADH** (p21). Handwritten, not printed; see Possible problems.

## Vocabulary

- **Cellular respiration**: glucose broken down with oxygen, in three stages.
- **Glycolysis**: glucose to two pyruvate, in the cytosol.
- **Pyruvate oxidation**: pyruvate to acetyl CoA, in the mitochondrion (named on the map; not defined on the slides).
- **Citric acid cycle**: completes the breakdown of glucose, in the mitochondrion.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation**: electron transport and chemiosmosis; makes most of the ATP; powered by redox.
- **Electron transport and chemiosmosis**: the two parts of oxidative phosphorylation named in its box on slide 19.
- **NADH, FADH2**: electron carriers that bring electrons to oxidative phosphorylation.
- **Substrate-level phosphorylation**: an enzyme transfers a phosphate from a substrate to ADP.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation (ATP)**: the ATP from the last stage, shown with its own star on slide 19.
- **Acetyl CoA**: the product of pyruvate oxidation that enters the citric acid cycle (named on the map).
- **Cytosol, mitochondrion**: the two locations labelled on the map.

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter. The questions below are the material's own checks; answers are given.

1. Name the three stages of cellular respiration in order. (Glycolysis, citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation. Note that the map shows pyruvate oxidation between glycolysis and the cycle, so an answer naming four steps is also correct.)
2. Where does glycolysis take place, and what does it make from one glucose? (Cytosol; two pyruvate.)
3. Which stage makes most of the ATP, and roughly what share? (Oxidative phosphorylation, almost 90%.)
4. Why is oxidative phosphorylation so named? (It is powered by redox reactions.)
5. Which electron carriers bring electrons from the citric acid cycle to oxidative phosphorylation? (NADH and FADH2.)
6. How does substrate-level phosphorylation make ATP? (An enzyme moves a phosphate from a substrate straight to ADP.)
7. Which stages make ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation? (Glycolysis and the citric acid cycle; hard: the student must recall it from slide 20, not from the figure alone.)
8. Glycolysis has an investment and a payoff. What is the net ATP? (2 ATP: 4 made minus 2 invested. This is the handwritten note's value; Campbell gives the same figure.)

## Possible problems

1. **Slide 21 handwriting "total ATP = 4 ATP" with an arrow to "investment".** The arrow points from the total-ATP line to the word "investment", so the 2 ATP spent is not labelled as the investment; 4 is the total made. Standard Campbell: glycolysis invests 2 ATP and makes 4 ATP, net 2. Flag: the arrow's target is ambiguous; the lesson should say "2 ATP invested, 4 made".
2. **Slide 21 handwriting "net ATP = 2 ATP" and "payoff = 2 ATP + 2 NADH".** The payoff in Campbell is the gross output, 4 ATP plus 2 NADH; 2 ATP is the net. The note writes the net as the payoff. Flag: decide whether "payoff" means gross or net before teaching it.
3. **Slide 21 handwriting "net energy production" line is cut at the bottom edge of the slide** and "Production" and "investment" are misspelled ("invesment", "Producton"). The last line ("= 2 ATP + 2 NADH") is partly cut off. Flag: read these as cropped; do not quote them word-for-word.
4. **Slide 19 map shows NADH from glycolysis and NADH and FADH2 from the cycle, but no electron carrier is labelled for pyruvate oxidation.** Pyruvate oxidation also makes NADH in Campbell. Flag: a student may conclude that pyruvate oxidation makes no NADH; the lesson should either say so or leave it out.
5. **Slide 18 says "three stages" but slide 19 draws four boxes** (glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation). Pyruvate oxidation is the bridge between glycolysis and the cycle, not a named stage on slide 18. Flag: say so explicitly so the count is not disputed.
6. **The "about 32 ATP per glucose" figure in the brief is not on these slides.** It comes from Campbell (about 30-32). Flag: keep it out of the narration unless it is labelled as outside the slides, or label it as the textbook's figure.
7. **Slide 21 has no title and no slide-level caption.** The figure stands alone; its meaning (substrate-level phosphorylation) is given only by slide 20's bullet. Flag: the lesson must name it in its own words.
8. **Slide 20 "almost 90%" is printed in blue, and "substrate-level phosphorylation" in red.** Colour is emphasis only; no conflict. Noted for the page, not an error.

## Storyboard

A water mill on a stream shows work done in stages, and the camera pushes onto the dim respiration map. The stages light one by one, with the bridge bracketed under the pyruvate oxidation box. Glucose splits into two pyruvate in the cytosol, the pyruvate crosses into the mitochondrion and becomes acetyl, the cycle wheel turns, and the violet box lights. White electron paths run over the top to it, and three ATP stars fill, the last one huge. Two columns compare the enzyme hand-off with the redox-powered burst. Then one glucose token travels the whole map, shedding carbon dioxide and filling carriers, and the lesson ends on the large burst and the textbook's estimate.
