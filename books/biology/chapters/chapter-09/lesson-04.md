## Concepts

1. **Glycolysis ("splitting of sugar") breaks glucose into two molecules of pyruvate** (p22). Stated.
2. **Glycolysis harvests chemical energy by oxidizing glucose to pyruvate** (p22, concept 9.2 heading). Stated.
3. **Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm (cytosol)** (p22). Stated.
4. **Glycolysis has two major phases: the energy investment phase and the energy payoff phase** (p22, p23). Stated and drawn (the two bands of the phase panel).
5. **Investment phase, step 1: hexokinase transfers a phosphate from ATP to glucose, making glucose 6-phosphate; the charged phosphate makes the sugar more reactive and traps it in the cell** (p24). Stated and drawn (ATP to ADP arrow).
6. **Step 2: phosphoglucoisomerase converts glucose 6-phosphate to fructose 6-phosphate** (p24). Stated and drawn.
7. **Investment phase, step 3: phosphofructokinase transfers a phosphate from a second ATP to the other end of the sugar, giving fructose 1,6-bisphosphate; this is a key step for regulation of glycolysis** (p24). Stated and drawn.
8. **Step 4: aldolase cleaves fructose 1,6-bisphosphate into two different three-carbon sugars, DHAP and G3P** (p24). Stated and drawn.
9. **Step 5: the isomerase converts DHAP to G3P; the reaction never reaches equilibrium, because G3P is used as fast as it forms** (p24). Stated.
10. **Payoff, step 6: G3P is oxidized (electrons to NAD⁺ make NADH) and a phosphate is attached to the oxidized substrate, giving 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate, a high-energy product** (p25). Stated and drawn. The slide calls it two sequential reactions: the redox first, then the phosphate attachment, which uses that energy.
11. **Step 7: phosphoglycerokinase moves the phosphate from 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate to ADP, forming ATP (substrate-level phosphorylation) and 3-phosphoglycerate** (p25). Stated and drawn (2 ADP to 2 ATP).
12. **Step 8: phosphoglyceromutase relocates the remaining phosphate, giving 2-phosphoglycerate** (p25). Stated.
13. **Step 9: enolase extracts a water molecule, forming a double bond and phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP), a compound with very high potential energy** (p25). Stated and drawn (2 H₂O released).
14. **Step 10: pyruvate kinase transfers the phosphate from PEP to ADP (a second substrate-level phosphorylation), forming pyruvate** (p25). Stated and drawn (2 ADP to 2 ATP).
15. **Net yield per glucose: 4 ATP formed minus 2 ATP used = 2 ATP; 2 NADH; 2 pyruvate; 2 H₂O** (p23). Stated.
16. **Energy-payoff cofactor balance: 2 NAD⁺ + 4 e⁻ + 4 H⁺ become 2 NADH + 2 H⁺** (p23, p25). Stated.
17. **Carbon count: glucose (6 C) is split into two 3-carbon sugars, which become two 3-carbon pyruvates; no carbon leaves as CO₂ in glycolysis** (p24 structures, p22 phases). Drawn from the structures (the slides do not state the CO₂ point; the brief supplies it).
18. **Glycolysis is the first stage of the respiration map: its ATP and NADH feed the later stages** (p23 inset). Drawn (the overview map with GLYCOLYSIS in the cytosol-side box and the ATP starburst under it).

## Vocabulary

- **Glycolysis**: "splitting of sugar"; glucose to two pyruvate in the cytoplasm.
- **Cytoplasm (cytosol)**: where glycolysis occurs.
- **Energy investment phase**: steps 1-5; 2 ATP used to phosphorylate and split the sugar.
- **Energy payoff phase**: steps 6-10; ATP and NADH formed.
- **Hexokinase**: step 1; phosphorylates glucose using ATP.
- **Phosphoglucoisomerase** (step 2): glucose 6-phosphate to fructose 6-phosphate.
- **Phosphofructokinase** (step 3): second ATP investment; key regulatory step.
- **Aldolase** (step 4): cleaves a six-carbon sugar into two three-carbon sugars.
- **Isomerase** (step 5; slide labels it "Isomerase"): DHAP to G3P.
- **Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP)** and **glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (G3P)**: the two three-carbon sugars.
- **Triose phosphate dehydrogenase** (step 6): oxidizes G3P and makes NADH.
- **1,3-bisphosphoglycerate**: high-energy product of step 6.
- **Phosphoglycerokinase** (step 7): substrate-level phosphorylation of ADP.
- **3-phosphoglycerate, 2-phosphoglycerate**: intermediates after steps 7 and 8.
- **Phosphoglyceromutase** (step 8): moves the phosphate.
- **Enolase** (step 9): removes water; forms PEP.
- **Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)**: very high potential energy (slide wording).
- **Pyruvate kinase** (step 10): substrate-level phosphorylation of ADP, forms pyruvate.
- **Substrate-level phosphorylation**: an enzyme moves a phosphate from a substrate straight to ADP.
- **Pyruvate**: the three-carbon end product (2 per glucose).
- **Pᵢ** (inorganic phosphate): drawn as a circled P on the slides.
- **Oxidation / reduction**: G3P loses electrons (oxidized); NAD⁺ gains them (reduced to NADH).

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter. These six to eight questions come from the material; the lesson writes its own quick checks and practice.

1. Where in the cell does glycolysis take place? (The cytoplasm, or cytosol.)
2. How many ATP does glycolysis use and form per glucose, and what is the net? (2 used, 4 formed, net 2.)
3. What does hexokinase do at step 1, and why does the phosphate matter? (It moves a phosphate from ATP to glucose, making glucose 6-phosphate; the charged phosphate makes the sugar more reactive and traps it in the cell.)
4. Why is phosphofructokinase (step 3) a key step? (It invests a second ATP and is a key regulatory step for glycolysis.)
5. Which step splits the six-carbon sugar, and what does it give? (Step 4, aldolase: two three-carbon sugars, DHAP and G3P.)
6. In step 6, what is oxidized, what is reduced, and what is made? (G3P is oxidized; NAD⁺ is reduced to NADH; a high-energy 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate forms with an added phosphate.)
7. Which steps are substrate-level phosphorylation in the payoff phase, and what do they make? (Steps 7 and 10: a phosphate moves straight to ADP, giving 2 ATP each time, 4 in all.)
8. How many NADH, pyruvate and CO₂ come from one glucose in glycolysis? (2 NADH, 2 pyruvate, no CO₂.)
