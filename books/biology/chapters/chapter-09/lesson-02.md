## Concepts

1. **In cellular respiration the fuel (glucose) is oxidized and O2 is reduced; the overall equation is C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy** (p12). Stated and drawn (equation with "becomes oxidized" and "becomes reduced" brackets).
2. **Respiration moves electrons, carried with hydrogen atoms, from the fuel to O2, and they drop in energy along the way** (p12, p16, p17). The "drop" is drawn on p17 and stated as "energy-yielding tumble" on p16; slide 12 shows only the redox labels. Stated and drawn.
3. **A dehydrogenase removes 2 H+ and 2 e- from the fuel and gives 2 e- and 1 H+ to NAD+ (a coenzyme); the other H+ is released into the solution** (p13). Stated and drawn (generic H-C-OH to C=O reaction).
4. **Electrons from organic compounds are usually first transferred to NAD+** (p14). Stated.
5. **Glucose and other organic molecules are broken down in a series of steps, not in one reaction** (p14). Stated.
6. **As an electron acceptor, NAD+ is an oxidizing agent in respiration** (p14). Stated.
7. **NADH is the reduced form of NAD+ and represents stored energy that is tapped to make ATP** (p14). Stated.
8. **NAD+ and NADH are a reversible pair: reduction of NAD+ (NAD+ + 2[H] → NADH + H+) and oxidation of NADH; the nicotinamide ring carries the change** (p15). Stated and drawn (oxidized form with N+; reduced form with an extra H on the ring carbon).
9. **NADH passes its electrons to the electron transport chain** (p16). Stated.
10. **The chain passes electrons in a series of steps, unlike one explosive reaction; O2 pulls electrons down the chain in an energy-yielding tumble** (p16, p17). Stated and drawn (chain icons on p17b).
11. **The energy yielded by the chain is used to regenerate ATP** (p16). Stated and drawn (ATP bursts on p17b).
12. **An uncontrolled reaction (H2 + ½ O2 → H2O) releases its energy explosively as heat and light** (p17a). Drawn; the comparison is the point.
13. **Respiration makes the same H2O from 2 H+ + 2 e- + ½ O2, but releases the energy in controlled steps down the chain to make ATP (free energy falls in steps, shown as a staircase-like drop in the G axis)** (p17b). Drawn.

## Vocabulary

- **Oxidation / reduction**: loss / gain of electrons (as in lesson 1; here applied to the fuel and O2).
- **Dehydrogenase**: an enzyme that removes hydrogen (2 H+ and 2 e-) from a substrate and passes electrons to a coenzyme.
- **Coenzyme**: a non-protein helper that carries electrons; here NAD+.
- **NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, oxidized form)**: the electron acceptor; carries a positive charge on the ring nitrogen.
- **NADH (reduced form)**: NAD+ with two electrons and one H+ added; carries stored energy.
- **Oxidizing agent**: the electron acceptor (NAD+ in respiration, O2 at the end).
- **Electron transport chain**: a series of carriers that passes electrons down an energy staircase to O2 (named here, built in lesson 6).
- **Uncontrolled vs controlled release**: one explosive step vs many small steps coupled to ATP synthesis.
- **Regenerate ATP**: make ATP again from ADP and phosphate (lesson 7 covers how).

## Numbers

- C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O (p12). One glucose, six oxygens, six carbon dioxides, six waters.
- Dehydrogenase (p13): removes 2 H+ and 2 e-; transfers 1 H+ and 2 e- to NAD+; releases the other H+ (1 H+).
- NAD+ + 2[H] → NADH + H+ (p15): 2 H atoms in (2 e- + 2 H+), one H+ out to solution.
- H2 + ½ O2 → H2O (p17a); 2 H+ + 2 e- + ½ O2 → H2O (p17b).
- p17b draws three ATP starbursts and four electron-transport complexes. These are drawn, not stated quantities; do not teach "three ATP per NADH" from them.
- No energy values appear on slides 12-17. The energy term on p12 is unquantified. (The brief mentions about −686 kcal/mol; it is not on these slides and is not used here.)

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter. Questions below are built from the material.

1. In C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy, which reactant is oxidized and which is reduced? (Glucose is oxidized, losing electrons and hydrogen; O2 is reduced, gaining electrons, to water.)
2. What does a dehydrogenase remove from its fuel, and where does each part go? (2 H+ and 2 e-; one H+ and both electrons go to NAD+, making NADH; the other H+ goes into the solution.)
3. Why is NAD+ called an oxidizing agent during respiration? (It accepts electrons from the fuel, so it is the electron acceptor and is reduced to NADH.)
4. How much does NAD+ gain when it becomes NADH? (2 electrons and 1 H+ are added to NAD+; the second proton of the pair leaves as H+ to solution, written NAD+ + 2[H] → NADH + H+.)
5. Why is NADH described as stored energy? (It carries two high-energy electrons; they can drop down the chain, and the drop is tapped to make ATP.)
6. What is the difference between H2 + ½ O2 burning in one step and the same reaction in respiration? (Same start and end, H2O; burning releases the energy explosively as heat and light, respiration releases it in small steps down the chain, and some is captured as ATP.)
7. Why does O2 sit at the end of the chain? (It takes the electrons and H+ to form water, so it keeps electrons moving down the energy staircase.)

## Possible problems

- **Slide 13 red handwriting over the H-C-OH bond**: curved arrow and loops, plus a dot. Reads as a marking of the bond that is broken; not a claim. Fine as emphasis, not as a mechanism.
- **Slide 13 red handwriting at the bottom**: two words that look like "enzyme" and a second word resembling "coenzyme". Not legible enough to confirm. Both would be correct for the dehydrogenase and NAD+ (dehydrogenase is an enzyme; NAD+ is a coenzyme), so nothing is taught from them.
- **Slide 13 printed structure**: a generic H-C-OH alcohol, not glucose. Loose: the printed text says "substrate (fuel molecule)". Teach the reaction type, and say glucose is oxidized in several dehydrogenase steps (lesson 4 and 5).
- **Slide 13 printed text**: "remove 2 H+ and 2 e-" then "transfer 1 H+ and 2 e- to NAD+" is correct (standard Campbell); no error.
- **Slide 15 red underline and curve under "Nicotinamide (oxidized form)"**: emphasis only; the label is correct.
- **Slide 15 bracket notation "2[H]"**: not explained on the slides. It means two hydrogen atoms (2 H+ and 2 e-). Explain it in the voice; do not leave it bare.
- **Slide 15 reduced-form drawing**: the extra H sits on the ring carbon in pink and the ring nitrogen has no +. Matches standard Campbell (NADH is the reduced nicotinamide ring); no error.
- **Slide 16 has no title**: the heading is blank in the source. Not an error; the lesson names the topic itself.
- **Slide 14 "NAD+ functions as an oxidizing agent"** and **"each NADH ... represents stored energy"**: both correct standard wording, but "stored energy" is a gloss; teach it as a carried-electron store that the chain can tap.
- **Slide 12 wording**: the printed "Energy" has no quantity and no ATP or heat split; the brief's −686 kcal/mol is not on these slides. Do not state it as a slide fact.
- **Slide 17 (b) "2 H" in the blue bar**: printed as "2 H" (atoms) and then "2 H+ + 2 e-"; consistent, but the bar writes atoms where the branches write ions. Teach the atoms first, then the split.
- **Slide 17 (b) drawn ATP count**: three starbursts and four complex icons are illustrations, not counts. Do not present them as "three ATP" per NADH.
- **Slide 17 (a) vs (b) free-energy axis**: both axes are unlabelled numerically. Fine for a qualitative lesson.
- **Brief row vs slides**: the brief says electrons "drop in energy as they move to O2". Slide 12 shows only the redox labels; the drop is on slides 16 and 17. Taught from 16 and 17.

### Questions

- `q1` id `c-dehydro`, `choice`, BAND: "What does a dehydrogenase do to its fuel?" Right: it removes two hydrogens (two protons and two electrons) and passes the electrons to NAD+. Wrong: it adds oxygen to the fuel (oxygen only comes in at the end of the chain); it makes ATP directly (the electrons are carried on to the chain first); it joins two molecules together (it takes hydrogen off). Hint: the name says it, "de-hydrogen". Picture: the reaction with the two lifted H lit.
- `q2` id `c-end`, `choice`, BAND: "What happens to the electrons at the end of the chain?" Right: oxygen takes them, with protons, and forms water. Wrong: they go back to glucose (they only move down); they are used up as heat (energy is released, the electrons are passed on); carbon dioxide takes them (CO2 is a product made earlier, not an acceptor). Hint: look at what waits at the bottom of the stairs. Picture: the staircase with O2 at the bottom, rest dimmed.
