## Concepts

1. **Energy flows into an ecosystem as sunlight and leaves as heat** (p2). Stated.
2. **Photosynthesis generates O₂ and organic molecules, which cellular respiration uses** (p2). Stated.
3. **Cells use chemical energy stored in organic molecules to regenerate ATP, which powers work** (p2, p3). Stated and drawn: the title question asks how food's energy becomes ATP.
4. **Matter cycles between photosynthesis and respiration**: light energy drives photosynthesis (CO₂ + H₂O into organic molecules + O₂); respiration in mitochondria breaks the organic molecules down back to CO₂ + H₂O, generating ATP and heat (p3). Drawn; the cycle is the figure.
5. **The breakdown of organic molecules is exergonic** (p4). Stated (concept 9.1).
6. **Catabolic pathways yield energy by oxidizing organic fuels** (p4, title). Stated.
7. **Fermentation is a partial degradation of sugars that occurs without O₂** (p4). Stated.
8. **Aerobic respiration consumes organic molecules and O₂ and yields ATP** (p4). Stated.
9. **Anaerobic respiration is similar to aerobic respiration but consumes compounds other than O₂** (p4). Stated; the slide names no acceptor.
10. **Cellular respiration includes aerobic and anaerobic respiration, but the term often means aerobic** (p5). Stated.
11. **Glucose is the traced fuel even though carbohydrates, fats and proteins are all consumed**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (ATP + heat) (p5). Stated and balanced.
12. **Redox reactions transfer electrons and release the energy stored in organic molecules; that energy is ultimately used to make ATP** (p6). Stated.
13. **Oxidation: a substance loses electrons. Reduction: a substance gains electrons, and its positive charge is reduced** (p7). Stated.
14. **Na + Cl example**: Na is oxidized (loses an electron to become Na⁺), Cl is reduced (gains an electron to become Cl⁻) (p8). Drawn.
15. **Generic X and Y example**: Xe⁻ is oxidized to X, Y is reduced to Ye⁻ (p9). Drawn; the same pattern as concept 14 with letters in place of elements.
16. **The electron donor is the reducing agent; the electron receptor is the oxidizing agent** (p10). Stated.
17. **Some redox reactions do not transfer electrons but change electron sharing in covalent bonds** (p10). Stated.
18. **Methane burning is such a reaction**: CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O + energy. Carbon is oxidized (its share of the bonding electrons moves toward oxygen), oxygen is reduced; methane is the reducing agent and O₂ the oxidizing agent (p11). Drawn with electron-dot structures.

## Vocabulary

- **Ecosystem**: the community of organisms and their non-living surroundings, as used for energy flow on p2.
- **Organic molecule**: a carbon-based fuel such as glucose, fat or protein (p2, p5).
- **ATP**: the molecule that drives most cellular work (p3).
- **Mitochondrion / cellular respiration in mitochondria**: where respiration takes place in plant and animal cells (p3).
- **Catabolic pathway**: a pathway that breaks molecules down and yields energy (p4).
- **Exergonic**: a reaction that releases free energy (p4).
- **Fermentation**: partial degradation of sugars without O₂ (p4).
- **Aerobic respiration**: consumes organic molecules and O₂ and yields ATP (p4).
- **Anaerobic respiration**: respiration that consumes a compound other than O₂ (p4).
- **Cellular respiration**: the general term; often means aerobic (p5).
- **Redox (oxidation-reduction) reaction**: a reaction that transfers electrons between reactants (p7).
- **Oxidation / oxidized**: loses electrons (p7).
- **Reduction / reduced**: gains electrons (p7).
- **Reducing agent**: the electron donor; it is oxidized (p10).
- **Oxidizing agent**: the electron receptor; it is reduced (p10).
- **Covalent bond, electron sharing**: a bond where electrons are shared rather than transferred (p10).

## Questions

No question slides exist in this chapter; the questions below come from the material. Answers are given so that the lesson's checks and practice can be built from them.

2. What is the traced fuel, and what does the equation release? (glucose; CO₂ and H₂O plus energy, as ATP and heat)

## Possible problems

- **Slide 5 handwriting (red):** a red arrow points at the "12" in C₆H₁₂O₆ with a short Arabic scribble beside it. It is too faint to read with confidence and carries no claim the lesson depends on; no use made of it.
- **Slide 6 handwriting (red, top):** Arabic notes across the top, apparently a reminder about the lecture ("things I must return to"). Not content; no claim used.
- **Slide 7 printed parenthesis:** "(the amount of positive charge is reduced)" is loose. Reduction is defined by gaining electrons; the positive charge going down is a consequence for cations, not a general definition (for Cl it goes from 0 to −1). Teach "gains electrons" as the definition; the parenthesis is said as "its charge drops".
- **Slide 4 printed:** "consumes compounds other than O₂" names no acceptor. The brief's example of sulfate is not on the slide; the lesson can name it only as an example outside the printed text, or leave it out.
- **Slide 5 printed:** "Energy (ATP + heat)" gives no value; the equation is balanced (6 each side, C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O). The brief's −686 kcal/mol is not printed here and is not used from these slides.
- **Slide 3 text layer:** "molecules,generating" is missing a space; the figure reads correctly. Not an error.
- **Slide 3 figure:** "Cellular respiration in mitochondria" is drawn with the arrow to heat and ATP; the figure does not show where the heat arrow comes from, so draw heat as leaving from the respiration step.
- **Slide 9 generic example:** the X and Y letters are not elements. Fine as an abstraction, but do not let the lesson treat X or Y as real compounds.
- **Slide 11 electron dots:** the dots are drawn as bonding pairs, with those near C in methane and those near O in CO₂ and water. This is a standard depiction and is consistent with the oxidation labels; no error found, but the lesson should say "shared pairs move toward oxygen" in words, not claim formal charges.
- **Slide 1 and slide 2 text:** no problems. The title card and "Life Is Work" heading match the chapter.
- **Slide 10 text layer:** "electron receptor" and "electron donor" are used as the printed synonyms of oxidizing and reducing agent. Consistent; no change.
