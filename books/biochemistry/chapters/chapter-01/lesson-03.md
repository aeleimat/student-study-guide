# Chapter 1, lesson 3: Chemical and Nutritional Classification

Source: pages/chapter-01/lesson-03/text.md and page_0010.png, page_0011.png (slides 10-11). Both PNGs were opened. Slide 10 is a figure (three structural formulas with printed counts); slide 11 is a two-column table with a mnemonic.

## Concepts

1. **Chemical classification counts the amino and carboxyl groups in the molecule** (p10, "Based on the number of amino and carboxylic groups"). Stated. Medium.
2. **Neutral: NH2 = COOH (one of each)** (p10). Stated; drawn with alanine (CH3–CH(NH2)–COOH, labels 1 and 1). Easy.
3. **Neutral members (12 names): glycine, alanine, serine, threonine, cysteine, cystine, valine, leucine, isoleucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan** (p10). Stated. Medium; see Possible problems.
4. **Acidic: COOH > NH2, carry a negative charge: aspartic acid and glutamic acid** (p10). Stated; drawn with aspartic acid (HOOC–CH2–CH(NH2)–COOH, labels 1 NH2 and 2 COOH). Easy.
5. **Basic: NH2 > COOH, carry a positive charge: arginine, histidine, lysine** (p10; "AHL": A arginine, H histidine, L lysine). Stated; drawn with lysine (two NH2, one COOH). Easy; the mnemonic is the slide's.
6. **Nutritional classification: essential and non-essential** (p11, column headers). Stated. Easy.
7. **Essential amino acids are not synthesised in the body and must be taken in the diet** (p11). Stated. Easy.
8. **The essential ten, by mnemonic "Try This VIP Mall"**: 1 Try: tryptophan; 2 This: threonine, histidine; 3 VIP: valine, isoleucine, phenylalanine; 4 Mall: methionine, arginine, leucine, lysine (p11). Stated. Hard to memorise; say it as the slide does.
9. **Non-essential amino acids are synthesised in the body, so they are not needed in the diet**: glycine, alanine, serine, tyrosine, cysteine, cystine, aspartic acid, glutamic acid, proline (p11, "The rest of amino acids"). Stated. Medium.
10. **Arginine and histidine are semi-essential: growing children must have them in food, adults do not** (p11, last row). Stated; the same slide lists both as essential in the mnemonic (see Possible problems). Medium.

## Vocabulary

- **Amino group (NH2)**: counted in the chemical classification (p10).
- **Carboxyl group (COOH)**: counted in the chemical classification (p10).
- **Neutral amino acid**: equal NH2 and COOH counts (p10).
- **Acidic amino acid**: more COOH than NH2; negative charge (p10).
- **Basic amino acid**: more NH2 than COOH; positive charge (p10).
- **AHL**: arginine, histidine, lysine, the mnemonic for basic (p10).
- **Essential amino acid**: must come from the diet, not made in the body (p11).
- **Non-essential amino acid**: made in the body (p11).
- **Semi-essential**: arginine and histidine, needed in the diet by growing children only (p11).
- **Try This VIP Mall**: the mnemonic for the essential amino acids (p11).
- **Synthesised in the body**: made by the body, which is what makes an amino acid non-essential (p11).

- **12** neutral names listed on p10.
- **2** acidic (aspartic acid, glutamic acid) (p10).
- **3** basic (AHL: arginine, histidine, lysine) (p10).
- **10** essential names in the mnemonic: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 (p11). Arginine and histidine are among the ten.
- **9** non-essential names: glycine, alanine (2), serine, tyrosine (2), cysteine, cystine (2), aspartic acid, glutamic acid (2), proline (1) (p11).
- **2** semi-essential: arginine and histidine (p11).
- **19** names across the essential and non-essential lists (10 + 9). Standard total is 20. Asparagine and glutamine are on neither list. Derived; see Possible problems.
- **1 and 2**: the counts printed on the p10 structures.

## Questions

No question slides in lesson 3.

Self-checks the slides imply: (a) given an amino acid, say whether it is neutral, acidic or basic by counting groups (for example, lysine is basic, aspartic acid is acidic); (b) given the mnemonic "Try This VIP Mall", list the essential amino acids it names; (c) say which amino acids are non-essential and why (made in the body); (d) say which two are semi-essential and when they are needed.

## Possible problems

- **Essential list counts arginine and histidine, then calls them semi-essential** (p11). The slide's own inconsistency, which the brief already flags. Lippincott and Vasudevan differ on how to label these two (essential for growing children, not for adults). Flag, do not decide: the lesson must say the list includes two semi-essential names.
- **Asparagine and glutamine on neither list** (p10, p11). Both are among the standard 20 (non-essential in standard texts). The slides do not place them.
- **Proline and methionine missing from the neutral list** (p10). Proline is non-essential on p11 and methionine is essential on p11, but neither is in the neutral list, although both are neutral by the counting rule. The neutral list is 12 names; the standard neutral group includes these two. Loose.
- **Cystine listed as an amino acid** on p10 (neutral), p11 (non-essential) and lesson 2's p8 (sulfur). Cystine is a dimer of cysteine (two cysteines joined by S–S), not one of the 20. Loose; the lesson should say it is the oxidised form of cysteine.
- **"Carry –ve charge" / "carry +ve charge"** (p10): charge depends on pH; the slide gives no pH. Loose; say "at physiological pH" if the lesson teaches the charge.
- **Histidine's class**: basic on p10 (AHL) but heterocyclic on lesson 2's p8. Two schemes, not an error; the lesson should name which scheme each classification uses.
- **Mnemonic check**: "Try This VIP Mall" gives 10 names, all of which are in the standard essential-and-semi-essential set (the two semi-essential are the ones flagged above). "AHL" for basic is the slide's own mnemonic; it matches standard basic amino acids.
- **Spelling**: "Glutamic Acids" (p10 box) and "Aspartic acid" (p10 box) differ in plural form. Cosmetic.

## Errata

1. Essential list counts arginine and histidine, then calls them semi-essential (p11): teach the lecture's ten names ("Try This VIP Mall"), then say two of the ten are semi-essential (growing children need them in food, adults make enough), leaving eight strict essentials. Screen: arginine and histidine chips are half-gold with a small "growing" tag; the voice says it in `semi`.
2. Asparagine and glutamine on neither list: kept off the lists, added as a short spoken aside ("also made in the body", non-essential) in `rest`; the screen shows two dashed mint chips "not on the slide" beside the nine.
3. Proline and methionine missing from the neutral list: the lecture's twelve are taught as printed; the voice adds that methionine and proline are neutral too (one of each group). Screen: two dashed cream chips "also neutral" after the twelve.
4. Cystine as an amino acid: taught in the list as printed, but drawn as two cysteines joined by a yellow S-S and said "the oxidized form of cysteine".
5. "Carry -ve / +ve charge" with no pH: said "at body pH" in the voice; the screen caption reads "at body pH".
6. Histidine basic here, heterocyclic in lesson two: said as "two schemes, both right"; the histidine chip carries both its green ring badge and its indigo "+".
7. The counting rule is loose for arginine and histidine (their extra basic nitrogen sits in a guanidino group and a ring, not a plain NH2): lysine and aspartic acid are the counted examples; the voice says to count arginine and histidine as "an extra basic group".
8. "Aspartic acid / Glutamic Acids" plural spelling: cosmetic, written singular everywhere.
9. Mnemonics are the slide's, kept verbatim. Voice: "Try, This, V I P, Mall" with "V I P" spelled with spaces, and "A, H, L" as letters; the lesson never says "AHL" or "VIP" as one word. On screen they are written "Try This VIP Mall" and "AHL".

## Plan

Added for length: counting on drawn molecules, the charge story, the sort bins, the shelf, tyrosine/phenylalanine, food combining, the two-label wrap. Dropped (neighbours cover): the metabolic tab (lesson four), side-chain shapes (lesson two), biological value of proteins (chapter two).

## Questions

Quick checks (side cards):
- `q1` id `c-lys`, `choice`: "Lysine has two amino groups and one carboxyl group. Which chemical class is it?" Right: basic. Wrong: neutral (counts are not equal); acidic (that needs extra carboxyl groups); essential (that is the nutritional scheme). Hint: count the groups.
- `q2` id `c-make`, `choice`: "Which of these can the body make for itself?" Options: leucine, glycine, lysine, tryptophan. Right: glycine. Wrong: leucine, lysine and tryptophan are all in "Try This VIP Mall".

Practice (`final1`, 7 questions):
1. `p-count` `choice`: "Glutamic acid has one amino group and two carboxyl groups. Which class?" Right: acidic. Wrong: neutral (counts differ); basic (that needs extra amino groups); essential (that is the nutritional scheme). Picture: glutamic acid formula with counters.
2. `p-sort` `grid` (Neutral / Acidic / Basic, `multi: false`): "Sort each by its groups." Rows: valine (neutral), aspartic acid (acidic), histidine (basic). Why lines: "one and one", "extra COOH", "A H L". Picture: three bins.
3. `p-charge` `choice`: "At body pH, which carries a positive charge?" Right: lysine. Wrong: glutamic acid (negative); alanine (no net charge); aspartic acid (negative). Picture: lysine with NH3+.
4. `p-mnem` `choice`: "Which is NOT one of the ten in Try This VIP Mall?" Options: threonine, methionine, tyrosine, phenylalanine. Right: tyrosine (the body makes it from phenylalanine). Wrong: the other three are in it. Picture: phenylalanine and tyrosine side by side.
5. `p-diet` `grid` (Essential / Non-essential, `multi: false`): "Diet or body?" Rows: tryptophan (essential), glycine (non-essential), leucine (essential), serine (non-essential). Why lines: "must eat it", "body makes it". Picture: gold and mint panels.
6. `p-semi` `choice`: "Who must get arginine and histidine from food?" Right: growing children. Wrong: adults only (adults make enough); nobody (they are essential in the slide's list); everyone, always (that is the eight strict essentials). Picture: sprout and tree.
7. `p-why` `choice`: "Why must essential amino acids come in the diet?" Right: the body cannot synthesize them. Wrong: they are the only ones that build proteins (all twenty do); they are the acidic ones (that is the chemical scheme); they are destroyed by cooking (not the reason). Picture: the broken bead chain.

Answer key (choice index from 1, to be fixed by the builder after shuffling): q1 basic, q2 glycine, p-count acidic, p-sort N/A/B, p-charge lysine, p-mnem tyrosine, p-diet E/N/E/N, p-semi growing children, p-why cannot synthesize.
