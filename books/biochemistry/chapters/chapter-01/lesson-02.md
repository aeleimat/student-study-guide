# Chapter 1, lesson 2: Structural Classification: the Side Chains

Source: pages/chapter-01/lesson-02/text.md and page_0008.png, page_0009.png (slides 8-9). Both PNGs were opened. Both slides are figures (picture boxes and structural formulas). Slide 9 credits the Valine, leucine and isoleucine picture to Foodnetworksolution.com (image credit, not content).

## Concepts

1. **Structural classification is by the side chain R, into four families: aliphatic, aromatic, heterocyclic and imino** (p8, "Based on side chain (R), AAs are classified into"). Stated. Medium.
2. **Aliphatic amino acids have six subgroups: a. simple, b. hydroxy-containing, c. sulfur-containing, d. branched-chain, e. acidic, f. basic** (p8). Stated; a list to memorise. Hard.
3. **a. Simple: glycine, alanine** (p8). Stated. Easy.
4. **b. Hydroxy-containing: serine, threonine** (p8). Stated; the hydroxy group (OH) is the feature. Easy.
5. **c. Sulfur-containing: cysteine, cystine, methionine** (p8). Stated. Medium; cystine is two cysteines joined by S–S, which the slide does not draw (see Possible problems).
6. **d. Branched-chain: valine, leucine, isoleucine** (p8, p9). Stated and drawn (three structures). Medium.
7. **e. Acidic: aspartic acid, glutamic acid** (p8). Stated. Easy. These two carry a second COOH in their R (drawn in lesson 3).
8. **f. Basic: arginine, lysine** (p8). Stated. Easy. Histidine is basic in the chemical classification (lesson 3), but is listed under heterocyclic here.
9. **Aromatic: phenylalanine and tyrosine, each with a benzene ring** (p8; p9 draws the benzene ring; tyrosine's benzene carries an OH, "phenol group"). Stated and drawn. Easy.
10. **Heterocyclic: tryptophan (indole ring) and histidine (imidazole ring)** (p8; p9 draws tryptophan and histidine). Stated and drawn. Medium: the ring names are new vocabulary.
11. **Imino: proline, a pyrrolidine ring in which the nitrogen is part of the ring** (p8; p9 draws proline, the ring N labelled NH2+). Stated and drawn. Medium.
12. **The five p9 panels show the structures as simple side-chain shapes**: glycine and alanine; histidine and proline; tryptophan; valine, leucine and isoleucine; phenylalanine and tyrosine. Drawn. Medium: the R group is the part that differs.

## Vocabulary

- **Aliphatic**: side chain is an open chain, no ring (p8 family).
- **Aromatic**: side chain has a benzene ring (p8; benzene ring, phenol group p9).
- **Heterocyclic**: side chain has a ring with a non-carbon atom (p8; indole ring, imidazole ring).
- **Imino**: amino acid whose ring includes the nitrogen (p8; pyrrolidine ring).
- **Simple, hydroxy-containing, sulfur-containing, branched-chain, acidic, basic**: the six aliphatic subgroups (p8).
- **Benzene ring**: six carbons with alternating double bonds (p8, p9 for Phe and Tyr).
- **Phenol group**: a benzene ring with an OH (p9, tyrosine).
- **Indole ring**: a benzene ring fused to a five-membered ring with one nitrogen (p8, tryptophan).
- **Imidazole ring**: a five-membered ring with two nitrogens (p8, histidine).
- **Pyrrolidine ring**: a five-membered ring with one nitrogen (p8, proline).
- **Branched-chain**: side chain with a branch point (CH with two CH3 in valine, for example) (p8, p9).
- **Cystine**: a sulfur-containing amino acid named on p8, not drawn.
- **Phe, Tyr, Trp, His, Pro, Gly, Ala** (three-letter and one-letter codes printed under the p9 structures).

2. **p9, five structure panels (all five panels are this lesson's figure).**
   - **Phenylalanine and tyrosine**: Phenylalanine: a benzene ring (drawn hexagon with alternating double bonds), a CH2 (H2 above the C) to the α-carbon CH–NH3+ (boxed) and COO- below. Tyrosine: the same, with a benzene ring that carries an OH (HO) at the para position; the HO is inside a red circle. Labels: "Phenylalanine; Phe; F (Benzene group)", "Tyrosine; Tyr; Y (Phenol group)".
   - **Tryptophan**: a bicyclic ring (benzene fused to a five-membered ring with a nitrogen at the lower right), a CH2 linked from the ring to the boxed CH–NH3+ and COO-. The ring N is drawn without an H.
   - **Histidine and proline**: Histidine: an imidazole ring (HN at left, N at right, both in a five-membered ring), a CH2 to the boxed CH–NH3+ and COO-. Label "Histidine; His; H (imidazole group)". Proline: a five-membered ring with a nitrogen drawn as NH2+ (boxed COO- on the carbon at the right). Label "Proline; Pro; P (Pyrrolidine group)".
   - **Glycine and alanine**: two small boxed units. Glycine: COO- on top, CH–NH3+ in the middle, H below. Alanine: the same with CH3 below. Labels "Glycine; Gly; G" and "Alanine; Ala; A".
   - **Valine, leucine and isoleucine** (a box headed "Branched-chain amino acid"): Leucine: H3N+ to the α-carbon (with H), COO- on top; below the α-carbon, a CH2 then a CH with two CH3 (CH3 at each end). Isoleucine: the α-carbon with H, NH3+ and COO-; the β-carbon carries H and CH3, then a CH2 and a CH3 below. Valine: the α-carbon with H, NH3+, COO-, and a CH with two CH3.

## Numbers

- **4** families: aliphatic, aromatic, heterocyclic, imino (p8).
- **6** aliphatic subgroups, a to f (p8).
- **14** aliphatic names: a 2, b 2, c 3, d 3, e 2, f 2 (p8).
- **2** aromatic (p8). **2** heterocyclic (p8). **1** imino (p8).
- **19** names on the slides in total (14 + 2 + 2 + 1). Standard total is 20 proteinogenic amino acids: asparagine and glutamine are not on p8 or p9, and cystine (a dimer, not one of the 20) is listed. Derived; see Possible problems.
- **4** ring names drawn: benzene, indole, imidazole, pyrrolidine (p8).
- **5** structure panels on p9.

## Possible problems

- **p8 "Indole ring" picture**: drawn as a six-membered ring with the N at the top, fused to a six-membered carbon ring whose left half has no double bonds drawn. Indole's nitrogen ring is five-membered. Check the picture against the standard indole ring before it is taught as indole; the label may be right and the drawing wrong, or the picture may be quinoline-like.
- **p9 tryptophan**: the ring N is drawn without its hydrogen (indole's NH). The drawing is small; check the ring size and N-H.
- **p9 red circle on the HO of tyrosine**: a circle mark, not a printed design element. Likely a student's or lecturer's mark. The OH at the para position is correct. Check the circle.
- **Scheme differences (not errors)**: the slide puts tryptophan under heterocyclic and histidine under heterocyclic. Some texts (Lippincott) class tryptophan as aromatic, and the slide's basic group (arginine, lysine) sits under aliphatic while histidine is basic on lesson 3's chemical scheme. Flag, do not decide; the lesson should say which scheme it uses.
- **Asparagine and glutamine not classified**: the standard 20 include both (polar, uncharged, amide side chains), but neither is on p8 or p9, nor in lessons 3 or 4 as members of a class. Loose: the 19 names here do not cover the 20.
- **Cystine**: listed on p8 as a sulfur-containing amino acid. It is two cysteines joined by an S–S bridge; the slide does not draw the bridge. Teach it as a dimer, or not at all, as the brief says.
- **p8 "Heterocyclic AA" box**: the slide's family name is "Heterocyclic AA" for tryptophan and histidine, but the header box for imino reads "Imino AA" with a capital and no hyphen; cosmetic only.

## Errata

1. p8 "Indole ring" picture (looks six-membered): taught and drawn as indole really is, a benzene ring fused to a five-membered ring with one N-H. The voice says "five-sided ring that holds one nitrogen with its hydrogen"; the slide's drawing is not copied.
2. p9 tryptophan drawn without the ring N-H: the page draws the N with its H (indole's NH), as above.
3. p9 red circle on tyrosine's OH: a mark, not a claim. The OH is correct; the page lights it as the "phenol group" (benzene ring plus OH).
4. Scheme: the lecture's (Trp and His heterocyclic; Arg and Lys aliphatic basic) is taught as printed. One sentence in `rings` says some books class tryptophan as aromatic and that histidine is also basic; a small italic tag on the page repeats it.
5. Asparagine and glutamine are not on p8 or p9 (19 names for 20 amino acids): not added to a class. The `wrap` beat says so in one sentence and "not on the slide" chips show them.
6. Cystine is listed but not drawn: taught as two cysteines joined by an S-S (disulfide) bridge, drawn from the cysteine piece; it is not one of the standard twenty (said in `wrap`).
7. Leucine and isoleucine are isomers: drawn and said as the same atoms in a different arrangement (checked against the p9 structures).
8. Imino: proline's ring N is the alpha-amino N itself; taught as "imino acid" as the slide does; the cosmetic "Imino AA" header is ignored.
9. Formulas are written out for the voice (C H three, S H group, sulfhydryl group, disulfide bridge); the screen shows real notation. Lesson numbers are words.

## Plan

Taught, in order: (1) backdrop, a box of building bricks with the same studs underneath and a different top on each: backbone the same, R different; the amino acid card from lesson one with the Structural tab lit; the four families named. (2) One question sorts them: an open chain, or a ring (and what is in it). (3) Aliphatic a, simple (glycine R = H, alanine CH3) and b, hydroxy (serine, threonine: OH). (5) d, branched: valine, leucine, isoleucine (leucine and isoleucine side by side as isomers). (6) e acidic (Asp, Glu: second COOH) and f basic (Arg, Lys), then the six subgroups counted; check q2. (7) The rings: benzene (Phe, Tyr: OH makes the phenol group), indole (Trp), imidazole (His), pyrrolidine (Pro, N in the ring), each ring drawn as a hexagon or pentagon in its class colour. (8) A "spot the class" sort in five small steps asking two questions (ring? what is in it?). (9) Wrap and six practice questions.

## Questions (checks and practice)

Quick checks (side cards):
- **q1** (after sulfur): What is cystine made of? Options: one cysteine and one methionine / two cysteines joined by an S-S bridge / two methionines / one cysteine with an extra S. Answer: two cysteines joined by an S-S bridge. Picture: the cystine drawing.
- **q2** (after charged): A side chain ends in a second carboxyl group. Acidic or basic? Options: acidic / basic. Answer: acidic (aspartic and glutamic acid). Picture: the six dots with e lit.

Practice (6, a picture each):
1. Serine has an OH group on its side chain. Which subgroup? Options: simple / hydroxy-containing / sulfur-containing / branched-chain. Answer: hydroxy-containing. Picture: serine R.
2. Valine, leucine and isoleucine share which subgroup? Options: simple / acidic / branched-chain / basic. Answer: branched-chain. Picture: the three forks.
3. Grid (3 rows), family from the ring, a choice among aromatic / heterocyclic / imino per row: benzene ring: aromatic; imidazole ring: heterocyclic; pyrrolidine ring: imino. Why lines: "six carbons", "two N", "N in ring". Picture: the three rings.
4. Grid (4 rows), acidic or basic: aspartic acid: acidic; lysine: basic; glutamic acid: acidic; arginine: basic. Why lines: "2nd COOH", "ends in NH2", "2nd COOH", "N-rich end". Picture: the four chains.
5. Tyrosine differs from phenylalanine by one group. Which? Options: an extra CH2 / an OH on the ring (phenol group) / a sulfur / a second ring. Answer: an OH on the ring. Picture: Phe and Tyr side by side.
6. How many subgroups does the aliphatic family have? Number blank. Answer: 6. Picture: the six dots.
All answers checked against the slide lists. Grids are at most 4 short rows; the e2e must answer every row wrong and measure every card.
