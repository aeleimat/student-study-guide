# Chapter 1, lesson 1: What Amino Acids Are: the General Structure

Source: pages/chapter-01/lesson-01/text.md and page_0001.png to page_0007.png (slides 1-7). All seven PNGs were opened. Slides 1 and 2 are the title and the lecturers' names (not content). Slide 3 is the learning outcomes, slide 4 the agenda. Slide 7 is a heading only.

## Concepts

1. **Amino acids are the structural units (building blocks) of proteins** (p5). Stated. Easy.
2. **Amino acids are obtained from protein by acid, alkali or enzymatic hydrolysis** (p5). Stated. Easy. The slide's cartoon shows a protein being broken into small shapes.
3. **There are 20 known amino acids, and 21 after adding selenocysteine** (p5). Stated; the count is a number to remember. Easy.
4. **Each α-amino acid has three named parts: a COOH group, an NH2 group, and a side chain R, which is characteristic for each amino acid** (p6). Stated; the drawing shows R, the α-carbon, COOH and NH2 on one carbon. Easy.
5. **The α-carbon is the carbon that carries the NH2 and COOH groups and the R group** (p6, the arrow labelled α carbon). Drawn. Easy.
6. **Glycine is the simplest amino acid, its R is H** (p9, glycine drawn with H below the α-carbon). Drawn on p9, not stated as "simplest" on p5 to p7. Easy. The brief asks for this; the slide shows only the drawing.
7. **Ionised forms: the amino group is NH3+ and the carboxyl group is COO-** (p9). Drawn only on p9 (glycine, alanine and others); p6 draws the neutral NH2 and COOH. The slides do not say when each form applies. Medium; keep as a light aside.
8. **Four ways to classify amino acids: structural, chemical, nutritional, metabolic** (p3 outcome 2, p4 agenda, p7 heading "Four classifications"). Stated across the slides. The four names are not listed on p7 itself (see Possible problems). Easy. This is the map for the chapter.

## Vocabulary

- **Amino acid**: the structural unit (building block) of proteins (p5).
- **Protein**: the polymer made of amino acids (p5 cartoon: "Protein").
- **Hydrolysis (acid, alkali, enzymatic)**: breaking a bond with water to release amino acids from protein (p5).
- **α-amino acid**: the amino acid whose amino group sits on the carbon next to the carboxyl group, the α-carbon (p6).
- **α-carbon**: the central carbon of the amino acid (p6).
- **Amino group (NH2)**: the nitrogen group on the α-carbon (p6). Written NH3+ in ionised form (p9).
- **Carboxyl group (COOH)**: the acid group on the α-carbon (p6). Written COO- in ionised form (p9).
- **Side chain (R)**: the group that differs from one amino acid to the next (p6).
- **Selenocysteine**: a 21st amino acid (p5).
- **Classification (structural, chemical, nutritional, metabolic)**: the four ways of grouping amino acids (p3, p4, p7).
- **Glycine, alanine**: the first two examples (p9; glycine's R is H, alanine's R is CH3).

## Possible problems

- **p7 heading only**: "Four classifications for amino acids:" is printed with no list under it. The four names come from the headings on p8 to p12 (structural, chemical, nutritional, metabolic). Loose; not an error of content.
- **p5 "20 known amino acids" and "21 after selenocysteine"**: the count is standard for proteinogenic amino acids. Some texts also count pyrrolysine as a 22nd. Not on the slides. Flag, do not decide.
- **p9 ionised forms vs p6 neutral forms**: the slides draw the NH2/COOH form (p6) and the NH3+/COO- form (p9) with no statement of pH. Loose; say the charged form is the one at body pH only if the lesson teaches it.
- **p9 red circle around the HO of tyrosine**: a circle mark, not a printed design element. Likely a student's or lecturer's mark. The OH is correctly drawn at the para position of the benzene ring. Check the circle; no answer is written on the slide.
- **p6 wording**: "is characteristic for each amino acids" (grammar only).
- **p1 and p2**: title and lecturer names. Not content.

## Errata

2. p5 "20, and 21 with selenocysteine": taught as printed. Pyrrolysine is not on the slides and is not mentioned.
3. p6 draws NH2/COOH, p9 draws NH3+/COO-, with no pH given: the general structure is drawn neutral (p6); the charged form is a short aside ("in the watery fluid of the body"), drawn on glycine and alanine, no pH number. The neutral group fades fully out before the charged one fades in.

5. p9 red circle on tyrosine's OH: a mark, no answer written, belongs to lesson two. Not used here.
6. "Glycine is the simplest": from p9's drawing (R = H), not printed as a claim on p5 to p7. Said and drawn.
7. p6 grammar "each amino acids": not repeated.
8. p1, p2: title and lecturers' names, not content.

## Plan

Taught, in order: (1) backdrop, a dinner plate (egg, milk, beans) seen from the front; the camera pushes into the egg, which resolves into a protein chain of beads, each bead an amino acid; (2) amino acids as bricks, protein as the tower and the chain; hydrolysis by acid, alkali and enzyme (three labelled cuts along the chain) frees the units; (3) 20, plus selenocysteine, 21: a shelf of twenty chips plus one gold-edged chip; (4) the general structure, built piece by piece on one carbon: alpha carbon, amino group, carboxyl group, H, R badge; "three named parts"; R is characteristic; (5) R compared on glycine (H), alanine (CH3), serine (CH2OH): cover the R and they are twins; (6) the ionised aside (NH3+, COO-); quick check q1; (7) the chapter's amino acid card with four classification tabs, quick check q2; (8) wrap and 7 practice questions.
Added for length (the slides give little): the plate and gut picture, the twins comparison with serine (serine is on p9's neighbour slides in lesson two; here only its R, CH2OH, as "a hydroxyl on a methyl group"), the practice set.
Dropped (neighbours cover): all other side chains and the structural classes (lesson two), charge, essential and glucogenic definitions (lessons three and four), the peptide bond (chapter two), zwitterion and isoelectric point (not in the slides).
Opening backdrop and push: the plate in a flat wide shot; the camera pushes into the egg yolk, which resolves into a bead chain (a protein), then pulls to the hydrolysis scene.
Thread: lesson one ends by building the chapter's amino acid card (`card` beat): one amino acid at the centre with four tabs (Structural, Chemical, Nutritional, Metabolic), all unlit at first, then lit one at a time. Lessons two to four open on the same card with one tab lit, so `cardMap` in the kit must be reusable.
Pacing: 12 narrated clips, 261.9 s of voice, plus 2 checks and 7 practice questions (about 6.5 to 7 minutes with questions).

## Questions

- `q1` id `c-r`, `choice`: "Which part of an amino acid is different from one amino acid to another?" Right: the side chain R. Wrong: the amino group (every amino acid has one, bar proline); the carboxyl group (also the same); the alpha carbon (the carbon they all share). Hint: cover the R and the three looked the same. Picture: the three amino acids with R covers.
- `q2` id `c-class`, `choice`: "Which classification sorts amino acids by whether the diet must supply them?" Right: nutritional. Wrong: structural (the shape of R); chemical (the charge, from the amino and carboxyl groups); metabolic (the fate of the carbon skeleton). Hint: think of the plate. Picture: the card with all four tabs.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice", 7 questions):
  1. `p-block` `choice`: "What is an amino acid?" Right: the structural unit, or building block, of protein. Wrong: a sugar that stores energy; a fat that insulates; a protein itself, already folded. Picture: the brick tower and chain.
  2. `p-hydro` `choice`: "Which of these releases amino acids from a protein?" Right: hydrolysis by acid, alkali or enzymes. Wrong: freezing the protein; drying it in air; shining a lamp on it. Picture: a chain with water splitting a bond.
  3. `p-count` `blanks` (numbers): "There are ___ amino acids in the common set, and ___ counting selenocysteine." Answers 20, 21. Hint: one extra for selenocysteine. Picture: the chip shelf with the counter hidden.
  4. `p-parts` `grid` (columns Same in every one / Differs; `multi: false`): "Which part is the same, and which differs?" Rows: amino group (same); carboxyl group (same); side chain R (differs); hydrogen on the α-carbon (same). Why lines: "all have it", "all have it", "R is characteristic", "all have it". Picture: the amino acid with the row's part lit.
  5. `p-gly` `choice`: "Which side chain makes glycine the simplest amino acid?" Right: R is a single hydrogen. Wrong: a methyl group, CH₃ (that is alanine); a benzene ring (far from simple); a carboxyl group (every amino acid has one already). Picture: glycine with R = H.
  6. `p-ion` `choice`: "In the charged form on the slides, how are the amino and carboxyl groups written?" Right: NH₃⁺ and COO⁻. Wrong: NH₂ and COOH (the neutral form); NH₃⁻ and COO⁺ (charges the wrong way round); NH₄ and CO₂ (not amino acid groups). Picture: charged glycine.
  7. `p-ways` `choice`: "Which of these is one of the four ways to classify amino acids?" Right: metabolic, by the fate of the carbon skeleton. Wrong: seasonal (no such class); by weight (no such class); by solubility in oil (no such class). Hint: structural, chemical, nutritional, metabolic. Picture: the card's four tabs.
- Answer key (what is right, not the order): q1 side chain R; q2 nutritional; practice: building block, hydrolysis, 20 and 21, grid Same / Same / Differs / Same, hydrogen only, NH₃⁺ and COO⁻, metabolic. The page author shuffles the order and keeps the key in the e2e.

## Kit requests
