# Chapter 2, lesson 1: What Proteins Are and What They Do

Status: extract written (all six slide PNGs opened).

Source: pages/chapter-02/lesson-01/text.md and page_0001.png to page_0006.png (slides 1-6). Slide 2 is the lecturers' names and is not content. Slide 3 is the learning outcomes. Slide 4 is the chapter agenda (definition, biological importance, classification).

## Concepts

1. **Proteins are organic, complex nitrogenous compounds made of C, H, O and N, sometimes with S** (slide 5, bullets 1-2). Easy. Stated.
2. **Proteins are polymers (chains) of amino acids, called polypeptides** (slide 5, bullet 3). Easy. Stated.
3. **Amino acids are joined by the peptide link, –CO–NH–: the COOH group of the first amino acid unites with the NH₂ group of the next, and water is released** (slide 5, bullet 4 and the diagram). Medium. Stated and drawn (the diagram shows H₂O leaving between two amino acids and the link boxed).
4. **A polypeptide chain has two ends: the C-terminal (a free COOH group) and the N-terminal (a free NH₂ group)** (slide 5, the two check bullets). Easy. Stated and marked on the chain strip (the N end reads H₃N⁺, the C end COO⁻).
5. **Proteins have many biological roles: enzymes, protein hormones, hemoglobin, plasma proteins, lipoproteins, immunoglobulins (antibodies), and supporting structures (cartilage, bone, skin, nail, hair)** (slide 6). Easy. Stated as a list of seven.
6. **One chain can carry many different amino acids in a set order, which is why proteins differ** (slide 6 picture, implied only: the slide shows a labelled sequence but does not state the point). Medium. Implied; teach it as the picture's message, not as a printed claim.
7. **The chain of amino acids is the root of the chapter's classification** (slide 4 agenda, slide 5 "polypeptide"). Easy. Sets up lessons 2-3.

Not on these slides: denaturation, which is listed as outcome 4 on slide 3. It is taught in lesson 3 (slides 12-13). Say so in the voice or leave it for lesson 3.

## Vocabulary

- **Protein**: an organic, complex nitrogenous compound built from amino acids (slide 5).
- **Amino acid**: the unit of a protein chain (slides 5-6).
- **Polymer**: a long chain of repeated units (slide 5).
- **Polypeptide**: a chain of amino acids joined by peptide links (slide 5).
- **Peptide link (peptide bond)**: –CO–NH–, the bond between the COOH of one amino acid and the NH₂ of the next (slide 5).
- **N-terminal end**: the end of a chain with a free NH₂ group (slide 5).
- **C-terminal end**: the end of a chain with a free COOH group (slide 5).
- **Enzyme, protein hormone, hemoglobin, plasma protein, lipoprotein, immunoglobulin (antibody)**: the biological examples on slide 6.
- **Supporting structure**: cartilage, bone, skin, nail and hair (slide 6).

## Questions

No question slides. Self-checks the slides imply:

1. Which group forms the peptide link, and what is released? (The COOH of the first amino acid and the NH₂ of the next; water.)
2. Which end of a chain is N-terminal and which is C-terminal? (N-terminal: free NH₂. C-terminal: free COOH.)
3. Name three biological roles of proteins from slide 6. (Enzymes, hormones, hemoglobin, plasma proteins, lipoproteins, antibodies, supporting structures.)

## Possible problems

- **Slide 5, "Formed of C,H,O,N (+/- Sulfer)"**: "Sulfer" is misspelled (sulfur). The composition is right for amino acid chains but too narrow for proteins in general: lesson 3's conjugated proteins add phosphorus (phosphoproteins), metals (metalloproteins) and heme iron (chromoproteins). Flag as loose.
- **Slide 5, bottom strip caption "PRIMARY STRUCTURE"**: the picture is labelled with a level that chapter 3 teaches. Not wrong, but the lesson should not explain levels here.
- **Slide 6, amino acid picture**: the three-letter codes are not explained on the slide (the text does not say what they stand for), and "Val" and "His" are small and partly hidden. Read them as Val and His in the voice if used, or leave the labels off.
- **Slide 3, outcome 4 "Discuss protein denaturation"**: no slide in lesson 1 covers denaturation. It appears only in lesson 3's slides 12-13 (heat denaturation). Flag for planning, not an error.
- **Slide 5, "Ends of the polypeptide chain"**: both drawn amino acids show ionised groups (H₃N⁺ and COO⁻), while the peptide link is drawn as neutral –CO–NH–. This is the usual textbook convention (zwitterion ends, neutral link); not an error. Note only so the voice does not contradict it.

## Errata

1. Slide 5 "Sulfer": written "sulfur" on screen and in the voice. The slide's list is taught as printed (C, H, O, N, sometimes S).
2. Slide 5 composition too narrow: taught as "the core of every protein", with one line that later lessons add phosphorus and metals. The picture shows C, H, O, N solid and S as a dashed "sometimes" chip.
3. Slide 5 caption "PRIMARY STRUCTURE": not shown and not explained (chapter three teaches levels). The chain strip is captioned "polypeptide chain".
4. Slide 6 three-letter codes (Val and His tiny): chips redrawn large, the code on each bead; the voice names only six in words (arginine, aspartic acid, cysteine, leucine, isoleucine, tryptophan). No claim of 19; the voice says "twenty standard kinds".
5. Slide 3 outcome 4 "denaturation": no slide here covers it; not raised in this lesson (it comes in chapter three).
6. Slide 5 ionised ends (H₃N⁺ and COO⁻) with a neutral –CO–NH– link: drawn as on the slide at the chain ends; the voice says "free amino group" and "free carboxyl group", so it does not contradict.
7. Slide 5 water: the picture shows the OH from the carboxyl and the H from the amino group leaving as H₂O (correct); the slide's text only says "water released".
8. Our own additions are tagged "our own arithmetic" on screen and in the voice: n amino acids give n − 1 links and waters; 20⁴ = 160,000 chains of four.

## Questions

Quick checks (side cards):

- `q1` id `c-water`, `choice`: "When two amino acids join by a peptide link, what is released?" Right: a molecule of water. Wrong: carbon dioxide (not part of this reaction); ammonia (the nitrogen stays in the link); a third amino acid (nothing extra leaves as an amino acid). Hint: the carboxyl gives an OH, the amino group gives an H. Picture: the frozen chain of four with the water bin lit.
- `q2` id `c-hb`, `choice`: "Which protein carries oxygen in red blood cells?" Right: hemoglobin. Wrong: an immunoglobulin (it defends against germs); a digestive enzyme (it speeds up a reaction); collagen (it supports skin and bone). Picture: the seven tiles with the red-cell tile hidden.

Practice `final1` ("Chapter practice", 7, a picture each). Right answer first as written; the builder shuffles and sets the key:

1. `p-elem` `choice`: "Which element makes a protein nitrogenous?" Right: nitrogen, from the amino group of each amino acid. Wrong: sulfur (only some proteins); calcium (not a core element); carbon (every organic compound has it). Picture: the C H O N chips with N lit.
2. `p-count` `choice`: "A chain of five amino acids has how many peptide links?" Right: 4. Wrong: 5 (one per amino acid, no); 6 (too many); 3 (too few). Hint: count the gaps between beads. Picture: five cards joined, links unlabelled. (Our own arithmetic.)
3. `p-ends` `grid` (columns N-terminal / C-terminal, `multi: false`): "Which end is it?" Rows: free amino group → N-terminal; free carboxyl group → C-terminal; where reading starts → N-terminal; the last amino acid's end → C-terminal. Why lines: "NH₂ end", "COOH end", "reading starts", "chain ends". Picture: the chain with both ends dimmed.
4. `p-link` `choice`: "Which group is the peptide link?" Right: –CO–NH–. Wrong: –S–S– (a bridge between two cysteines, chapter three); –CH₂–OH (a side chain group); –COOH (a free carboxyl group). Picture: two cards with the boxed link hidden.
5. `p-order` `choice`: "What makes one protein different from another?" Right: which amino acids it has, how many, and in what order. Wrong: only how long the chain is (equal chains can differ); only its first amino acid (the whole order counts); only the water it holds (the chain is the protein). Picture: two chains of four with swapped beads.
6. `p-role` `choice`: "Which two are both supporting structures?" Right: cartilage and nail. Wrong: hemoglobin and skin (hemoglobin carries oxygen); an antibody and hair (an antibody defends); insulin and bone (insulin is a hormone). Picture: the tile board with the structure tile hidden.
7. `p-branch` `choice`: "Which three branches grow from the protein tree?" Right: simple, conjugated, derived. Wrong: essential, non-essential, semi-essential (chapter one's amino acids); primary, secondary, tertiary (levels, chapter three); acidic, basic, neutral (chapter one). Picture: the tree with the branch labels hidden.

Every practice card gets short "why" lines for wrong answers; the e2e answers every row wrong and measures each card.

## Kit requests

Shared (for `protein.js`):
- `aminoAcid({R, cls, scale})`: Cα grey bead, NH₂ indigo, COOH pink, H white, R badge in the class colour; ionised form NH₃⁺ and COO⁻ by fading one label fully out before the other shows. Setters: `.show(parts)`, `.pulse(group)` (amino, carboxyl, h, r), sub-groups addressable. Used at several scales.
- `peptideBond({a, b})`: two aminoAcid cards plus the join: `.form(p)` (p 0 to 1) slides them, peels the OH and H, spawns a water (water.js) that drifts away, tints the link orange and shows the –CO–NH– box; `.hydrolyse(p)` runs it backwards. Pure function of p.
- `peptideChain({n, codes, colors})`: bead chain of n amino acids with orange links and optional large three-letter chips; `.links(k)`, `.ends(show)` with N-terminal (indigo, free NH₃⁺) and C-terminal (pink, COO⁻) tags and labels, `.readArrow(p)`, `.dim(parts)`. Chapter three reuses it as the primary-structure bead chain.
- `sideChain(name)`: the 20 side chains as class-coloured badges (chapter one will ask for it too).
- `proteinTree({chain})`: root chain plus three branches (simple, conjugated, derived colours from BRIEF.md), `.grow(p)`, `.light(branch)`, `.dim(f)`, `.focus(branch)`; lessons two and three open on it and push into a branch, so its layout must stay fixed.
