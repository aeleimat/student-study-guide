## Concepts

2. **Structure decides function** (p4). Stated. Easy.
3. **Folded proteins are held together by several bonds or forces** (p4). Stated; the list comes on slides 6-9. Easy.
4. **Four levels of structure, from primary to quaternary, are the chapter's map** (p4, p5; p1 shows the strip). Stated; lessons 2 and 3 teach them. Easy.
5. **Wide variation in amino acid sequence gives different shapes** (p4). Stated. Easy.
6. **Covalent (strong) bond 1: the peptide bond, –CO–NH–, formed from a COOH and an NH₂ with water removed** (p6, figure). Stated and drawn. Easy; ties to chapter 1's amino acid structure.
7. **Covalent bond 2: the disulfide bond (bridge), between the SH groups of two cysteines, losing 2 H and giving S–S** (p7, figure). Stated and drawn. Easy; cystine from chapter 1 is the example.
8. **Non-covalent (weak) bond 1: hydrogen bonds, between the –CO of one peptide bond and the –NH of a nearby peptide bond** (p8, figure). Stated and drawn. Medium: the figure is the key.
9. **Non-covalent bond 2: electrostatic interactions (ionic bonds, salt bridges) between oppositely charged side groups** (p8, figure with lysine and glutamic acid). Stated and drawn. Medium; link to the charged amino acids of chapter 1.
10. **Non-covalent bond 3: hydrophobic interactions between non-polar side chains of neutral amino acids; no true bond, but they matter for the structure** (p9). Stated, with no mechanism. Medium: the slide does not say why non-polar side chains gather (they are pushed together away from water); the lesson can give that reason, marked as the explanation rather than slide text.

## Vocabulary

- **Protein folding**: the process by which a polypeptide chain becomes its native 3D structure (p4).
- **Native (3D) structure**: the biologically active shape of a protein (p4).
- **Polypeptide chain**: the chain of amino acids before folding (p4).
- **Primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary structure**: the four levels (p5).
- **Covalent bond**: strong bond; peptide and disulfide bonds (p6).
- **Peptide bond (–CO–NH–)**: the amide link between amino acids (p6).
- **Disulfide bond (bridge), S–S**: the cross-link between two cysteine SH groups (p7).
- **Hydrogen bond**: weak bond between a C=O and an N–H (p8).
- **Electrostatic interaction, ionic bond, salt bridge**: attraction between oppositely charged side groups (p8).
- **Hydrophobic interaction**: non-polar side chains clustering (p9).

## Possible problems

- **Slide 9 heading "Hydrophobic interactions (van der Waals)"**: loose. Hydrophobic interactions are not van der Waals forces; van der Waals forces are weak attractions among packed atoms. The brief's rule: teach hydrophobic interactions and mention van der Waals only as the weak attractions among packed chains, if at all.
- **Slide 9 text "formed between the non-polar side chains of neutral amino acids"**: narrow. Standard texts describe hydrophobic side chains without restricting them to the neutral class (chapter 1's chemical classes); check before teaching it as a rule.
- **Slide 9 "Although no true bonds exist"**: contested wording. Hydrophobic interactions are usually described as an entropy-driven effect (water pushed out), not as a bond; the slide does not give the mechanism.
- **Slide 8 hydrogen-bond figure**: formal charges drawn on backbone atoms (H⁺ on N–H, O⁻ and C⁺ on the carbonyl, N⁻ on the peptide N). Backbone atoms are neutral; the H-bond is a partial-charge (δ⁺/δ⁻) interaction. Do not teach the drawn charges as real.
- **Slide 8 salt-bridge figure, right panel**: labelled "Hydrogen bonding" but drawn as the same lysine–glutamate pair with a shared proton (the ionic pair seen as an H-bond). Two labels for one pair confuse a learner. Teach the left panel as the salt bridge, and say that the right panel shows the same pair's hydrogen bond, not a second kind of bond.
- **Slide 8 "H bonds are formed between the –CO group ... and the –NH group ..."**: correct for backbone H-bonds, but narrow: side-chain H-bonds also exist (lesson 3, slide 15 lists H-bonds between side chains).
- **Slide 4 "It involving four stages"** and "The wide variation in AAs sequences → different conformations": loose grammar; the meaning is right.

## Errata

1. Slide 8 hydrogen-bond figure, formal charges on backbone atoms: not taught as real. The picture draws neutral backbone atoms with small δ+ on the H and δ− on the O; the voice says "slightly negative, slightly positive".
2. Slide 8 salt-bridge pair, right panel labelled "Hydrogen bonding": one pair, one picture, labelled "salt bridge (electrostatic)". The voice says the close pair can also share a hydrogen, so some books draw it as a hydrogen bond: not a new kind of bond. The count stays at five.
3. Slide 9 "(van der Waals)": the heading is "hydrophobic interactions". Van der Waals is said once, as the tiny attractions among close-packed side chains, "a different thing"; no picture.
4. Slide 9 "side chains of neutral amino acids": taught as "non-polar side chains" (valine, leucine, isoleucine, phenylalanine ring, from chapter 1's branched and aromatic classes). The picture shows them as grey-gold beads; "neutral" is not used as a rule.
5. Slide 9 "no true bonds exist": kept as the lecture says ("a tendency, not a link"); the reason (water crowds them out, so they gather in the core) is our explanation, drawn as water pushing the beads in and tagged "our explanation", not claimed as slide text.
6. Slide 8 "H bonds between the CO and NH of nearby peptide bonds": taught as printed; one clause adds that side chains can make hydrogen bonds too (lesson three's list).
7. Slide 4 "It involving four stages": taught as "four levels"; slide 3's five outcomes are not taught (they are the chapter's map across lessons 1-4).
8. Slides 1 and 5 strip uses single-letter codes on the beads: the screen shows three-letter chips (Ala, Glu, Met, Leu ...); the voice names no codes.
9. Slides 6-7 formulas: real notation on screen (–CO–NH–, S–S, SH, H₂O via wChem); the voice says "carbonyl group joined to an N H group", "sulfhydryl group", "disulfide bridge". Lesson numbers as words.
10. "Strong" vs "weak": added the point that weak bonds count together (very many of them); standard biochemistry, not on the slides, said as such.

## Questions

- `q1` id `c-ss`, `choice`: "Two cysteine side chains lose two hydrogens and join. What forms?" Right: a disulfide bridge (S–S). Wrong: a peptide bond (joins a carboxyl and an amino group, with water lost); a salt bridge (needs a plus and a minus); a hydrogen bond (joins C=O and N–H, nothing is lost). Hint: look at the two sulfurs. Picture: the cysteine pair with the yellow bridge.
- `q2` id `c-core`, `choice`: "Valine and leucine are non-polar. Where do they end up in a folded protein?" Right: together inside, in the core, away from water. Wrong: on the surface in the water (that is where polar and charged side chains sit); at the N-terminal end (folding, not the chain's ends, sets their place); each bonded to a lysine (that is a salt bridge, which needs a charge). Hint: water crowds them out. Picture: the folding chain with the core as a dashed circle.
- `final1` ("Chapter practice"):
  - `p-sort` `grid` (columns Strong / Weak, `multi: false`): "Strong (covalent) or weak?" Rows: peptide bond (Strong); hydrogen bond (Weak); disulfide bridge (Strong); salt bridge (Weak).   - `p-hb` `choice`: "A hydrogen bond between backbone atoms forms between which two groups?" Right: the C=O of one peptide link and the N–H of another one nearby. ## Kit requests

Shared in `protein.js`, each with setters that are pure functions of their argument (rounded cache values), colours from BRIEF.md:
1. `aminoAcid({ r, rClass })`: the general amino acid (Cα grey bead, NH₂ indigo, COOH pink, H white, R badge in class colour); setters `.ionise(0..1)` (NH₃⁺ and COO⁻ drawn as separate layers, no cross-fade on one spot), `.glow('amino'|'carboxyl'|'R')`, `.rLabel(text)`.
2. `sideChain(name)`: the 20 R shapes by structural class (chains, hydroxy, sulfur, branched, acidic, basic, benzene hexagon, indole, imidazole, pyrrolidine pentagon) as badges with rings in class colour; cysteine with an explicit SH (yellow S) handle.
3. `peptideForm(a, b)`: joins two amino acids, `.join(0..1)`, `.water(0..1)` (an H₂O from water.js leaves), the –CO–NH– link in orange with a label.
4. `beadChain({ n, labels, classes })`: beads with three-letter chips, orange links, N and C tags; `.curl(0..1)` (straight to compact blob, the same chain), `.glowAt(i)`.
5. `helix`, `sheet`: cyan ribbon coil (3.6 residues per turn) and broad zigzag arrows, each with `.draw(0..1)` and an `.hbonds(0..1)` overlay.
6. `foldedGlobule({ seed })`: green compact shape with an optional pocket and `.partner(0..1)` docking; `.bonds({ peptide, ss, h, salt, core })` overlays lighting each bond type on one figure; `quaternary(n)` packing 2 to 4 of them in lavender.
7. `levelStrip`: four chevrons (names written out) with pieces inside (bead chain, helix and sheet, globule, four globules); setters `.lit(level)`, `.dim(level)`, `.fill(0..1)`, `.small(0..1)` (shrinks to a corner map), and an empty-frame mode.
8. Bonds: `hBond(a, b)` dashed cyan with `.grow`, with δ+ and δ− marks; `saltBridge(plus, minus)` dashed pink-to-indigo with attraction arrows; `disulfideBridge(s1, s2)` thick yellow S–S with `.form(0..1)` and two H beads leaving; `cysPair` (two cysteines, `.bridge(0..1)`, `.hLeave(0..1)`); `hydrophobicCore` (grey-gold non-polar beads, polar beads, water, `.fold(0..1)`).

10. Bond chips: five small icons (peptide, disulfide, hydrogen, salt bridge, hydrophobic) and a strong/weak two-column card; a lock badge for "strong".
