## Concepts

1. **Primary structure: the number, type and sequence of amino acids in the chain** (p10). Stated. Easy; builds on chapter 1 (the amino acid units).
2. **The peptide bond is the main bond of primary structure** (p10). Stated. Easy; the printed form is "-CO-HN-" (see Possible problems); teach it as –CO–NH–.
3. **One changed amino acid can cause a physiological defect** (p10). Stated, no example. Medium: the lesson can add one clear example (sickle-cell hemoglobin) if kept brief; the slide gives none.
4. **The N-terminal end (free NH₂ of the first amino acid) and the C-terminal end (free COOH of the last)** (p10, p11). Stated and drawn. Easy.
5. **Amino acids are counted from the N-terminal** (p10, p11). Stated; the chain on p11 is numbered 1, 5, 10, 15, 20. Easy.
6. **Secondary structure: the chain folds by hydrogen bonds between specific atoms** (p12). Stated. Medium: the idea that the H-bonds are between backbone atoms, not side chains, is on the table on p12.
7. **α-helix: a right-handed coil with 3.6 amino acids per turn; the hydrogen bond is within one chain, from the carbonyl O of one peptide bond to the N–H four peptide bonds along** (p12, p13). Stated and drawn. Medium: the "4th" peptide bond means the residue four places along (n to n+4); say it that way.
8. **β-sheet: the chain is pleated (zigzag) and the strands lie side by side, held by hydrogen bonds between neighbouring strands** (p12, p14). Stated and drawn. Medium.
9. **Two kinds of β-sheet H-bond: intrachain (between different segments of one chain) and interchain (between two adjacent chains)** (p12, p14). Stated and drawn. Medium: slide 12's table calls both "hydrogen bonds" and the names confuse; teach it as two cases.
10. **Parallel and antiparallel β-sheets: all strands run the same way (parallel) or alternate direction (antiparallel)** (p14, right panels). Drawn only (arrows N→C). Medium; not stated in words on the slide.

## Vocabulary

- **Primary structure**: number, type and sequence of amino acids (p10).
- **N-terminal and C-terminal ends**: the free NH₂ end and the free COOH end (p10).
- **Secondary structure**: local folding of the chain stabilized by backbone hydrogen bonds (p12).
- **α-helix**: right-handed coiled secondary structure, 3.6 residues per turn (p12, p13).
- **β-sheet (β-pleated sheet)**: side-by-side strands in a zigzag pleat (p12, p14). The slide spells it "Bleated" (see Possible problems).
- **Intrachain and interchain hydrogen bonds**: within one chain or between two chains (p12, p14).
- **Parallel and antiparallel β-sheets**: the strands run the same or opposite way (p14).

## Possible problems

- **Slide 10 "Main bond: Peptide bond (-CO-HN-)"**: order reversed. The peptide bond is –CO–NH– (slides 6 and 8 print it correctly). Teach –CO–NH–.
- **Slide 10 "Any change in 1 AA → physiological defect."**: loose; a single amino acid change can cause disease (sickle-cell is the classic case) but not every change does. The slide gives no example.
- **Slide 12 "1ry structure (The polypeptide chain) will be folded by hydrogen bonds..."**: wrong label. The sentence sits under the secondary-structure heading and describes secondary structure; "1ry" should not be used there (it reads as primary). Teach it as the secondary-structure sentence.
- **Slide 12 "Bleated (Zigzag) peptide chain"**: "Bleated" is a typo for "pleated" (already noted in the brief).
- **Slide 12 "Stabilized by (held together by) Intrachain bond ... between carbonyl O of 1ST peptide bond & H of NH of the next 4th peptide bond"**: wording is ambiguous ("next 4th" could mean n+3 or n+4). Standard texts say the C=O of residue n H-bonds to the N–H of residue n+4. Teach it that way.
- **Slide 12 β-sheet row, "between different segments of the same chain: Intrachain"**: correct, but the row's wording of the two cases (same chain vs adjacent chains) reads as two kinds of one bond. Teach as two cases.
- **Slide 13 "3.6 amino acid ... 0.54 nm"**: the 0.54 nm bracket spans one full turn (3.6 residues), so it is the pitch (rise per turn). The rise per residue is about 0.15 nm. The slide does not say which; if the lesson gives a number, say "0.54 nm per turn".

## Errata

1. Slide 10 "-CO-HN-": taught and drawn as –CO–NH– (carbonyl, then N–H). Voice: "carbonyl then N H".
2. Slide 10 "any change in 1 AA → physiological defect": said as "can cause a physiological defect; not every change does". Added the sickle-cell example (hemoglobin β chain, position 6, glutamic acid → valine), counted from the N-terminal end, shown on 8 beads numbered 1–8 (Val-His-Leu-Thr-Pro-Glu-Glu-Lys; sickle: Val at 6). Kept brief; no mechanism beyond "clumps, cells bend".
3. Slide 10 free NH₂ and COOH vs slide 11's +H₃N and COO⁻: both true (body pH). Drawn as NH₂ / COOH tags as on slide 10, with a tiny "at body pH NH₃⁺ / COO⁻" aside on the figure only; not in the voice.
4. Slide 12 "1ry structure … will be folded": wrong label; screen reads "the polypeptide chain folds"; the voice says "the primary chain does not stay stretched out". "1ry" is never written.
5. Slide 12 "Bleated": written and said "pleated".
6. Slide 12 "next 4th peptide bond": taught as C=O of residue n to N–H of residue n + 4 ("four places further along"; 1→5, 2→6, 3→7). On screen the helix beads are numbered and the dashes run 1–5, 2–6, 3–7.
7. Slide 13 "0.54 nm": decided as the pitch: "about 0.54 nm per turn", with the rise per residue 0.15 nm as a small bracket (0.54 / 3.6 = 0.15). The helix on screen has 3.6 residues per turn (beads 100° apart), so the one-turn bracket is 0.54 nm and holds 3.6 beads; nothing shown contradicts the drawing.
8. Slide 12 intrachain/interchain in the sheet row: taught as one bond (C=O to N–H) in two cases: two segments of one chain (hairpin), two adjacent chains. Slide 12 uses "intrachain" for the helix too: said as "inside the same chain".
9. Slide 14 parallel / antiparallel: drawn only on the slide; taught with N→C arrows (same way = parallel; alternate = antiparallel). A gap filled, not an error.
10. H-bonds join backbone atoms, never side chains: stated on screen ("backbone only"); the slide leaves it implicit.

## Questions

Side-card checks:
- `q1` id `c-end`, `choice`: "Which end of the chain is amino acid number one?" Right: the N-terminal end (free amino group). Wrong: the C-terminal end (counting runs towards it); the middle; either end. Hint: we count from the free NH₂ end. Picture: the 8-bead chain with both end tags.
- `q2` id `c-n4`, `choice`: "In an α-helix, the carbonyl O of amino acid 1 bonds to the N–H of amino acid…" Right: 5 (four places along). Wrong: 2 (the next unit, the chain's own link); 4 (only three places along); 9 (two turns on). Hint: n → n + 4. Picture: the numbered helix with bead 1 lit.

Practice `final1` (7):
- `p-primary` `choice`: "Which describes primary structure?" Right: the number, type and sequence of amino acids. Wrong: the coiling of the chain (secondary); the hydrogen-bonded pleats (secondary); several chains packed together (quaternary). Picture: the 20-bead chain.
- `p-ends` `grid` (columns N-terminal / C-terminal): "Which end?" Rows: free amino group; free carboxyl group; amino acid number 1; last amino acid. Why: "free NH₂", "free COOH", "counting starts", "chain ends". Picture: chain with both tags.
- `p-sickle` `choice`: "One amino acid of hemoglobin is swapped (Glu → Val at position 6). Which level changed first?" Right: primary structure (the sequence). Wrong: secondary structure; the peptide bonds (all still there); the number of chains. Hint: the sequence is the order of beads. Picture: the swap.
- `p-atoms` `choice`: "Which atoms make the hydrogen bonds of secondary structure?" Right: carbonyl O and N–H of the backbone peptide bonds. Wrong: the side chains R; the sulfur atoms of two cysteines (disulfide, tertiary); the α-carbons. Picture: the backbone close-up.
- `p-shape` `grid` (columns α-helix / β-sheet): "Which structure is it?" Rows: 3.6 amino acids per turn; strands side by side; zigzag pleat; right-handed coil. Why: "per turn", "between strands", "pleated", "coil". Picture: the two structures.
- `p-blanks` `blanks` (numbers): "In an α-helix, the C=O of residue 1 bonds to the N–H of residue ___, and that of residue 10 to residue ___." Answers 5, 14. Hint: add four. Picture: numbered helix, counts hidden.
- `p-sheet` `choice`: "Four strands all run N→C in the same direction. Which sheet?" Right: a parallel β-sheet. Wrong: antiparallel (strands alternate); an α-helix (a coil, not strands); a random coil (no regular bonds). Picture: four arrows.

Answer key: q1 N-terminal, q2 5. Practice: sequence; grid N/C/N/C; primary; carbonyl O + N–H; grid α/β/β/α; blanks 5/14; parallel.
